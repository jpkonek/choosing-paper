# Review and scope — 12 October 2026

I checked the new counting argument and its use in **Three Complete Rankings Cannot Cover**, including the no-saturated-cut argument, rich far ends, light sets, and the finite-versus-countable ultrafilter contradiction. I found no gap in that argument. In particular, the counting induction legitimately uses (IB): it first makes the advice on the perturbation set common on both sides, then transfers the comparison. It does not incorrectly assume that arbitrary perturbations can be added to a comparison.

I also checked the principal extension in **Complete Rankings for Any Number of Worlds**, particularly the own-region argument, the endpoint dichotomy, and the concluding counting and clique arguments. The use of regions where one world disagrees with all the others preserves the dominance comparisons at the additional worlds. The conclusion for the vacuous model is supported by these arguments. The more general clique conclusion is conditional and should not be reported as universal domination of all targets containing three vertices.

For **Incomplete Rankings and Coverage**, I reviewed the stated reductions, the one-additive-world argument and its use with a complete partner, the finite-cancellation extension theorem, and the final residual alternatives. I have not performed a fresh line-by-line audit of all 46 pages and their dependencies. Two qualifications to the informal summary matter:

1. At a fine world with the specified SI and weakly AC pairs, (NC) is characterized as equivalent to a **full weak extension on all regular comparisons**. The note separately gives a sufficient condition using the smaller family of comparisons needed for a one-sided extension. Its result should not simply be paraphrased as “a one-sided extension exists iff (NC).”
2. The last residual alternative does not always give two fine worlds violating (NC). In its most general form it gives fine **debt** orders and at least two worlds without one-sided extensions. Credit fineness may fail. The stronger statement about two violations of (NC) requires the additional credit-fineness assumptions spelled out in the note.

These are qualifications to the summary, not errors found in the corresponding written theorems.

The previously missing **P1 Status Round 5.md** is now accessible. The new complete-case proof does not depend on its proposed near-vertex comparability lemma, and I did not re-audit those superseded conditional arguments.

The accompanying **Vacuous Targets and Transfinite Construction.tex** contains the new elementary results from this session:

- strictly positive value for good advice over silence is compatible with an undominated vacuous model when (S) is omitted, even for complete additive rankings;
- tying good advice with silence on mixed problems, while retaining strictness on unanimous problems, gives complete independent rankings with (E), (LA), (S), and (C), whose unique undominated model is the vacuous model;
- increasing unions of the partial rankings preserve the ordinal requirements and protected undominatedness; closed sections require a separate invariant, for which permanent open forbidden neighbourhoods suffice.

The last result identifies a sufficient bookkeeping invariant, not a proved extension lemma or a completed transfinite construction. None of these results settles the remaining incomplete-ranking problem under strict (G). The new arguments are author-checked and have not been independently refereed.
