### 1.2 Three questions separated; companion not forced through the sufficient conditions; optional properties versus requirements
**Finding.** The three questions are cleanly separated in the abstract, in §1 and in the section structure (§3 = Q1; §§4–6 = Q2; §7 = Q3).

§7 already said: "It does not pass through Bayes choices or compact families, and nothing requires a ranking built in this way to meet their hypotheses." The introduction said only "not through the conditions of Sections 4–5".

Three presentation issues remained:
- In §1 the Q2 heading said "checkable independently", but the abstract says "checkable without knowing the conclusion".
- §1 said the companion proves improvements exist "for coherent sets of desirable gambles". The improvements are of *incoherent* sets, so this was misleading.
- §7 called the constructed ranking "acyclic", which LDG never defines. Prop 7.1(a) proves the *stronger* property that every cycle consists of equal models and that distinct models are never tied. After the AUDIT point 1 correction, the companion defines acyclicity as the weaker chain condition, which allows ties (veto–Brier). LDG's undefined use of the word therefore risked importing the stronger property as if it were a requirement.

**Fixed.**
- **§1 Q3.** The sentence now reads: "...proves that such improvements exist when the models are sets of desirable gambles on an arbitrary set of worlds (...), and obtains its ranking directly in this way. It does not pass through the conditions of Sections 4–5, and nothing requires its ranking to satisfy the Bayes-choice or compact-family conditions."
- **§1 Q2.** The heading is now "checkable without knowing the conclusion", matching the abstract.
- **§7.** "the ranking is acyclic (Prop 7.1(a))" is replaced by: "every cycle of the ranking consists of equal models, and distinct models are never tied (Prop 7.1(a)). These are properties of this construction, not requirements of the general argument, which assumes no property of the relations." This restates Prop 7.1(a) word for word, so no new claim is introduced.
- **§7 cross-reference.** I added the references RX68 suggested: the consequences that apply are "Proposition 3.1, and Proposition 3.5 except part (c)".
- **§8 opening.** "the losses considered jointly cover the intended coherent class" now reads "every intended coherent model is undominated under at least one of the losses considered". This is the definition of coherent admissibility, and it removes a clash with the companion's technical term "covers".

Requirements versus optional properties are now consistently separated:
- §3 lists the conditions the argument uses: evaluability, coherent admissibility, closure under ties, and the dominance conclusion.
- Transitivity is used only in Prop 3.3.
- Prop 7.1's properties are flagged as features of the construction.

## 3. Requires sign-off (NOT applied)

### 3.1 Probable mis-citation of SSK09 "Example 1" (§3.2, first paragraph)
The text says closure under ties fails "in Example 1 of [9], where an incoherent forecast has the same total scores at every world as a coherent one." The bibliography says numbers refer to TR 865.

I read TR 865 (stat.cmu.edu/tr/tr865) through a fetch-and-summarise tool. The summary says:
- **Example 1** concerns scores that are unbounded below: P(A₁) = 1, P(A₂) = 0 with a log-type rule, giving total scores (∞, ∞, −∞). The point is that such forecasts cannot be weakly dominated. It is not about ties.
- No example states an exact tie in every state.
- The nearest is **Example 3**. A merely proper rule for A₀ makes "the score from P(A₀) identical to the score one would get by replacing P(A₀)" with a coherent value, so that "the forecasts might as well be coherent as far as the scores are concerned."

WL68 had already flagged this sentence as unverified. **Suggested action.** Check against the TR and either:
- change "Example 1" to "Example 3", with the phrase adjusted to "where replacing an incoherent forecast by a coherent one leaves every score unchanged"; or
- drop the parenthetical citation, since the merely-proper case of §8.1 already illustrates the point.

Nothing in the paper's mathematics depends on this sentence.

**What moved, section by section.**
- **Abstract.** Rewritten in the new order.
  - It no longer opens with "what follows once …". It opens with the derivation: "Our main results derive the dominance property … from hypotheses that can be checked without knowing it."
  - It then gives the Bayes-choice conditions, and the general theorems presented as a weakening of the classical selection argument (closed subsets in place of the full closed comparison sets; KKM plus compactness).
  - Only after that come the consequences (bookkeeping), the construction and the applications.
- **§1 Introduction.** Rewritten in the same order.
  - Paragraph 2 defines the dominance property as the target: "We never assume it."
  - "Conditions that yield the dominance property" covers the former Q2 text.
  - A new paragraph, "The general theorems weaken the classical argument", explains in plain words:
    - the classical selection argument (Cor 4.2) needs every comparison of the assigned model with a fixed rival at a fixed world to give a closed set of priors;
    - Thm 4.1 needs only closed subsets that still serve every prior at a world it charges;
    - KKM still applies, and Ex 4.4 shows the weakening is genuine;
    - Thm 4.6 adds the compactness that links the finite sets;
    - the abstract compactness condition is equivalent to the dominance property and so explains nothing.
  - "What the dominance property gives" (bookkeeping) comes after these.
  - "Constructing rankings" now says that Prop 7.1 does not assume dominance and reduces it to the existence of improvements. It also says the companion proves dominance directly through its repair theorem, and so bypasses the selection argument and its weakening.
- **§2.** The old §3.1 list of "four conditions" is removed. The new §2.1 does three things:
  - defines C_L;
  - defines the **dominance property** as a target claim: "a claim to be established, not an assumption";
  - defines **closure under ties**. It stays in the setting, rather than with permissibility, because the theorem statements (Thms 3.2, 4.1(c), 4.6) and several examples use it. The lead's brief allowed a different order that achieves the same aim.

  *Evaluability* and *coherent admissibility*, the family conditions, moved to §6.3 (permissibility), where they are used.
- **§3 (was §4).** The opening now says that this section and the next contain the main results, which derive the dominance property.
  - Thm 3.2 gains the clause "in particular, the ranking has the dominance property". This is an immediate restatement, since the assigned models are undominated and coherent.
  - Its proof now cites Prop 6.1(a) as "the purely logical Proposition 6.1(a) in Section 6". This is a forward reference to a logical observation that does not use the theorem, so there is no circularity.
  - Ex 3.4: "the dominance conclusion fails" becomes "the ranking lacks the dominance property".
- **§4 (was §5).**
  - Thm 4.1(c) reads "if L is closed under ties". Its proof now says "So L has the dominance property, and (b) and (c) follow by the purely logical Prop 6.1(a)".
  - A new paragraph after the proof, "A weakening of the classical selection argument", is the plain-words explanation the lead asked for (point 3).
  - Ex 4.4 is reworded: "has the dominance property and is closed under ties".
  - Rem 4.7 (collapse) is kept. Its framing now reads: "equivalent to the conclusion they would be used to prove, so assuming them explains nothing. Suppose a ranking has the dominance property, however it was established …". "Dominance conclusion" becomes "dominance property" throughout.
  - Rem 4.8 and the finite-guarantees paragraph: the same rewording.
- **§5 (was §6).** Unchanged.
- **§6 (was §3), now after the theorems and labelled bookkeeping.**
  - It opens by saying that it records what follows once the dominance property has been established, by Sections 3–5 or by other means such as the companion's direct argument. Nothing in it is a reason to assume the property.
  - Prop 6.1 (formerly prop:conclusion) is introduced as "a purely logical observation", with (a) stated as "If L has the dominance property, then …". Ex 6.2 (formerly ex:conclusion-stronger) follows.
  - §6.2: Prop 6.3 and Ex 6.4 now say "with the dominance property".
  - §6.3: the definitions of the family 𝔏, evaluability and coherent admissibility are placed here.
  - Prop 6.5 (permissibility) is now hypothesised as: "each ranking meets the hypotheses of Theorem 4.1 or 4.6 (in particular of Theorem 3.2 or 3.3), or has had the dominance property established by other means". Its proof opens: "By those theorems, or by assumption, every ranking has the dominance property."
  - The closing sentence now refers to the theorems' hypotheses. The former line "The rest of the paper concerns how to establish …" is removed.
- **§7.**
  - The opening now says: "… build a ranking, and derive the dominance property for it directly."
  - A new paragraph after the proof says that part (c) does not assume the dominance property: it is an equivalence that reduces dominance to the existence of specific improvements, which must then be proved.
  - "The dominance property itself comes from Prop 7.1(c), once the improvements are shown to exist."
  - On the companion: "proves the dominance property directly, by proving that coherent models cannot be dominated and that every incoherent model has a coherent replacement accepted as strictly better at every world.
