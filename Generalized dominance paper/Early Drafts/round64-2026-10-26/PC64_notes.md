## 0. Outcome in plain English

1. **The chosen triangle.**
   T := conv{p, q, s}, with p = (1,2,2)/5, q = (2,1,2)/5 and s = (3,1,1)/5.
   - p and q are two vertices of the reversed triangle, and the edge [p,q] lies on the reversed triangle's edge line m₃ = 2/5.
   - The edge [p,s] lies on the line m₂ = m₃, which passes through e₁. So T has exactly one cevian edge.
   - The vertices p (types ACC) and s (types TCC) are not strictly visible from any world. Only q (types CAS) is strictly visible, from world 3.
   - So T is **neither orthant-planar nor VB-type** (PROVED, CHECKED; §1).
2. **T is covered exactly under the binding framework (PROVED; every step CHECKED exactly; §2).** The ranking is complete and transitive.
   - World 3 first vetoes every acceptance that q rejects, then uses Brier.
   - World 2 uses Brier.
   - World 1 counts a wrong acceptance six times as heavily as a correct one.
   - The domain is the half-space U = {h : 22h₁ + 11h₂ + 17h₃ > 0}.
   - Every requirement holds: strict (G), (IB), global coherence of undominated models, direct (C), target undominated, every incoherent model dominated by a coherent undominated model, and (LF).
3. **The proposed characterisation "covered interior targets = VB class ∪ orthant-planar polygons" is false.** T is a counterexample.
4. **The mechanism is general (Theorem BX, PROVED).** Replace Brier by a *box chart* c_j(h) = min(l_j h_j, u_j h_j) in the veto–Brier ranking. The single exempt point of W62 Theorem `thm:exempt` then becomes one exempt *normalised box*, which may be degenerate.
   - A cevian segment is a degenerate box. That is how both bad corners of T are exempted at once.
   - Global coherence is automatic for a simple reason: the vetoes are convex and the chart is concave, so every lexicographic Bayes set is a convex cone.
5. **Exact reach on triangles (Corollary BX3, PROVED).** An interior triangle is a target of some veto–box ranking iff it has at most one non-strictly-visible vertex, or it has exactly two and the edge joining them is cevian.
   - CHECKED census: among random triangles with exactly one cevian edge, 16.8% are newly covered. Among those with exactly two cevian edges, 31.4% are.
6. **The cover genuinely needs both ingredients (§5).** For T, the following each fail:
   - every VB-type ranking (PROVED, from P61 V6);
   - every orthant-diagonal (R2) chart, which needs all edges cevian;
   - every composition (CC63);
   - every single-level concave chart, with no veto (PROVED here: ray determinant ≠ 0);
   - every continuous piecewise-linear single chart (corner value system of rank 6; PROVED modulo transferring C62 Lemmas 4.2–4.4).
7. **What stays unreached among triangles (§6).**
   - Two non-strictly-visible vertices joined by a *non-cevian* edge. In most such cases (class N33, which includes CC63's two-cevian example) a corner-mismatch lemma excludes direction-continuous facet-linear charts at the shared corner (PROVED for that class).
   - Three non-strictly-visible vertices with some edge non-cevian. This includes the reversed triangle.
   - Replacement conjecture (§4.3, CONJECTURE): *an interior triangle is covered iff every edge joining two non-strictly-visible vertices is cevian.*

## 1. The triangle and the two exact facts

Notation as in W62 §2. For a vertex v of T, F_v = {h ∈ cl D_T : v·h = 0} is generated by the two extreme rays of the edges at v. World types (FD62 §4.2, P61 V1):
- S (strictly visible): both generators have h_j < 0;
- T (tangential): both ≤ 0, one = 0;
- A: both ≥ 0;
- C: opposite strict signs.

**Data (CHECKED, `ta_cover.out` §1).**

|---|---|---|
| [p,q] | (2, 2, −3) | no (the line m₃ = 2/5) |
| [p,s] | (0, −5, 5) | yes, e₁ (the line m₂ = m₃) |
| [q,s] | (−1, 4, −1) | no (the line m₂ = 1/5) |

| vertex | generators of F_v (×25) | types (worlds 1,2,3) | strictly visible from |
|---|---|---|---|
| p = (1,2,2)/5 | (2,2,−3), (0,−5,5) | A C C | none |
| q = (2,1,2)/5 | (2,2,−3), (−1,4,−1) | C A S | world 3 |
| s = (3,1,1)/5 | (0,−5,5), (−1,4,−1) | T C C | none |

The S column agrees with an independent ray test: v + t(v − e_j) ∈ relint T for small t > 0, using barycentric coordinates.

**Fact 1 (not orthant-planar; PROVED).**
- The ray r = (2,2,−3) of the non-cevian edge [p,q] has no zero coordinate, so it lies in the open mixed orthant σ = (+,+,−).
- Near r, ∂D_T is the union of pieces of the two distinct planes p^⊥ and q^⊥. So D_T ∩ σ is not a half-space intersected with σ, and T is not orthant-planar.
- This is CC63 Lemma O1's edge argument, refereed in REF63 C2. Equivalently, by REF62_R2 §4: in three worlds a polygon is orthant-planar iff every edge line passes through a simplex vertex.

**Fact 2 (not VB-type; PROVED).**
- By P61 Lemma V1, a vertex v ≠ e_j is strictly visible from j iff h_j < 0 on F_v∖0. It suffices to test the two generators.
- p and s fail this test for every world (table above).
- For a polytope, uniform visibility of a vertex is strict visibility.
- P61 Cor V6 (refereed) says K ⊆ relint Δ is VB-type iff all but at most one extreme point is uniformly visible. T has two exceptions, p and s.

## 2. The cover

### 2.1 Veto–box rankings (definition)

Setting: W62 §2, with μ satisfying W62's (M): finite, the same null sets as Lebesgue measure, and a finite first moment. This is the measure every construction of the project uses. The binding framework allows "Lebesgue or a positive density"; finiteness and a first moment are needed only to make the scores finite. Also:
- K′ ⊆ Δ is nonempty, compact and convex, and U = D_{K′};
- B = (B₁,…,B_n) is a veto profile over K′ (W62 Def `def:vb`), with φ_i(h) = max_{v∈B_i}(−v·h) and a_i(X) = ∫_X φ_i dμ;
- 0 < l ≤ u in ℝⁿ, and R := ∏_j [l_j, u_j] is a box.

Define
  c_j(h) := min(l_j h_j, u_j h_j),  b_j(X) := −∫_X c_j dμ,
and let world j rank lexicographically: X ⪯_j Y iff (a_j(X), b_j(X)) ≤_lex (a_j(Y), b_j(Y)).
- With R = {𝟙} this is W62's VB(K′,B).
- Without vetoes it is FD62's box chart.
- Call it VBox(K′, B, R).

### 2.2 Lemmas

Each is W62's lemma with h_j replaced by c_j. Only the indicated steps change.

**Lemma BX1 (the chart; PROVED, CHECKED).**
- (a) c_j is continuous, concave and positively homogeneous, and |c_j(h)| ≤ u_j|h_j|.
- (b) sign c_j(h) = sign h_j for every h.
- (c) c_j(h + t𝟙) ≥ c_j(h) + t l_j for t ≥ 0.
- (d) For β ∈ ℝⁿ₊, Σ_j β_j c_j(h) = min_{ρ∈R} (β⊙ρ)·h.

*Proof.*
- (a), (b): c_j is the minimum of two linear functions of h_j with positive slopes.
- (c): a concave, positively homogeneous function is superadditive, and c_j(t𝟙) = t l_j.
- (d): a linear function of ρ_j attains its minimum over [l_j, u_j] at an endpoint. The choices for different j are independent, and β ≥ 0. ∎

**Lemma BX2 (axioms; PROVED).** VBox is a ranking on M_U. Each ⪯_j is a total preorder, hence reflexive, transitive and (TC). It satisfies strict (G) and (IB).

*Proof.*
- This is W62 Lemma `lem:axioms`, verbatim, except that b_j(X) − b_j(Y) = −∫_{X∖Y} c_j + ∫_{Y∖X} c_j.
- That difference is < 0 by BX1(b), because X∖Y ⊆ {h_j > 0} and Y∖X ⊆ {h_j < 0} a.e., and X△Y is non-null.
- Integrability comes from BX1(a) and (M). ∎

**Lyapunov, Zorn, separation.**
- W62 Lemmas `lem:lyap`, `lem:zorn` and `lem:sep` hold verbatim. They use only the score structure: integrable f = (φ, −c), the lexicographic pairs, and nonnegative vetoes with a_ω(∅) = 0.
- In `lem:sep` read b_ω = −∫ c_ω. For an admissible triple (W₀, β, α) put
  g(h) := Σ_{ω∉W₀} α_ω φ_ω(h) − Σ_j β_j c_j(h),
  so that J(X) = ∫_X g dμ for X ⊆ N(W₀).

**Lemma BX3 (uniqueness; PROVED).** {g = 0} is null, and the minimisers of ∫_X g over models X ⊆ N(W₀) are exactly the models equal a.e. to X*(W₀,β,α) := N(W₀) ∩ {g < 0}.

*Proof.* By W62 Lemma `lem:veto`(c) and BX1(c), g(h + t𝟙) ≤ g(h) − t β·l < g(h) for t > 0, because β·l > 0. The rest is W62 Lemma `lem:unique`. ∎

**Lemma BX4 (coherence; PROVED).** Put
  W := {β⊙ρ + Σ_{ω∉W₀} α_ω v_ω : ρ ∈ R, v_ω ∈ B_ω}.
Then g(h) = max_{w∈W}(−w·h). Every w ∈ W satisfies w ≥ 0 and 𝟙·w ≥ β·l > 0. Hence X*(W₀,β,α) = D_{K(W₀,β,α)} a.e., where
  K(W₀,β,α) := conv(K′ ∪ ⋃_{ω∈W₀} Â_ω ∪ ν(W))
is a credal set containing K′.

*Proof.*
- BX1(d) and the definition of φ_ω give g as a sum of independent maxima. Since α ≥ 0, that is the maximum of the sums.
- W is compact, as a continuous image of a product of compact sets.
- The rest is W62 Lemma `lem:coh` verbatim. ∎

**Theorem BX-family (PROVED).**
- VBox(K′,B,R) satisfies (G) and (IB), and every model is weakly dominated by an undominated model.
- A model is undominated iff it equals X*(W₀,β,α) a.e. for an admissible triple. Hence every undominated model is coherent.
- A model tied at every world with an undominated model equals it a.e.
- Consequently (W) and direct (C) hold, U = C*, and every incoherent model is dominated by a coherent undominated model.

*Proof.* W62 Theorem `thm:family` verbatim, with BX3 and BX4 in place of `lem:unique` and `lem:coh`. W62 Lemma `lem:dominance` uses completeness, which these rankings have. ∎

### 2.3 Box exemption

**Theorem BX (PROVED).** Let K′ ⊆ Δ be nonempty, compact and convex. Let A_i ⊆ conv(K′ ∪ {e_i}) be compact (possibly empty), and B_i := conv({0} ∪ A_i). Let R be a box and β ∈ ℝⁿ₊∖{0}. Put
  K := conv(K′ ∪ A₁ ∪ … ∪ A_n ∪ ν(β⊙R)).
If K is full-dimensional and K′ ⊆ relint K, then VBox(K′,B,R) covers K on M_{D_{K′}}.

*Proof.*
- The triple (Ω, β, ∅) is admissible, with W = β⊙R. As in W62 Thm `thm:cover`, Â_ω = conv A_ω, so K(Ω,β,∅) = K.
- So D_K is undominated by BX-family.
- W62 Lemma `lem:LF`(a) gives D_K ∈ M_U and (LF), since K′ ⊆ relint K. ∎

*Remarks.*
- R = {r}, a point, is W62 Thm `thm:exempt`, since every p ∈ Δ is ν(β⊙r). So Theorem BX strictly extends the one-exempt-point theorem.
- With A = ∅ and β = 𝟙 it is FD62 Prop FD-B: boxes.
- ν(β⊙R) is a "normalised box". It may be degenerate: l_j = u_j is allowed, and β may have zeros.
- In three worlds and inside relint Δ, ν(R) is a hexagon, a quadrilateral, a segment on a cevian line, or a point. Its edges are cevian, since corners differing only in coordinate l lie on a line through e_l.
- **Every cevian segment [a,b] ⊆ relint Δ is a degenerate box.** If b = (1−τ)a + τe_l with τ ∈ (0,1) (swap a and b if τ < 0), then [a,b] = ν(R) for R_l = [a_l, a_l + τ/(1−τ)] and R_i = {a_i} for i ≠ l.

### 2.4 The cover of T (PROVED; CHECKED, `ta_cover.out`)

**Data.**
- K′ = {k} with k = (22, 11, 17)/50. Its barycentric coordinates in (p, q, s) are (1/10, 3/5, 3/10), so k ∈ relint T.
- U = {h : k·h > 0}.
- A₃ = {q} and A₁ = A₂ = ∅. The identity q = (10/11)k + (1/11)e₃ holds exactly, so q ∈ conv(K′ ∪ {e₃}). This is where strict visibility of q from world 3 is used.
- R = [1,6] × {2} × {2} and β = 𝟙, so ν(R) = [ν(1,2,2), ν(6,2,2)] = [p, s].

**Theorem BX gives** K = conv({k} ∪ {q} ∪ [p,s]) = T. The scores are:

| world | first level | second level |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | — | E₁(X) = −∫_X min(h₁, 6h₁) dμ = ∫_X (6h₁⁻ − h₁⁺) dμ |
| 2 | — | E₂(X) = −∫_X 2h₂ dμ (Brier) |
| 3 | V₃(X) = ∫_X (q·h)⁻ dμ | E₃(X) = −∫_X 2h₃ dμ (Brier) |

The factor 2 at worlds 2 and 3 can be dropped without changing any world's order. The target triple is then β = (1,2,2).

**Why the target comes out right.**
- 𝟙·c(h) = min((1,2,2)·h, (6,2,2)·h) = min(5 p·h, 10 s·h).
  - Since (6,2,2)·h − (1,2,2)·h = 5h₁, the first expression applies when h₁ ≥ 0 and the second when h₁ < 0.
  - So {𝟙·c > 0} = D_{{p,s}}.
- The veto-free region is U ∩ {q·h ≥ 0}, which equals U ∩ D_q a.e.
- Hence N(Ω) ∩ {𝟙·c > 0} = D_{k} ∩ D_q ∩ D_p ∩ D_s = D_T, because k ∈ T.

## 3. Which triangles the new class reaches

**Corollary BX3 (PROVED).** Let T ⊆ relint Δ₃ be a triangle ("bad" = not strictly visible). T is a covered target of some veto–box ranking on some convex one-sided domain with (LF) iff
- (V) T has at most one bad vertex; or
- (X) T has exactly two bad vertices and the edge joining them is cevian.

*Proof (⇐).*
- (V) is W62 Thm `thm:exempt` (R a point).
- (X): let a, b be the bad vertices, joined by a cevian edge through e_l, and let c be strictly visible from world j.
  - Choose ε > 0 with k := c + ε(c − e_j) ∈ relint T (strict visibility).
  - Take K′ = {k}, A_j = {c} (note c = (k + εe_j)/(1+ε)), and R the degenerate box with ν(R) = [a,b] (§2.3, last remark).
  - Theorem BX gives conv({k, c} ∪ [a,b]) = T.
  - CHECKED: this construction on 200 random class-X triangles (100 with one cevian edge, 100 with two), with sign-correctness, veto guidance and the target identity tested on random rational gambles: 0 failures (`x_class.out`).

*Proof (⇒).*
1. **The triple.** A convex one-sided domain with (LF) is D_{K′} with K′ ⊆ relint T (P61 remark; W62 `lem:LF`(b)). Undominatedness of D_T and BX-family give D_T = D_{K(W₀,β,α)} a.e., so T = K(W₀,β,α) by W62 `lem:open`.
2. **W₀ = Ω.** ν(β⊙l) ∈ ν(W) ⊆ T has support supp β ⊆ W₀. Since T ⊆ relint Δ, supp β = Ω, hence W₀ = Ω and W = β⊙R.
3. **Bad vertices lie in the box.**
   - Extreme points of T lie in K′ ∪ ⋃Â_ω ∪ ν(β⊙R), and none lies in K′ ⊆ relint T.
   - An extreme point x ∈ Â_ω ⊆ conv(K′ ∪ e_ω) is x = (1−t)k + te_ω with t < 1. So the open segment (k, x) ⊆ relint T makes x strictly visible from ω.
   - Hence every bad vertex lies in Bx := ν(β⊙R) ⊆ T. Here β ≫ 0, since Bx ⊆ relint Δ.
4. **Three bad vertices are impossible.** They would give T = Bx. But a normalised box in relint Δ₃ is never a triangle.
   - In log-ratio coordinates the constraints are x_i − x_j ≤ log(u_i/l_j).
   - The constraint for (i,j) is redundant iff l_k ≥ u_k for the third index k.
   - So: no degenerate coordinate gives 6 irredundant constraints (a hexagon); one gives 4 (a quadrilateral); two give a segment; three give a point.
5. **Two bad vertices force a cevian edge.** Let the bad vertices be a, b. The edge [a,b] lies on a supporting line L of T, and Bx ⊆ T lies on one side of L. So Bx ∩ L is a face of Bx containing [a,b]: an edge of Bx, or Bx itself if Bx is a segment. Its line passes through a simplex vertex. ∎

## 4. What this does to the proposed characterisation

### 4.1 The refutation

The statement "in three worlds, the covered interior targets are exactly the VB class together with the orthant-planar polygons" is **false**.
- T is covered (§2).
- T is not VB-type (Fact 2) and not orthant-planar (Fact 1).
- The counterexample is robust. Every class-X triangle is one, and class X is about a sixth to a third of all partially cevian triangles (§3).

### 4.2 The nearby true statement

Every interior target in the following union is covered (PROVED):
- the **box-exempt class** BX, of targets conv(K′ ∪ ⋃A_i ∪ ν(β⊙R)), meaning that every extreme point outside one normalised box ν(β⊙R) ⊆ K is uniformly visible;
- the orthant-planar polygons (R2).

Notes:
- BX contains the VB class (a point is a degenerate box) and all normalised boxes.
- Theorem BX holds in every number of worlds n ≥ 2, and with β on ∂Δ (boundary exempt boxes). I have not checked what it adds for n ≥ 4 beyond composition's Theorem F targets.
- Composition does not reach T. By CC63 N3 its n = 3 interior targets are VB-type, and the one-cevian angle criterion fails for T (§5). Theorem BX is a different mechanism: the vetoes act on h, not on c(h).

### 4.3 Replacement conjecture (CONJECTURE, triangles only)

*An interior triangle is covered iff every edge joining two non-strictly-visible vertices is cevian.* Equivalently: classes V, X, or all three edges cevian.

- **For it:** it fits every known cover (VB, R2, composition, BX). It fits every proved exclusion (the reversed triangle is in the complement). On class N33 there is a first-order corner obstruction for direction-continuous facet-linear charts (§6.1).
- **Against it, or at least not supporting it:** on N∖N33 (one-cevian: 250 of 250 sampled) and on 80 of 271 one-cevian 3-bad triangles, the single-chart direction system at the target prior *has* real admissible solutions (§6.2). For those triangles no first-order obstruction is known.

So the conjecture is a proposal for the next round, not a characterisation.

## 5. Why simpler constructions fail for T (scoped exclusions)

| construction class | verdict for T | status |
|---|---|---|
| VB-type: sublinear vetoes then Brier, convex one-sided domain | excluded: 2 bad vertices | PROVED (P61 V6) |
| orthant-diagonal charts d_σ⊙h (R2), on any domain | excluded: T is not orthant-planar | PROVED (R1/R2 class; Fact 1) |
| composition L⋆K₀ (L VB-type, K₀ orthant-planar) | excluded: n = 3 interior composed targets are VB-type (CC63 N3); also the one-cevian angle criterion fails, since no vertex is strictly visible from world 2 (`ta_scope.out` (d)) | PROVED (refereed REF63 C1, C11) |
| **single-level concave chart, no vetoes** (any concave sign-correct c on U = D_{K′} with K′ ⊆ relint T; includes every box chart) | **excluded** | **PROVED here** |
| continuous piecewise-linear single chart with the full certificate | excluded: the corner value system has rank 6 (`ta_scope.out` (b)) | CHECKED rank; exclusion PROVED modulo transferring C62 Lemmas 4.2–4.4 |
| 𝒱_B: measurable vetoes then a Brier-type error with one common weight | excluded if s counts as non-visible: Thm A then needs one π exposing both p and s | PROVED conditional on O61 Cor A(d) (one-world tangency at s; a sketch, not refereed) |

**Proof of the concave-chart exclusion (O61 Thm B, steps 3–6, run without vetoes).**
1. Suppose a single-level ranking with concave sign-correct c on U covers T. By separation (O61 §7 reduction), D_T is a Bayes set: D_T ⊇ U ∩ {π*·c > 0} and D_T ∩ {π*·c < 0} is null, for some π* ≥ 0, π* ≠ 0.
2. Γ := π*·c is continuous (concave on the open set U) and vanishes on ∂D_T∖0 ⊆ U (LF).
3. On each facet, Γ = 0, so each π*_j c_j is linear there. A sum of concave functions that is linear on a convex set has each summand affine there, and the facet is a cone containing 0.
4. If world j ∈ supp π* has a relint crossing on a facet, then c_j = s_j h_j on that facet. The slope s_j is the same on every crossing facet and is positive. This is O61 step 5: the superdifferential at a crossing point is {s_j e_j}, and c_j ≤ s_j h_j globally.
5. For T, world 1 crosses only on F_q; worlds 2 and 3 cross on F_p and F_s (`ta_scope.out` (a)). At each extreme ray every world with r_j ≠ 0 crosses on an adjacent facet, and c₁ = 0 at r_ps, where r₁ = 0. Hence Σ_j π*_j s_j r_j = 0 at the three rays.
6. The ray matrix is nonsingular: det = −1/625 for the rays as listed, and 5 for the integer rays (2,2,−3), (−1,4,−1), (0,−1,1). So π*⊙s = 0, hence π* = 0, a contradiction. ∎

The cover of §2 is concave at the error level too (𝒱_conv, O61's class). It escapes only because the veto removes F_q from what the chart has to decide.

## 6. What remains unreached, and one scoped lemma

Categories, as computed in `census.py` (bad = not strictly visible):
- **V:** at most one bad vertex.
- **X:** exactly two bad vertices, joined by a cevian edge.
- **N:** exactly two bad vertices, joined by a non-cevian edge.
- **N33:** the subclass of N in which both bad facets meet all three coordinate planes. In `census.py` the label "N" is used only for N∖N33.

V and X are covered. N, N33 and 3 (unless all edges are cevian) are open.

### 6.1 Corner-mismatch lemma (PROVED; scoped)

**Lemma CM.** Let r be a non-cevian extreme ray of cl D_T shared by facets F_a and F_b. Suppose each facet has three *forced coordinates* (h_m vanishes somewhere on the facet minus 0, for every m). Let π* ≫ 0. Then there are no nonzero linear maps L_a: v_a^⊥ → π*^⊥ and L_b: v_b^⊥ → π*^⊥ such that both of the following hold:
- (L_a h)_m = 0 wherever h_m = 0 on F_a, for every m, and likewise for L_b;
- L_a(r) = κ L_b(r) for some κ > 0.

*Proof.*
1. On the plane v_a^⊥, each row (L_a)_m vanishes on the line {h_m = 0} ∩ v_a^⊥, so (L_a h)_m = λ_m h_m.
2. Then π*·L_a h = 0 on v_a^⊥ forces π*⊙λ ∝ v_a. So L_a h = θ_a (v_a ⊘ π*)⊙h with θ_a ≠ 0, and likewise for L_b.
3. Direction matching at r gives v_a⊙r ∝ v_b⊙r. Since r has no zero coordinate, v_a ∝ v_b, hence v_a = v_b, which is impossible. ∎

**What it excludes.** Any construction in which a single chart decides both F_a and F_b near r and has the following three properties:
- (FL) its direction field is linear on each of F_a, F_b near r;
- (F) the forced-coordinate property holds on each facet, which follows from sign-correctness and continuity of the direction field across the coordinate planes at the crossing points;
- (DC) direction continuity at r.

This covers vetoes elsewhere, as long as both bad facets are left to the chart. For continuous PL single charts, (FL) and (F) are C62 Lemmas 4.3–4.4 (refereed). For PL charts with jumps they are CONJECTURE (C62 §6.1). CHECKED: in every N33 triangle sampled (1 695), v_a⊙r ∦ v_b⊙r. Every two-cevian triangle of class N is N33, including CC63's two-cevian example.

### 6.2 First-order corner systems (CHECKED, `survey.out`, `census.out`)

The *value* system (continuous PL single charts) has full rank 6 on every partially cevian triangle with at least two bad vertices tested (556 random triangles, `survey.out`). So continuous PL single charts never cover them, modulo the C62 transfer.

The *direction* system (only directions matched at corners) has real admissible solutions for:
- T itself (unique: `ta_scope.out` (c));
- all 250 sampled one-cevian triangles in N∖N33;
- 80 of 271 one-cevian and 296 of 483 two-cevian 3-bad triangles.

The reversed triangle has no real solution (C62 Cor 4.5). Where a solution exists, the first-order obstruction that excludes the reversed triangle is absent. Whether a global chart exists there is open.

### 6.3 Side test: veto followed by a non-concave orthant-diagonal chart

Is coherence automatic if the box chart of Theorem BX is replaced by an R2 chart of a non-box orthant-planar polygon (the cyclic triangle)?
- No. There are exact witnesses of non-convex lexicographic Bayes sets for some orthant scalings (7 witnesses; `veto_r2_test2.out`; CHECKED witnesses).
- With unit scalings no witness was found in about 10⁴ accepted pairs (NUMERICS).
- So in this architecture concavity of the chart, not merely orthant-planarity, is what makes Theorem BX work. For triangles this loses nothing (§3 proof, step 5).
