I read the report and the composition and negative referee reports. My assessment is that round 63 made real progress, but its evidence supports a more cautious conclusion than “the reversed triangle is probably impossible.”

**What we have learned**

The composition theorem delivered genuinely new exact coverage in four worlds and at the boundary in three worlds. That is a successful construction programme, even though it did not enlarge the three-world interior class.

The negative work is increasingly systematic: several substantial families fail at related corner configurations. That justifies investigating whether a common axiom-level obstruction lies underneath.

However, these are **not independent failures of arbitrary rankings**. Much of the machinery still passes through scalarisation, supporting faces, local linear structure, or transitivity. Repeatedly finding the same corner equations may show that those architectures share a limitation. It does not establish that the axioms force those equations.

Two scope corrections also matter:

- Composition does help with some three-world **boundary** targets; “nothing new” concerns the interior setting.
- The report excludes composed contaminations whose centre has **exactly one zero coordinate**. It explicitly leaves multiple zeros untreated. The status message overstates that exclusion.

**1. Authorise the impossibility attempt—but give it a different task**

Do not start by extending another scoring exclusion. Start by translating the proposed obstruction into comparisons between actual models.

The central question should be:

> Does global coherence of undominated models, together with the dominance requirement, force enough comparisons around the reversed triangle to contradict guidance or (TC), without introducing prices?

The collar trade is a useful source of rivals, but its numerical positivity is not itself an ordinal argument. “Their summed payoff is positive at every world” does not imply that every permitted ranking prefers adding them. That inference is precisely what a general proof must earn.

Require the negative argument to identify:

1. the measurable rivals;
2. which comparisons the axioms force, including unresolved disjunctions between worlds;
3. where coherent undominated dominators are required;
4. how independence transfers those comparisons;
5. the final guidance or (TC) violation.

No preassigned defender family, selection, scalarisation, or additive cancellation may enter silently.

A valuable outcome short of impossibility would be **one new necessary relational lemma**, proved from those assumptions, which genuinely excludes a mechanism not already excluded by the scoring results.

**2. The binding order condition is (TC), not transitivity; completeness is not required**

Use the original weaker condition:

\[
A\preceq_i B,\quad B\preceq_i C
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\neg(C\prec_i A).
\]

Do not assume that this gives \(A\preceq_i C\). Do not assume completeness.

This matters immediately. E61’s pointwise-best/priority-region argument uses a transitivity-like condition, labelled (TP), to concatenate an accepted comparison with a guidance comparison. That implication is not supplied by (TC) alone.

For example, the pattern
\[
Y\preceq_i X,\qquad X\prec_i M,\qquad Y\ \text{and}\ M\ \text{incomparable}
\]
does not by itself violate (TC). Transitivity would require the missing comparison; (TC) need not.

Therefore the first foundational task is:

> Reprove the precise collar/priority lemma needed for the negative argument under (TC), or exhibit why it fails without (TP).

If the proof needs transitivity, publish the conclusion conditionally. But do not call it the agreed axiom-level impossibility, and do not add transitivity to the framework merely to obtain it.

The remaining binding requirements are strict (G), the original (IB), global coherence, the agreed domain conditions and target-local freedom, direct (C), target undominatedness, and the incoherent-model dominance conclusion. Preserve their actual definitions.

In particular:

- **(S) and (B):** not requirements.
- **(W):** a useful stronger objective, not a default assumption for a general impossibility.
- **Finite scores, continuity, homogeneity, convexity, completeness:** optional hypotheses requiring explicit labels.

**3. Keep the positive branch, but make it challenge the proposed obstruction**

The unfinished cap analysis should not be reported as an exclusion. The referee distinguishes an explicit failed system, several local lemmas, and the still-open general cap conjecture.

More broadly, the constructive task should now be paired with the negative one:

> Find a ranking mechanism satisfying guidance, independence and (TC) that escapes the proposed relational collar lemma, then investigate whether its undominated models can be made coherent.

The important escape may be a deliberately missing comparison, rather than another scoring formula. But a local counterexample alone is only a diagnostic—not a cover.

I would also keep **one partially cevian triangle** as a secondary exact target. The proposed three-world characterisation should be attacked with a potential counterexample before becoming a classification programme. We currently have a classification of known constructions, not of all permissible rankings.

**4. Go ahead with the covering paper now**

Do not wait for the three-world characterisation. The paper already has three meaningful positive components:

- veto–Brier coverage;
- orthant-planar coverage;
- composition, with genuinely new higher-dimensional and boundary examples.

The dimensional limitation of composition is itself informative. Present it alongside the positive theorem rather than treating it as a disappointment.

Keep the scoped exclusions clearly separate from the open universal question. The paper should not conclude that pari-mutuel models are impermissible unless a theorem under the substantive framework actually establishes that.

**What this means for v3.2**

The revised organisation is the right safeguard. Nothing in round 63 warrants strengthening its axioms.

The crucial distinction is now:

- Some obstacles are substantive: guidance can force a model to be undominated, and global coherence then constrains its advice.
- Others come from the chosen proof architecture: a common numerical representation, regular local charts, or a particular supporting-face analysis.

The next round should locate exactly where the reversed-triangle argument crosses from the first category into the second.

**My recommended round 64 is therefore:**

1. **Main negative task:** an ordinal collar argument under incomplete (TC) relations, starting with the disputed transitivity step.
2. **Main positive task:** construct an escape from that precise lemma and test its admissibility consequences.
3. **Secondary geometric task:** challenge the proposed three-world characterisation on one partially cevian triangle.
4. **Writing:** proceed with the three positive covering theorems and their accurately scoped limitations.

That is a justified strategic shift. It pursues a genuine impossibility without mistaking another restricted exclusion for one—and keeps open the possibility that incomparability, rather than more elaborate prices, is the missing mechanism.
