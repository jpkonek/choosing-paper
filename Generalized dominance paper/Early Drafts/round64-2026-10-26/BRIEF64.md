## The binding framework (use exactly this; anything else is an extra hypothesis and must be labelled)
- Finitely many worlds Ω = {1,…,n}; for the reversed triangle n = 3. Gambles h ∈ ℝⁿ; μ is Lebesgue measure (or a measure with a positive density). A *model* is a measurable X ⊆ U, identified with models a.e. equal to it. U is the domain.
- Domain: decomposable (closed under splices (X∩S)∪(Y∖S) and under limits in measure), with local freedom (LF) at the target D_K (W62 definition). One-sided or two-sided domains are both allowed.
- Each world i has a relation ⪯_i on models. Assumed: reflexivity, a.e.-equal models tied, and
  **(TC)**: A ⪯_i B and B ⪯_i C imply ¬(C ≺_i A),
  where X ≺_i Y means X ⪯_i Y and ¬(Y ⪯_i X). **Not assumed: transitivity, (TP), completeness.** The pattern Y ⪯_i X, X ≺_i M, with Y and M incomparable, is permitted.
- **(G)** strict guidance and **(IB)** independence exactly as in `W62_note.tex` §2. (By (IB), each ⪯_i is determined by a relation on *records*: the pair (X∩S, Y∩S) for any S ⊇ X△Y.)
- Dominance: Y weakly dominates X if Y ⪯_i X at every i; Y dominates X if in addition Y ≺_i X at some i. Undominated = dominated by nothing.
- Requirements for a cover of K: (G); (IB); **global coherence** (every undominated model is coherent, i.e. a.e. equal to D_Q for a credal set Q); **direct (C)** (a model tied at every world with a coherent undominated model is coherent and undominated); **target undominated** (D_K is a model and undominated); **incoherent-model dominance** (every incoherent model is dominated by a coherent undominated model); (LF) at D_K.
- NOT requirements: (S), (B), (W) (a useful stronger objective only), finite scores, continuity, homogeneity, convexity of levels, completeness, transitivity, numerical representation, uniqueness, regularity, single charts, menu forms. Any of these used in a proof must be named as a hypothesis, and the result is then a scoped (conditional) result, not the axiom-level one.

## Roles

### OR64 — ordinal collar argument (main negative task)
Target: the reversed triangle K = {m ∈ Δ₃ : m_i ≤ 2/5} (vertices (1,2,2)/5 and permutations; D_K is the open cone on r_j = 2·𝟙 − 5e_j).

1. **(TC) audit of E61 first.** E61 Lemma 3.1 uses (TP): "Y ⪯_j X and X pointwise at least as good at j as M ⇒ Y ⪯_j M". (TC) does not supply this. For each of E61 Lemma 3.1, Cor 3.2 (box lemma), Cor 3.3 (inclusion vetoes), Lemma 3.4 (collar lemma) and the broad impossibility theorem: either reprove it under the binding framework (reflexivity + (TC), no completeness), or exhibit precisely why it fails without (TP) — ideally with an explicit relational counter-model satisfying the axioms in the relevant local configuration. Separate two questions: (i) does the lemma hold under (TC) for E61's definition of "priority region"? (ii) is there a different, ordinal notion of priority under which the lemma is transitivity-free? *Lead's observation, to be checked, not assumed:* if R is a "strong" priority region at j (no model meeting R non-null is ⪯_j any model avoiding R), then the box-lemma conclusion "X_i = (U∖R) ∩ {h_i > 0} is undominated" seems to follow from strict (G) at i alone, with no transitivity. If true, the real question moves to (iii): do the axioms force any such region to exist? A ranking class merely *having* vetoes is architecture.
2. **The ordinal argument.** Answer the central question in AUDIT_r64 §1: does global coherence of undominated models, with the dominance requirements, force enough comparisons around the reversed triangle to contradict (G) or (TC), with no prices? The argument must identify explicitly:
   1. the measurable rivals used;
   2. which comparisons the axioms force, including unresolved disjunctions over worlds ("some world refuses …");
   3. where coherent undominated dominators are required, and what they must look like;
   4. how (IB) transfers comparisons between contexts;
   5. the final (G) or (TC) violation.
   No preassigned defender family, selection, scalarisation or additive cancellation may enter silently. Positivity of a summed payoff is not an ordinal fact: "the collar trade improves Brier at every world" does not imply any world must prefer it.
   Useful starting facts (verify them): (a) any model pointwise best at one world is undominated by strict (G) alone, so global coherence constrains such models; (b) if P is disjoint from D_K and D_K ∪ P is incoherent, then some world refuses "add P" (¬(D_K∪P ⪯_i D_K)), by target undominatedness and direct (C); by (IB) that refusal holds in every context; (c) every incoherent model, e.g. D_K ∪ P, needs a coherent undominated dominator D_Q.
3. **Ledger.** For the existing exclusions of the reversed triangle (E61 broad impossibility; O61 Thm A; IN63 Thm C; DC63; C62), locate the exact step at which each crosses from consequences of the binding framework into architecture (common numerical representation, regular local charts, supporting-face analysis, transitivity, completeness, preassigned defenders).
4. **Success criteria, in order:** (A) an axiom-level impossibility for the reversed triangle under the binding framework; or (B) a conditional impossibility whose extra hypothesis is named and shown to be needed by the argument (do not call it axiom-level); or (C) at least one new *necessary relational lemma*, proved from the binding framework, that excludes a mechanism not already excluded by the scoring results.
