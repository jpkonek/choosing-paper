## 0. Outcome

**The full construction is not obtained.** I did not find three global record orders with a closed competitive coherent selection on all of Δ through T1. I also have no proof that none exists.

What I did obtain is a set of theorems about the three classes of global orders that BRIEF21 suggests. Each theorem says exactly what the class can and cannot contribute. Together they pin down the only kind of world that can still do the non-additive work.

1. **Unanimity at the first level, over any family of bounded charges, of any cardinality (Thm AA1.1, PROVED).**
   - Such a world either adds up in the ladder sense, or it has a non-null region Λ′. Every non-null subset of Λ′ contains a non-null set that the whole family ignores.
   - On that region the order is decided entirely by the tie-breaker.
   - Neither non-countability nor finite additivity of the family helps. Combined with M3 (no cover on (LF)+(Av) domains has every world adding up), this gives: in every cover, the non-additive work of some world is done by its tie-breaker, on a region where every endorsing family ignores some non-null subset of every non-null set.
2. **Orders generated by basic exchanges closed under composition (Prop AA1.2, PROVED).**
   - Composition preserves endorsement by any bounded charge. So the generated world adds up whenever its generators have a common strictly positive endorser.
   - Non-additivity must therefore be written into the generators.
   - A finite certificate against adding up can never consist of generators that compose in sequence. It needs records with overlapping, non-composable supports, as in D2's designs.
3. **Geometric or lexicographic primaries (Thm AA1.3, PROVED; the main new result).** This extends L3.4 from vetoes to every weakly guiding additive primary.
   - Suppose world i keeps the strict comparisons of a scalar, weakly guiding, countably additive primary F. Then along every edge [e_i, e_j] that pays F, the function t ↦ F(C_t) is continuous up to its first hit of F(B^j).
   - At an uncountable set of edge models, world i's comparisons out of those models must coincide **exactly** with F's.
   - Consequences:
     - (a) A primary that is blind on a vertex-adjacent wedge of the edge is impossible. This covers lexicographic and veto-type first levels with such blind sets.
     - (b) After a strictly guiding primary, a tie-breaker of any kind, with no representability assumed, must be inactive at uncountably many edge models. A tie-breaker that is active at every non-vertex edge model is impossible. For lex(F, S) this becomes an exact separation condition (Sep). Moreover, retaining a strictly guiding primary makes the world add up (Lemma AA1.3d), so such a world can never be the non-additive one. This constrains, on the edges, the route that Z3 §2.4 lists as uncovered by Y1.4: "arbitrary non-representable tie-breakers after an additive level 0".
     - (c) Every nonzero primary is paid on at least one of the two edges at its world.
4. **Competitive sets of unanimity triples (Prop AA1.4, PROVED).**
   - Under unanimity, the competitive coherent set at p is contained in the intersection, over all member triples, of their face-Bayes sets.
   - T1 ∈ Γ(u) forces T1 to be Bayes for every member triple.
   - So unanimity shrinks the competitive sets; it does not enlarge them.

**Combined statement (Thm AA1.5, PROVED from the above, M3 and X3.2).** In every cover of T1 on the full domain:
- some world k does not add up, and for every family F of bounded charges endorsing it, Λ_F is non-null;
- world k retains no strictly guiding additive primary, and every primary it retains is blind on its blind set Λ_k;
- every primary retained by any world, and paid on an edge E_ij, is not totally blind on any vertex-adjacent wedge W^{ij}_η, and the world coincides with it out of a continuum of record models.

So, of the order types the brief proposed, none can be the non-additive world of a cover:
- unanimity at the first level, over any family of charges, including weightings built from cones or sign patterns of the target;
- retained scalar weightings, whether geometric or lexicographic;
- exchange generators with a common positive endorser.

Its acceptance set must be a non-cone family of records whose non-endorsability is witnessed only by overlapping, non-chainable supports (D2-type). Any primary it retains must avoid wedge blindness on the edges.

**Nearby reachable result and proposed shift of aim.** The reachable statement is the classification above. The shift I propose is to stop searching among orders defined by geometric primitives, unanimities or priorities. Instead, first exhibit a **three-world full-domain system on all of Δ, for any coherent target, with a world of D2 type that does not add up**. It is not yet known that such worlds can live in a three-world cover with closed sections on the whole simplex. If they cannot, T1 is settled negatively by M3; if they can, the construction shows how such a world handles the edge record points of Thm AA1.3.

**Plain-English status.**
- No cover was built.
- The orders the brief suggested trying (unanimity over large families, orders built from geometric primitives, lexicographic priorities) are each shown to be unable to do the needed non-additive work.
- What is left is a single, sharply described kind of world.
- Whether such a world can exist in a three-world system on the whole simplex is the open question I recommend next.

## 1. Setting: record orders and the competitive correspondence

**Records and the transition lemma** (X1 Thm X1.1.1, AUDIT_nonlinear_programme). A world is a set A_i of realizable transition records with:
- ι ∈ A_i;
- closure under composition of composable records;
- every i-worsening record in A_i;
- no nontrivial i-improving record in A_i.

Then X ⪯_i Y iff rec(X, Y) ∈ A_i. Every preorder with (IB) and strict (G) arises this way.

**Ladders** (write-up §8.6(i)). For world k and a.e. mixed pair g with advice range {+, 0, −}, the ladder has two steps: silence (step 1) and wrong acceptance (step 2). Contradictory advice × is ranked last. The ladder profile λ_k(X) ∈ {0,1}^{steps}, and the ladder difference of a record is

  δ_k(r) := λ_k(Y) − λ_k(X)  for r = rec(X, Y).

It depends on r only, and

  δ_k(r∘s) = δ_k(r) + δ_k(s),

by telescoping, since rec(X, Z) = rec(X, Y)∘rec(Y, Z). Relevant comparisons, endorsers, (NC) and blind sets are as in §8.6(i) and Lemma M30. A bounded charge ν ≥ 0 on Ld_k that vanishes on null sets **endorses** world k if ν(δ_k(r)) ≥ 0 for every accepted record (or every relevant one; the results below hold for either reading). World k **adds up** if some endorser is positive on every non-null ladder set. By Lemma M30(c), that is equivalent to an endorser whose countably additive density is positive a.e.

**The competitive correspondence.** For a triple of world orders, put

  Γ(p) := {C coherent and undominated : C competitive on supp p}.

A cover is a selection p ↦ C_p ∈ Γ(p) with closed sections for every evaluable X, together with global (C), and T1 = C_{p*} for some p*. Obligation 1 asks to define the orders first and then determine Γ.

For complete additive triples, Γ(p) is the set of coherent, undominated Bayes models at weights w with supp w ⊆ supp p. This is Aumann convexity plus separation (Y3.7 Step 0), not Bayes at the index. Prop AA1.4 extends this to unanimities.

**Standing necessary conditions used** (refereed or once-read results, as listed in BRIEF21):
- (M3) On every decomposable domain with (LF) and (Av), in particular the full domain, no ranking with strict (G), (IB), (LA), (S), (C) and T1 ∈ C_L has every world adding up (write-up §8.6(i)).
- (L3.1) Vertex models are precise.
- (L3.4) Vetoes cannot be paid.
- (Thm gm) No additive cover.

## 2. Unanimity at the first level over arbitrary families of charges

Let F be a nonempty family of bounded charges P ≥ 0 on Ld_k that vanish on null sets. Normalise the nonzero members to states. Let 𝓜 be the weak-* closed convex hull of the normalised family, and for P ∈ 𝓜 let ρ_P be the density of its countably additive part (Yosida–Hewitt). Put

  Λ_F := ess sup { A ⊆ Ld_k : ρ_P = 0 a.e. on A for every P ∈ 𝓜 }.

The class in braces is closed under countable unions, so the essential supremum is attained by one of its members, unique mod null.

World k is **endorsed by F** if P(δ_k(r)) ≥ 0 for every P ∈ F and every accepted record r. It is a **first-level unanimity over F with tie-breaker ⪯′** if, writing δ := δ_k(rec(X,Y)), X ⪯_k Y holds iff one of:
- P(δ) ≥ 0 for all P ∈ F, with > 0 for some P;
- P(δ) = 0 for all P ∈ F, and X ⪯′ Y.

Here ⪯′ is any relation that makes ⪯_k a preorder with (IB) and strict (G). No condition is placed on ⪯′ beyond that: it may be incomplete, non-representable, or lexicographic.

**Theorem AA1.1 (PROVED; one input transferred by inspection, see the end of the proof).** Let world k be endorsed by F, of any cardinality.
- **(a)** There is a bounded charge ν that endorses world k and whose countably additive density is positive a.e. off Λ_F. Hence world k's blind set satisfies Λ_k ⊆ Λ_F. If Λ_F is null, world k adds up.
- **(b)** If 𝓜 charges every non-null ladder set (sup_{P∈𝓜} P(A) > 0 for all non-null A), then Λ_F is null.
- **(c)** Every non-null A ⊆ Λ_F contains a non-null N with P(N) = 0 for all P ∈ 𝓜. For every record r whose ladder difference is supported in N, P(δ_k(r)) = 0 for every P ∈ F.
- **(d)** If world k is a first-level unanimity over F, it is endorsed by F. On records whose ladder difference is supported in a set N as in (c), its order is exactly the tie-breaker: X ⪯_k Y iff X ⪯′ Y.
- **(e)** If F consists of countably additive measures, then Λ_F = ess ⋂_P {ρ_P = 0}. It is attained on a countable subfamily (P_n), and ν = Σ_n 2^{−n} P_n works in (a).

*Proof.*
- **Endorsement passes to 𝓜.** For bounded δ, P ↦ P(δ) is linear and weak-* continuous, and {P : P(δ) ≥ 0} is closed and convex.
- **(a)**
  - By the definition of Λ_F, Λ_F^c is the essential union of the sets {ρ_P > 0}, P ∈ 𝓜. In a finite measure space an essential union is attained by a countable subfamily (P_n).
  - Put ν := Σ_n 2^{−n} P_n. This is a norm-convergent series of states, so ν is a state. Since |δ_k| ≤ 1, ν(δ) = Σ 2^{−n} P_n(δ) ≥ 0 for every accepted record.
  - The countably additive band projection on ba is linear and norm-continuous, so ρ_ν = Σ 2^{−n} ρ_{P_n} > 0 a.e. on Λ_F^c.
  - Every endorser's density vanishes on Λ_k (Lemma M30(c), the canonical blind set is minimal), and ν is an endorser. Hence Λ_k ⊆ Λ_F.
  - If Λ_F is null, ν is positive on every non-null ladder set, so world k adds up.
- **(c)** On A ⊆ Λ_F, every P ∈ 𝓜 has zero countably additive part. 𝓜 is weak-* compact and convex. The key lemma of Theorem fullsupport (N22 Lemma 1.1; write-up §5, proof sketch of Thm fullsupport) gives a non-null N ⊆ A with P(N) = 0 for every P ∈ 𝓜. If the ladder difference δ of r is supported in N, then |δ| ≤ 1_N, so |P(δ)| ≤ P(N) = 0.
- **(b)** Suppose Λ_F were non-null. By (c), Λ_F contains a non-null N that every P ∈ 𝓜 annihilates, against the hypothesis. (This is Thm fullsupport.)
- **(d)** An accepted record satisfies P(δ) ≥ 0 for all P in either clause, so world k is endorsed by F. For records supported in N, every P(δ) = 0 by (c), so only the second clause can apply, and it reads X ⪯′ Y.
- **(e)** For a countably additive P, ρ_P is its whole density. Convex combinations and weak-* limits of such states do not enlarge the essential union of positivity sets beyond that of F: convex combinations of members are countably additive, so if they all vanish on A, any weak-* limit Q of them has Q(B) = lim P_α(B) = 0 for every B ⊆ A. (This step needs countable additivity of the members. For purely finitely additive members it fails; see the example below.) The rest is (a).

*Transfer.* Thm fullsupport and its key lemma are stated on L^∞(H, μ). The sketch uses only:
- a finite measure space;
- Kneser's minimax theorem on {0 ≤ f ≤ 1_{A′}};
- the countable chain condition.

All of these hold on (Ld_k, μ⊗#). This is the same transfer as Lemma M30(c) makes for Theorem K31; here it is by inspection of the printed sketch. ∎

**Example (why 𝓜 and not F).** Let F consist of the purely finitely additive {0,1}-valued states P_x, x ∈ H, where P_x contains every set of μ-density 1 at x. Then:
- every density ρ_P with P ∈ F is 0;
- yet F charges every non-null set (take x a density point);
- so by (b), Λ_F is null, and the world adds up.

A definition of Λ through the members of F alone would have called this world totally blind. Purely finitely additive members do not produce blindness unless the whole closed convex hull fails to charge some set. (PROVED: the density-point ultrafilters exist by Zorn's lemma, since the sets of density 1 at x form a filter that contains no null set.)

**Corollary AA1.1′ (PROVED from AA1.1 and M3).** In every cover of T1 on the full domain there is a world k with the following property: every family F of bounded charges endorsing world k has Λ_F non-null.

If that world is a first-level unanimity over some F, then:
- every non-null subset of Λ_F contains non-null sets on which the world is its tie-breaker;
- the non-additive content of the cover lies in that tie-breaker.

Neither non-countability nor finite additivity of F contributes anything. Only the weak-* closed convex hull matters, and when it charges every set the world adds up.

*Proof.* By M3 some world k does not add up. If some F endorsing k had Λ_F null, k would add up by (a). The rest is (c), (d). ∎

**What AA1.1 excludes.** It excludes, as sources of the required non-additivity:
- BRIEF21's "unanimity at the FIRST level over a non-countable family";
- credal-set worlds (CP-a);
- unanimities of additive worlds;
- "unanimity over a set of geometric weightings".

Each of these either adds up, and so falls under M3, or passes the work to a tie-breaker on a region that the family ignores.

## 3. Orders generated by basic exchanges

Let G_k be a set of realizable records, the **generators**. Let A_k be the least set containing ι, G_k and every k-worsening record, and closed under composition. When A_k contains no nontrivial k-improving record, it is a world by the transition lemma; call it **the world generated by G_k**.

**Proposition AA1.2 (PROVED).**
- **(a)** A bounded charge ν ≥ 0 on Ld_k that endorses every generator (ν(δ_k(g)) ≥ 0) endorses the generated world. In particular, if some ν that is positive on every non-null ladder set endorses G_k, the generated world adds up.
- **(b)** Let r_1, …, r_m be accepted records that compose in sequence (r_1∘r_2 is defined, then (r_1∘r_2)∘r_3, and so on). Then Σ_j δ_k(r_j) is never ≤ 0 a.e. while being nonzero. So a certificate against adding up, of the form Σ_j λ_j δ_k(r_j) ≤ 0 and not a.e. 0, can never consist of sequentially composable accepted records with all λ_j = 1.

*Proof.*
- **(a)** Ladder differences add under composition: δ_k(r∘s) = δ_k(r) + δ_k(s), by telescoping of profiles. A worsening record has δ_k ≥ 0. The set of records with ν(δ_k) ≥ 0 therefore contains the generators, ι and the worsenings, and it is closed under composition. So it contains A_k.
- **(b)** The composite r := r_1∘⋯∘r_m is accepted, and δ_k(r) = Σ_j δ_k(r_j). A record with ladder difference ≤ 0, nonzero on a non-null set, is nontrivial and k-improving, hence not accepted. ∎

So in a "carefully chosen set of basic exchanges closed under composition only", the non-additivity must be written into the generators: the generators must have no common strictly positive endorser. And the certificates that witness this must use records that cannot be chained, because their supports overlap with mismatched middle advice. This is the mechanism of D2's projective-plane designs, in which two generators overlap on a set where their sum would be 2. It also agrees with the necessary conditions N17.3 for (M1) certificates. Part (b) is elementary and is stated only so that a generator search can apply it as a filter.

## 4. Retained primaries on an edge: L3.4 for every weakly guiding additive primary

**Definitions.**
- A **weakly guiding additive primary at world i** is F(X) := ∫_H ℓ(g, a_X(g)) dμ(g), where ℓ ≥ 0 is measurable with an integrable envelope, ℓ(g, ·) is nondecreasing in world-i rank, and ℓ(g, best_i(g)) = 0. In particular F(B^i) = 0.
- World i **retains** F if F(X) < F(Y) implies X ≺_i Y.
- The edge is E_ij = [e_i, e_j], with q_t := (1−t)e_i + t e_j. An **edge system** consists of:
  - a selection t ↦ C_t := C_{q_t}, with each C_t competitive on supp q_t;
  - world-i and world-j sections {t : C_t ⪯_i X} and {t : C_t ⪯_j X}, closed in [0,1] for every model X.
- F is **paid on E_ij** if P := F(B^j) > 0.

Competitiveness at the end points gives C_0 = B^i and C_1 = B^j (strict (G)). None of the following is used: (IB), coherence, (C), undominatedness, the third world, the target.

**Theorem AA1.3 (PROVED).** Let world i retain a weakly guiding additive primary F that is paid on E_ij, and let f(t) := F(C_t) for an edge system. Then:
- **(a)** t* := inf{t : f(t) ≥ P} lies in (0, 1]. f is continuous on [0, t*), and liminf_{t↑t*} f(t) ≥ P. Hence f maps [0, t*) onto [0, P).
- **(b) Record models.** For c ∈ (0, P), let τ(c) := min{t : f(t) = c}. This minimum exists, and τ(c) < t*. The map τ is strictly increasing, so there are uncountably many record models C_{τ(c)}. Out of each record model, world i coincides with F:
  - C_{τ(c)} ⪯_i X if F(X) ≥ c;
  - X ≺_i C_{τ(c)} if F(X) < c.
- **(c)** Consequently, no record model C_{τ(c)} admits a Z with F(Z) = c and Z ≺_i C_{τ(c)}. Equivalently: if hypothesis **(Imp)** holds (every edge model C ≠ B^i, B^j admits Z with F(Z) = F(C) and Z ≺_i C), then **no edge system exists**.

*Proof.*

**Step 1: lower semicontinuity below P.**
- For c ∈ (0, P) there is a model Y_c with F(Y_c) = c. Take the splice equal to B^j on a set A and to B^i off A. Then F(splice) = ∫_A ℓ(g, a_{B^j}) dμ, because ℓ(·, a_{B^i}) = 0. This is a non-atomic finite measure of total mass P, so Lyapunov gives every value in [0, P].
- By retention:
  - f(t) < c gives C_t ≺_i Y_c;
  - f(t) > c gives Y_c ≺_i C_t, hence C_t ⋠_i Y_c.
- So {f < c} ⊆ S_c := {t : C_t ⪯_i Y_c} ⊆ {f ≤ c}, and S_c is closed.
- For c′ < c < P this gives cl{f ≤ c′} ⊆ cl{f < c} ⊆ {f ≤ c}. Intersecting over c ↓ c′, {f ≤ c′} is closed for every c′ < P.
- Hence liminf_{s→t} f(s) ≥ min(f(t), P) at every t.

**Step 2: upper semicontinuity where f < P.**
- Let f(t) < P and c ∈ (f(t), P]. Suppose t_n → t with f(t_n) ≥ c.
- Let D be the set where C_t and B^j differ. For A ⊆ D, let Y_A be C_t with B^j's advice on A. Then F(Y_A) − f(t) = ∫_A [ℓ(g, a_{B^j}) − ℓ(g, a_{C_t})] dμ.
  - This is a non-atomic signed measure on D, with value P − f(t) > 0 at A = D.
  - By Lyapunov its range is an interval containing 0 and P − f(t).
  - Choose A with F(Y_A) = c′ ∈ (f(t), c). Then A is non-null.
- On A, B^j's advice is strictly better at world j, since rank is injective a.e. So Y_A ≺_j C_t by strict (G).
- Competitiveness at q_{t_n} (supp ⊆ {i, j}): C_{t_n} ⪯_i Y_A or C_{t_n} ⪯_j Y_A.
  - The first is impossible, since F(Y_A) = c′ < c ≤ f(t_n) gives Y_A ≺_i C_{t_n} by retention.
  - So C_{t_n} ⪯_j Y_A for all n. The closed world-j section gives C_t ⪯_j Y_A, which contradicts Y_A ≺_j C_t.
- Hence limsup_{s→t} f(s) ≤ f(t) wherever f(t) < P.

**Step 3: (a).**
- By Steps 1–2, f is continuous on the relatively open set {f < P}, which contains 0.
- If f(t*) < P, then f < P near t*, which contradicts the definition of t* unless t* = 1. But f(1) = P. So f(t*) ≥ P and t* > 0.
- Step 1 at t* gives liminf_{t↑t*} f(t) ≥ P.
- Intermediate values on [0, t*) complete (a).

**Step 4: (b) and (c).**
- {t < t* : f(t) = c} is nonempty by (a). Its infimum τ(c) satisfies f(τ(c)) = c, by continuity on [0, t*). The infimum is not t*, because f stays below P on [0, t*) and liminf_{t↑t*} f(t) ≥ P, so f ≥ (c+P)/2 > c on some (t* − δ, t*).
- For t < τ(c), f(t) < c, since f(0) = 0, f is continuous and τ(c) is the first hit.
- Let F(X) = c. For t < τ(c), retention gives C_t ≺_i X, so t ∈ {s : C_s ⪯_i X}. That set is closed, so C_{τ(c)} ⪯_i X.
- The cases F(X) ≷ c are retention.
- Monotonicity of τ: for c < c′, the path reaches c before c′, since f(0) = 0 and f is continuous.
- (c) is immediate from (b). ∎

**Relation to earlier results.**
- L3.4 is the special case where F is a veto (ℓ > 0 only at i-wrong acceptances), with the same two-step argument.
- AUDIT Prop 4 is the level-minimality in (b) at points that are approached from below.
- The new content is twofold:
  - **any** weakly guiding primary, paid on the edge, produces the continuum of record models, with no veto structure needed;
  - the conclusion is exact: world i equals F out of every record model.

**Corollary AA1.3a (blind wedges; PROVED, with one elementary step CHECKED).** For η > 0 let

  W^{ij}_η := {pairs with an orientation g such that g_i > 0 > g_j and g_i < η|g_j|}.

Suppose world i retains a primary F with ℓ(g, ·) ≡ 0 for a.e. pair in W^{ij}_η, and F is paid on E_ij. Suppose also that the edge models are coherent, so C_t = D_{K_t} with K_t ⊆ [e_i, e_j] (X3.2). Then no edge system exists.

*Proof.*
- **Every record model is K ≠ {e_i}.** For c ∈ (0, P), the record model C := C_{τ(c)} has F(C) = c > 0 = F(B^i), so C ≠ B^i, and therefore K := K_{τ(c)} ≠ {e_i}.
- **It errs at world i on part of the wedge.** Pick m ∈ K with m_j = b > 0. For g ∈ W^{ij}_η with g_i/|g_j| < min(η, b/(1−b)) (any ratio if b = 1):

    P_K(g) ≤ m_i g_i + m_j g_j = (1−b) g_i − b|g_j| < 0.

  So C does not accept g, and errs at world i on this open cone of pairs.
- **Correcting the error gives an F-neutral improvement.** Let Z be C with i-best advice on that cone. Since ℓ ≡ 0 there, F(Z) = F(C), and Z ≺_i C by strict (G). This contradicts Theorem AA1.3(c). ∎

CHECKED (`aa1py/edge_primary.out` (1)): the inequality (1−b)g_i + b g_j < 0 for g_i/|g_j| < b/(1−b), in 4000 exact rational samples, 0 failures. This is elementary, and the script only guards against a sign slip.

NUMERICS (`edge_primary.out` (2)): f(t) = F(C_t) along the posterior edge family ν(q_t ⊙ T1) at ε = 3/10, for the Brier-rank primary blind on W^{12}_{1/2} ∪ W^{21}_{1/2}:
- f is continuous and nondecreasing from 0 to P ≈ 0.712;
- it is flat (= 0) up to t ≈ 0.1, since those models err only inside the blind wedge.

So that family has record models at every level, as the theorem predicts for any family.

**Corollary AA1.3b (strictly guiding primary with an active tie-breaker; PROVED).** Let F be strictly guiding at world i (ℓ strictly increasing in rank a.e.), so F is paid on both incident edges. In every edge system, for every c ∈ (0, P), world i's comparisons out of C_{τ(c)} are exactly F's. In particular:
- **(i)** If world i strictly refines F at some edge model C ≠ B^i, B^j (some Z with F(Z) = F(C) has Z ≺_i C), that model is not a record model.
- **(ii)** Let world i be lex(F, S), where S is a second additive, strictly guiding level (or the first of any further levels). Let C ≠ B^i, B^j be an edge model, and let E and G be its world-i error set and correct set inside the opposite-sign region of E_ij. Write e_F(g), e_S(g) > 0 for the increments of F and S produced by moving C's advice at g one step at world i: down (a correction) on E, up (a worsening) on G.

  Then (Imp) holds at C unless

    ess sup_E (e_S/e_F) ≤ ess inf_G (e_S/e_F).        (Sep_C)

  *Proof.*
  - If (Sep_C) fails, there are non-null A₀ ⊆ E and B₀ ⊆ G and a constant κ with e_S/e_F > κ on A₀ and < κ on B₀.
  - Shrinking one of them, Lyapunov gives non-null A ⊆ A₀ and B ⊆ B₀ with ∫_A e_F = ∫_B e_F.
  - Let Z be C corrected on A and worsened on B. Then F(Z) = F(C) and

      S(Z) − S(C) = ∫_B e_S − ∫_A e_S < κ(∫_B e_F − ∫_A e_F) = 0.

  - So Z ≺_i C. ∎

  Hence, by (i), every record model C_{τ(c)} satisfies (Sep_{C_{τ(c)}}). That is an exact separation condition on the ratio e_S/e_F, imposed at a continuum of edge models. It is the edge form of Thm LX's proportionality, stated as a separation rather than an equality.
- **(iii)** Hence a lexicographic world with an additive first level carries an edge system only if its second level satisfies (Sep) at uncountably many nested record models, and its later levels are inactive there. This is the edge analogue of Thm LX's conclusion. Thm LX covers two levels; here the number of levels is arbitrary.
- More importantly, (i) applies to **arbitrary, non-representable tie-breakers after an additive level 0**: they must be inactive at uncountably many edge models of each incident edge. Z3 §2.4 lists this route as not covered by Y1.4; on the edges it is now constrained exactly.

**Corollary AA1.3c (every nontrivial primary is paid somewhere; PROVED).** Let F be weakly guiding at world i, with ℓ(g, wrong acceptance) > 0 on a non-null set of mixed pairs. Then F(B^j) > 0 or F(B^k) > 0.

*Proof.* On a mixed pair, B^j accepts the j-good orientation. That is i-wrong exactly when sign g_i ≠ sign g_j, and likewise for B^k. Since the pair is mixed, sign g_i differs from sign g_j or from sign g_k. So every mixed pair is i-wrongly accepted by B^j or by B^k. ∎

So Theorem AA1.3 applies to every retained primary that charges wrong acceptance anywhere, on at least one edge.

**Lemma AA1.3d (retention implies endorsement; PROVED, elementary).** If world i retains a weakly guiding additive primary F, then every accepted comparison satisfies F(X) ≤ F(Y). Hence the ladder charge of F, whose density is the step increments of ℓ (bounded, by the integrable envelope), endorses world i. In particular:
- a world that retains a strictly guiding additive primary adds up;
- a world that does not add up retains only primaries whose ladder densities vanish on its blind set Λ_i.

*Proof.* If X ⪯_i Y and F(Y) < F(X), retention gives Y ≺_i X, a contradiction. The rest is the definition of an endorser and the minimality of the canonical blind set (Lemma M30(c)). ∎

## 5. Competitive sets of unanimity triples

**Proposition AA1.4 (PROVED).** Let each world k satisfy: X ⪯_k Y implies ψ(X) ≤ ψ(Y) for every ψ in a family F_k. Here each ψ is an additive functional with weakly guiding, integrable penalties (countably additive). This covers pure unanimity, and first-level unanimity with any tie-breaker. Let C be competitive on S ⊆ Ω. Then, for every choice (ψ_k)_{k∈S} ∈ ∏_{k∈S} F_k, C is Bayes for (ψ_k)_{k∈S} at some w ∈ Δ with supp w ⊆ S. Hence

  Γ(p) ⊆ ⋂_{(ψ_k) ∈ ∏ F_k} { coherent Bayes models of (ψ_k)_{k∈supp p} at weights supported in supp p }.

*Proof.*
- For every X some k ∈ S has C ⪯_k X, hence ψ_k(C) ≤ ψ_k(X). So no X has ψ_k(X) < ψ_k(C) for all k ∈ S.
- The range {(ψ_k(X))_{k∈S}} is convex (Lyapunov on the full domain; finitely many integrable integrands).
- Separating the range minus (ψ_k(C)) from the open negative orthant gives a nonzero w ≥ 0 with w · (ψ(X) − ψ(C)) ≥ 0 for every X. ∎

**Corollary AA1.4′ (PROVED).** If T1 ∈ Γ(u), then T1 is Bayes for every member triple, each at some weight.

In particular, if every F_k contains the Brier functional ψ^B_k(X) := ∫ rk_k(g, a_X(g)) |g_k| dμ, then T1 ∉ Γ(u). The reason:
- the Brier triple's Bayes models at w are a.e. the precise models {w · g > 0} (Lemma Y3.1(b) with m ≡ 1, and B^k at vertices);
- none of these equals D_{T1}.

So adding members to a unanimity family can only remove models from Γ. Unanimity makes competitiveness harder, never easier. With AA1.1 it is not a route to a cover.

## 6. What a cover's worlds must look like, and what is left

**Theorem AA1.5 (PROVED from AA1.1′, AA1.3, AA1.3a–d and M3; uses X3.2 for coherent edge models).** Every cover of T1 on the full two-sided domain has the following properties.
- **(i)** Some world k does not add up. For every family F of bounded charges endorsing world k, the set Λ_F is non-null, and world k is not determined by F on non-null subsets of Λ_F (Thm AA1.1(c)).
- **(ii)** That world k retains no strictly guiding additive primary. Every primary it retains endorses it and is blind on Λ_k (Lemma AA1.3d).
- **(iii)** For **every** world i and every additive primary F that i retains and that is paid on an edge E_ij:
  - F is not totally blind on any wedge W^{ij}_η (Cor AA1.3a);
  - i coincides with F out of uncountably many record models of E_ij (Thm AA1.3(b)).

  Every primary charging wrong acceptance somewhere is paid on at least one edge (Cor AA1.3c).
- **(iv)** If world i retains a strictly guiding additive primary F (so i adds up, and i ≠ k), its tie-breaking is inactive at the record models of both incident edges. For lex(F, S), the separation condition (Sep) holds at each record model (Cor AA1.3b).
- **(v)** If some world's order is generated by basic exchanges, its generators have no common strictly positive endorser, and every finite certificate of that uses non-chainable records (Prop AA1.2).
- **(vi)** If every world is a unanimity of families containing the Brier functional, T1 ∉ Γ(u) (Cor AA1.4′).

*Proof.* (i) is AA1.1′. (ii) is AA1.3d applied to the world k of (i). (iii) holds because a cover restricts to an edge system on every edge, with coherent edge models (X3.2), and then AA1.3, AA1.3a, AA1.3c apply. (iv) is AA1.3b. (v) is AA1.2. (vi) is AA1.4′. ∎

**What this excludes, in the brief's own terms.**

| Proposed global order | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Unanimity at the first level over a non-countable family of countably additive weightings | Adds up, or is a tie-breaker on Λ_F (AA1.1(e)) |
| Same, over finitely additive or "geometric" charges (cones of the target, sign patterns, credal weightings) | Only the closed convex hull matters; adds up when it charges every set (AA1.1(b)) |
| Lexicographic priority with an additive first level that is blind on a vertex-adjacent wedge (e.g. "first count errors where the target's geometry says they matter") | Impossible on the edge (AA1.3a) |
| Strictly guiding primary + arbitrary non-representable tie-breakers | Tie-breakers must be inactive at a continuum of edge models (AA1.3b), and the world adds up (AA1.3d), so it cannot be the non-additive world |
| Exchange-generated orders with endorsable generators | Add up (AA1.2) |
| Unanimity triples containing Brier members | T1 not competitive (AA1.4′) |

**What is left.** The non-additive world k of a cover must:
- (a) retain no strictly guiding primary;
- (b) not be determined, on Λ_F, by any family of charges;
- (c) have an acceptance set that is not a convex cone of ladder differences, its non-endorsability being witnessed only by non-chainable records with overlapping supports;
- (d) avoid wedge-blind retained primaries on the edges.

The only known worlds of this kind in full systems are D2's world 1 (two worlds, U-domain, a nested chain, projective-plane designs) and the doubly blind two-world systems of T2n. D2's world 1 is consistent with (a)–(d):
- it retains no primary, since rule R⁺ accepts D(δ) > 0 only under a tail condition;
- its generators overlap in every neighbourhood of the chain's end.

**CONJECTURE AA1.C (the next decisive question).** There is a three-world system on the full two-sided domain, with strict (G), (IB), (LA), (S) on all of Δ (closed sections for every model) and (C), for **some** coherent target set, in which one world does not add up.

This is not about T1. If it is false for every target, then by M3 no non-additive cover of anything that needs a non-adding-up world exists in three worlds, and T1 is settled negatively. If it is true, its construction must show how a D2-type world handles the record models of Thm AA1.3 on all three edges at once. That is exactly the interaction that no two-world construction tests.

**Why I did not proceed to a T1 construction.** By AA1.5, a T1 cover needs at least one world of the residual kind (a)–(d), defined on all of Δ with three edges. The only known examples of that kind are the two-world D2 and T2n worlds. No such world is known in a three-world system on all of Δ, even without a target constraint. Defining a T1-adapted version first, and then hoping that Γ contains T1 with a closed selection, would repeat the pattern the audit criticises: fixing a pleasant family, here a design, and fitting it.
