**Yes to the refusal-allocation formulation. But change the plan in one crucial respect: the allocation is already the ranking. There should not be a later stage that “realises” it through a nonlinear score.**

If \(R(r)\) is the set of worlds refusing the ordered record \(r=\operatorname{rec}(X,Y)\), define
\[
X\preceq_i Y\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad i\notin R(r).
\]
Once that rule satisfies the axioms, the construction is complete. Auxiliary weights are tools for discovering or certifying parts of it—not something the final relation must possess.

Also, openness means precisely that, **for each fixed rival \(X\)**,
\[
\{a:i\in R(\operatorname{rec}(C_a,X))\}
\]
is open. It does not require openness as the rival changes.

### 1. Use the adaptive experiment—but preserve its mathematical content

Yes, run it adaptively. However, repeatedly replacing \(q\) after producing a balanced swap could continue indefinitely without revealing anything useful.

At each step, retain:

- the exact records;
- the chosen refusal requirements, with world choices still revisable where necessary;
- any neighbourhoods on which refusal has been promised;
- all applicable defence, guidance, protection, (IB), and (TC) constraints.

When a new swap appears, ask whether the **accumulated allocation requirements** remain jointly feasible. Do not silently discard earlier obligations.

Distinguish three outcomes:

- **The weight problem fails:** a limitation of common reweighting.
- **The relational constraints fail:** a possible axiom-level obstruction, subject to checking every clause.
- **Weights fail but the relational constraints survive:** the most interesting positive outcome. Extract the surviving allocation.

Increasing concentration is diagnostic only. It does not by itself establish that a limiting ranking exists or that no ranking exists.

### 2. Do not make world-dependent weights a major branch

A small labelled test is fine. But common \(q\) has a clear role: it provides known-valid relations satisfying the non-section requirements. World-dependent \(q_i\) removes that guarantee and risks starting another long field-design investigation.

The main escape should be **dropping the need for one weighting to certify the whole allocation**, not adding parameters to the weighting scheme.

### 3. Choose the continuum milestone, with two corrections

Choose **(a)**, not finite families alone.

But do not require “uniform strict bounds” as a property of the final ordinal rule. Those bounds are useful certificates where the reweighting method supplies them. The actual requirement is an open refusal neighbourhood for each refused comparison. Its size may depend on the rival and world.

More importantly, an allocation only on records whose old side is selected is insufficient. Include:

- their reverse records;
- the records appearing in relevant mixed triangles;
- all common-context copies.

Otherwise (TC) and protection remain unchecked precisely where previous constructions failed.

For a partial milestone, distinguish **accepted**, **refused**, and **unassigned**. An unassigned comparison is not yet an incomparability: incomparability requires both directions to be refused.

### 4. Include overlapping swaps and their forced interactions

Start with swaps, but not an isolated collection of swaps.

Include the applicable PP30 consequences and the splice patterns needed to test interactions between those swaps. In particular, include triangles in which:

- one comparison concerns one swap;
- another concerns a different swap;
- the third is guidance or a comparison with another selected source.

These interactions are the substantive test. Adding many unrelated rivals would mostly enlarge the computation.

Use the hypotheses ledger: a derived constraint should enter only with its applicability established.

### 5. Construct the ordinal allocation this round

Do **not** defer the genuinely non-additive rule to round 55.

The constructor should work from the beginning on the record-based allocation itself. The weight solver supplies candidate refusals and certificates; the constructor decides whether those refusals can form one coherent rule.

A useful division of work is:

- **Solver:** supplies compatible refusal patterns and exact failures of common weighting.
- **Constructor:** formulates one rule covering a parameterised family of records, independently of the solver’s chosen weights.
- **Adversary:** checks mixed triangles, inverse records, and fixed-rival limits.
- **Referee:** checks the imported results and any claimed continuum extension.

### The result I would prioritise

Aim for this specific outcome:

> One explicit record-based allocation handles a compact, genuinely two-dimensional family of sources and overlapping measurable swap rivals; its refusals are open in the source, its defence and protection obligations hold, and its relevant common-context copies satisfy (TC)—even though no common \(q\) certifies the prescribed refusals.

That would be meaningful progress beyond the reweighted examples. It would still be a module, not full coverage, and the write-up should state exactly which records remain unassigned.

**The central strategic correction is to stop treating the ordinal rule as something to be built after the allocation. The allocation is the object we are trying to construct.** The adaptive game should help discover its structure; it should not become another sequence of increasingly elaborate scores.
