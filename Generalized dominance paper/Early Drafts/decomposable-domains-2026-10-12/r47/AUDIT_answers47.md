The caveat about optional comparisons becoming forced is correct. But I would **not adopt the proposed defaults about the geometry, or make a general sweep theorem the main task**. Here are my answers.

1. **Near T: leave the geometry open.**

   I am not proposing either continuous motion of credal boundaries or a plateau followed by jumps. Nor do I have a reason to prefer boundaries “leaving through the cone’s edges.”

   The construction should determine which geometry is compatible with the comparisons. Closed sections impose this exact boundary requirement:
   \[
   p_n\to p,\qquad C_{p_n}\preceq_i X\ \text{for all }n
   \quad\Longrightarrow\quad C_p\preceq_i X,
   \]
   for each **fixed** \(X\).

   A plateau does not automatically solve this. For example, if models constantly equal to \(C\) approach its boundary, reflexivity forces the boundary model to be no worse than \(C\) at every world. If \(C\) is undominated, they must tie at every world. Thus a jump between unrelated constant selections is not freely available.

   **Do not build in either continuity in measure or permission to jump arbitrarily.**

2. **A sweep theorem would not, by itself, establish selected realisability of the padding pattern.**

   The inference
   > every point is crossed by some selected boundary, therefore every two-set pattern is realised by one selected model

   is invalid. The same model must realise both parts simultaneously, almost everywhere on the specified sets.

   There is a concrete geometric counterexample to that inference. Take \(\varepsilon=3/10\) and the continuous path
   \[
   K_t=(1-t)M+t\{e_1\},\qquad 0\le t\le1.
   \]
   Consider
   \[
   g=(1,-3/2,-1/2),\qquad h=(1,-1/2,-3/2).
   \]
   T is silent on both. Along this path, both become accepted simultaneously. Every \(K_t\) is invariant under swapping worlds 2 and 3, so no member accepts near \(g\) while remaining silent on the corresponding swapped neighbourhood near \(h\).

   Yet that joint pattern is coherently realisable: the convex hull of \(e_1\) and \((0.55,0,0.45)\) accepts \(g\) and is silent on \(h\), with strict margins that persist on small neighbourhoods.

   This is **not an (S)-construction**, and does not settle the particular padding configuration. It shows why pointwise sweeping is insufficient.

   The useful adversarial theorem would be stronger and specific:

   > Every admissible selection through T realises both of the relevant padding records, at indices whose supports force the conflicting verdicts.

   Investigate that alongside the construction. Do not spend the round proving a weaker sweep statement that would leave this gap untouched.

3. **For the first test, your conditions are useful but need adjustment.**

   We need not keep **both** padding records unrealised or refused. What matters is preventing the offending pair from being accepted at the **same world**, while satisfying all genuine defence obligations.

   Also, check **all relevant decompositions** of the proposed repair’s reverse—not merely the particular split used in Proposition 4.7.

   A partial family is acceptable as a construction step, provided:

   - its index domain and selected values are explicit;
   - every defence obligation generated by those values is included;
   - newly introduced coherent defenders are registered and checked;
   - closure cannot force the offending comparisons;
   - the section conditions being verified are stated precisely.

   Relative closedness on a partial index domain is not a proof of extendability to the simplex. A family consisting only of T and precise models would demonstrate that certain padding obligations are absent, but **would not itself constitute significant progress**. The desired result is a mechanism that continues to work as the selected family grows.

4. **Yes: initially, T should dominate its own inverted rivals.**

   For an inverted departure \(d\) supported where T is silent, the intended comparison is
   \[
   T\preceq_i T\!\cdot d\qquad\text{at every world}.
   \]

   This is the simplest meaningful test of the proposed mechanism. There is no axiom limiting how many rivals T may dominate, and these comparisons do not themselves enlarge T’s index fibre.

   Their compatibility with other comparisons and closed sections still needs proof. A different coherent dominator should remain available if required, but there is no reason to introduce one merely to avoid “overloading” T.

5. **Precise boundary models are an option, not a requirement.**

   The vertices are forced to be the ideal models. The rest of the boundary is not forced to be precise.

   Precise edge models provide a useful benchmark because we already know how to obtain a valid additive edge system. They also avoid some padding patterns involving silence on positive-measure sets. But prescribing the entire boundary—or a neighbourhood of it—as precise could introduce another unnecessary restriction.

   **Treat precise boundary selections as one candidate to test, not part of the brief’s assumptions.** And do not preserve every comparison of the additive benchmark automatically.

6. **Additive structure is allowed wherever it works.**

   I cannot cite a verified proof, under bare (S), of the specific claim that **every** integrable endorsing field must fail **near T**. R46.1 establishes neither that claim nor general non-additivity on the edges.

   We must distinguish:

   - impossibility of an entirely additive covering system;
   - impossibility of additive endorsement of certain comparisons;
   - a theorem locating that failure near T.

   These are different statements. Earlier global impossibility results should be checked under their exact hypotheses before being used here.

   Accordingly, **yes: use additive comparisons locally near T if they help**. What we must avoid is accepting an entire additive order irrevocably and then discovering that its compulsory comparisons prevent the necessary repairs.

7. **Unselected coherent models may be dominated—but not through an inverted-departure record.**

   Yes, a coherent model may be dominated under this particular ranking. Coverage asks that it be vindicated under *some* ranking, not under every ranking.

   Also, “unselected” need not mean “dominated”: an undominated model may instead be tied at all worlds with a selected model. Full (S), through KKM, supplies the relevant weak domination.

   However, the proposed use of inverted departures here is impossible. Their new advice contains a pattern that **no coherent model realises**. Therefore their worse endpoint is necessarily incoherent. They cannot directly dominate an unselected coherent model. Other comparisons would be needed for that.

**For round 47, I recommend one central objective:** determine whether the incoherence-certificate mechanism survives **selected-family, support-sensitive defence obligations**, with the family and verdicts chosen jointly.

Use the repaired padding configuration as the adversary. Do not require universal coherent realisability, do not freeze a geometric transition near T, and do not assume that omitting an optional record makes it permanently unavailable. The result we want is a repeatable co-design mechanism—or a proof that the supposedly optional padding obligations become unavoidable in every admissible selection. That would address the substantive gap.
