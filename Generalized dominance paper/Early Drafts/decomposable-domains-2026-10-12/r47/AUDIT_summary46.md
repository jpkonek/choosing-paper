**Round 46 contains useful results, but I would adjust the proposed strategy substantially.** The most promising opportunity is not another edge-first assembly. It is to remove an unnecessarily strong requirement from the new construction: **defending every coherent model under the same ranking.**

My previous local-defence test was necessary but insufficient. The collaborator is right that passing it alone does not discriminate between promising and hopeless constructions.

**First, the new edge theorem is being overinterpreted.**

R46.1 proves that some edge-selected comparison must disagree with **the particular balanced score Ω**. It does **not** prove that non-additive comparisons must occur on every edge.

There is a simple additive countercheck: score advice at world \(i\) by integrating
\[
|g_i|\times\text{its guidance rank},
\]
and select the precise model \(D_{\{p\}}\) at index \(p\). Unique weighted minimisation gives competitiveness and undominatedness; dominated convergence gives closed sections. This system satisfies our relevant axioms and has edge comparisons that disagree with Ω.

It does not cover T1. But it establishes that **“the edges are where non-additivity must be built” does not follow from R46.1**. We should not redirect the programme on that inference.

**Second, PP23 contains a potentially important co-design opportunity.**

Its covering layer must defend a record whenever its starting advice is realisable by **any coherent model**. That is stronger than we need.

For a ranking designed around T, the required defence obligations come from its **selected models**. Other coherent models need not be competitive under that ranking.

This distinction matters in the padding obstruction. Proposition 4.7 constructs two records whose starting advice is realised by auxiliary coherent models. Universal coverage then forces both comparisons, producing the contradiction. In a co-designed system, the missing step is:

> Why must those auxiliary advice patterns be realised by selected models?

There is a concrete example of the gap. One padding record starts with an answer where T is silent, and silence on another positive-measure set. **Neither T nor any precise model realises that starting pattern.** Thus even a candidate collection containing T and every precise model need not incur this particular obligation.

That is not a covering construction. But it identifies genuine freedom in choosing the family—not merely changing a numerical threshold.

**I would make the next major effort a selected-family version of the incoherence-certificate construction.**

The attractive part of inverted departures is that their resulting advice cannot be coherently realised. Lemma 4.4 uses that fact to prevent certain dangerous compositions, without reserving a permanently protected region. That is a substantive mechanism worth developing.

Co-design these three things together:

1. **Which coherent models are selected**, with T included and the required vertex behaviour.
2. **Which worlds defend their disagreement records**, respecting the support of each selection index.
3. **Which coherent models dominate incoherent rivals**, using incompatibility of advice patterns to control compositions.

The exact defence requirement is simple. If a record can start from \(C_p\), at least one accepting world must lie in \(\operatorname{supp}(p)\). If it can start only from an unselected coherent model, that fact alone imposes no defence obligation.

**The first adversarial test should be the repaired padding configuration—including its steep lines and measurable mass—not another simplified cell model.** Attempt the B-heavy repair excluded by universal coverage, while jointly choosing the selected family and its required comparisons. Either:

- construct it and show that the omitted padding comparisons are not forced back by other selected models, independence, (TC), or closed sections; or
- prove that every permissible selected family must realise the offending patterns.

Either outcome would address the actual co-design problem.

I would retain Conjecture 4.9 as a secondary problem. Solving its stronger universal-coverage version is neither a necessary gateway nor sufficient for (S). Likewise, further exclusions of fixed priorities should not take the lead.

**We still lack a breakthrough construction.** But this is a more consequential direction than adding new gates: retain the geometric protection supplied by incoherence certificates, while allowing the selected family itself to determine—and limit—the comparisons that must be supplied.
