**Proceed with the two-direction construction, but change several defaults before writing the brief.** In particular, do not turn the candidate geometry into another fixed architecture, and do not forbid additive or lexicographic proof devices merely because earlier implementations failed.

There are three corrections to make first.

- **Keeping the present N means keeping \(T\preceq_3N\) as guidance.** Its status is determined by their advice, not by how we label the comparison. To obtain a non-guidance comparison, N itself must change. What should be dropped is the requirement that *every future repair* be guidance-worse at its designated world.
- **The proposed paths do not currently join:** \(R_1=N\), but \(F_0=L_0\ne N\).
- **Retreats and restorations are the same splice family.** For \(L=\{T\ne N\}\),
  \[
  N[H\mapsto T]=T[L\setminus H\mapsto N].
  \]
  Handle arbitrary measurable subsets of \(L\) once.

Also, the blanket claim that every candidate repair is mixed at every world needs checking. The geometry establishes particular gains and losses; it does not automatically establish both at every world for every measurable rival.

### 1. Indices and sections

**Require actual closed fixed-rival sections along a non-isolated family.** Otherwise we repeat round 56’s isolated-index success.

But let the constructor choose the index placement jointly with the relation. Fixed continuous paths are a useful candidate, not a requirement of the axioms.

Here is an explicit repair of the missing geometric bridge. Write
\[
K_N=\operatorname{conv}(M\cup M_0),\qquad
K_{L_0}=\operatorname{conv}(v_3,M_0).
\]
Since \(K_{L_0}\subseteq K_N\), use
\[
J_\beta=D_{(1-\beta)K_N+\beta K_{L_0}},
\qquad 0\le\beta\le1.
\]
This joins N continuously to \(L_0\). Between them, advice changes only where their advice differs.

Thus the candidate curve is
\[
T\xrightarrow{R_\alpha}N
\xrightarrow{J_\beta}L_0
\xrightarrow{F_t}D^*.
\]
It can be indexed continuously along the segment from \(u\) to the edge midpoint. **This supplies geometry only; no defence or domination follows from it.**

For the intended milestone, every registered model should be defended against **every measurable rival**, and its fixed-rival sections should be closed along the assigned path. Domination can remain restricted to the specified repair families.

### 2. Deliverable relation

Choose **one joint system of repair choices and verdicts**. Do not require a certificate supporting *every possible choice* from the parameter windows. That would replace useful flexibility with a much stronger obligation.

A total certificate relation is welcome, but optional. A partial commitment system is also acceptable if it proves simultaneous consistency and the required sections—not merely consistency of each finite generation separately.

Do not prescribe that both worlds 1 and 2 must be non-additive. Nor should all lexicographic keys be prohibited. Instead:

> Any proposed mechanism must meet the stated obligations, and every imported exclusion must be checked against its exact hypotheses.

A certificate without closed sections may establish consistency of commitments. It cannot establish the required sections of the constructed system.

### 3. What may be revised?

**Everything optional from round 56 may be revised.** Preserve T and the original axioms. Treat N, its index, D*’s particular edge placement, the mutual-defence commitments and the reservation choices as construction choices.

The existing module is a successful example, not an irreversible starting state.

In particular, use rival-dependent reservation neighbourhoods. Do not carry forward the fixed-ball policy that forces tie plateaus unless those plateaus are deliberately wanted.

### 4. Scope of rivals

Your proposed scope is right, with the splice identity above removing duplication:

- N’s fills in all three classes;
- all measurable T/N splices;
- the cross-class and common-context copies generated by the installed comparisons.

The explicit \(F_t\) window addresses class-3 fills. It does not automatically handle the other classes. Those cases may need different coherent candidates rather than forced use of the same curve.

### 5. Recursion

**Do not make an entire infinite recursion the prerequisite for reporting success. But require one genuinely repeatable step.**

A useful theorem would say:

> For every source in a specified non-isolated family and every fill in the stated measurable class, there is an admissible repair in the family, and all those choices coexist in one relation.

That is stronger than “generation two works” and weaker than a full covering theorem.

If the proposed family includes the edge endpoint, its sections must be checked there. If it does not, label the result as an interior construction and leave endpoint extension explicitly open.

Also, successive parameters increasing does **not** by itself imply that they converge to \(1\). That requires an additional argument.

### 6. Four-model alternative

Keep it as a genuinely separate alternative, not a small variation of the descending chain.

Its question should be: can repairs return to previously used coherent models through non-guidance comparisons, while handling entire measurable rival families? A four-cycle among the selected models alone would not be informative enough.

There is no need to spend substantial effort on it if the two-direction construction produces a repeatable operation. But it is worth retaining precisely because it abandons permanent guidance-worsening.

### 7. Success criterion

Your criterion is good after two changes:

1. Require a **joint choice** of parameters and relation, not a relation supporting every candidate parameter.
2. Call it a **successful extension milestone**, not full coverage.

The meaningful partial result would be the same simultaneous theorem for fills alone or splices alone, with a non-isolated source family, all measurable rivals in that class, and genuine section proofs.

A finite model collection, isolated indices, or separate relations for separate rivals would fall short.

### 8. Does upward sharing still exclude the edge?

**Yes, under its stated sharing hypothesis.**

Fix one earlier non-null fill \(X_{E_m}\). If every sufficiently late dominator also dominates that *same* rival, and their indices approach the edge, closed sections force the edge-selected model to dominate it too. The edge obstruction rules that out.

This is independent of the reversed-comparison error. It applies to a **fixed rival repeatedly served by later dominators**. It does not automatically apply when each later model repairs a different fill of a different source.

The central instruction for the brief should be:

> **Use the parameter windows to choose repairs jointly with the relation. Keep the geometry revisable. Prove coexistence across a continuum of sources and rivals, rather than selecting a convenient path first and asking the ranking to accommodate it afterwards.**
