**This is genuine progress over round 55: there is now an explicit construction handling a continuum of fills simultaneously. But the proposed continuous-chain strategy is not yet justified, and I found a consequential error in the argument about its endpoint.**

I read PP67 and PP68. I have not rerun their scripts or independently verified the computer-assisted parts.

### What improved—and what remains untouched

The positive result is substantive: one coherent model can dominate every fill in the specified family, while the registered models’ defences and protection remain jointly consistent. That is considerably stronger than proving that a suitable dominator *would have to exist*.

However, the module places its models at finitely many isolated indices. Its treatment of accumulation works by keeping those models separated and reserving fixed neighbourhoods. **It does not yet show how distinct nearby selected models can coexist.** PP67 explicitly leaves future-carrier cascades and some unresolved clauses open.

So this is a useful construction, not yet a recursive mechanism.

### Two corrections before round 57

**First, PP68’s Proposition 4.7(b) reverses a comparison.**

The restriction established earlier is
\[
\neg[N\preceq_1D]\ \lor\ \neg[N\preceq_2D],
\]
where \(D\) is selected on the edge.

But the endpoint argument uses
\[
\neg[D\preceq_1N]\ \lor\ \neg[D\preceq_2N].
\]
These are different statements. Indeed, PP67 explicitly accepts **both**
\[
D^*\preceq_1N,\qquad D^*\preceq_2N.
\]

Therefore, the claimed conclusion that the chain’s defending world must eventually become constant **does not follow from the supplied argument**. Do not impose that restriction on the constructor without a separate proof.

**Second, the displayed chain is a geometric chain, not a demonstrated chain of dominators.** Its models approach \(D^*\), and their advice changes in the stated direction. That does not establish the comparisons needed to dominate their predecessors’ fills.

### An exact test I worked out

There is a simple obstruction to using a *fixed* later member as a uniform successor.

For the proposed family,
\[
C_t=D_{\operatorname{conv}(v_t,M_0)},\qquad
v_t=(1-t)(7,7,16)/30+t(1/2,1/2,0),
\]
consider \(g=(1,1,-r)\). Its expectation at either endpoint of \(M_0\) is \(1\), while
\[
v_t\cdot g=1-b_t(1+r),
\qquad b_t=(1-t)16/30.
\]

For every \(s<t<1\), there is an open region where \(C_s\) is silent and \(C_t\) gives the majority answer. Choose a bounded non-null piece \(E\) there, and put
\[
X=C_s[E\mapsto\text{majority answer}].
\]

**Then \(C_t\) cannot dominate \(X\).** If it did, (IB) would imply that
\[
Z=C_t[E\mapsto\text{silence}]
\]
is no worse than \(C_s\) at every world. Protection of \(C_s\), followed by (C), would force \(Z\) to be coherent. But \(Z\) is a bounded modification of a cone and can be chosen nonconic—a contradiction.

For example, \(s=0,t=1/2,r=1\) gives the exact expectations \(-2/30\) and \(14/30\).

This **does not exclude the path**. It means the later parameter must depend on the fill. Staying silent on part of that fill avoids this particular obstruction, but is not itself a proof of domination.

### The strategic restriction to remove

PP67’s **“guidance closure” is optional**, despite the passage describing it as derived.

The axioms force
\[
T\preceq_3N.
\]
They do not force \(N\) to be pointwise worse than \(T\) at world 3.

Imposing that stronger requirement at every generation creates the strictly descending chain. The endless-chain problem is therefore partly a consequence of the chosen construction. Moreover, PP68 already shows that the module forces non-guidance defences elsewhere: avoiding them cannot be the general organising principle.

### My recommendation

**Proceed, but make the main task a second-generation extension without prescribing its geometry.** Require it to handle:

- every measurable fill of \(N\);
- the unresolved \(T/N\) splices;
- the cross-class obligations generated by those defences;
- their common-context copies and fixed-rival section limits.

Allow the new defenders, their indices and their reservation neighbourhoods to vary. Do not require guidance-worsening, fixed reservation balls, or membership in the displayed chain.

The continuous chain can remain **one candidate**, with a precise deliverable: an explicit choice \(t=t(s,E)\), and a single relation supporting all those choices simultaneously. Merely exhibiting the curve or checking another finite generation would not meet it.

A genuinely different alternative is **recurrent repair among four or more coherent models**, with some required defences being non-guidance comparisons. The short-cycle results explain why two or three models may fail; (TC) does not automatically exclude a four-cycle. This remains speculative, but it tests the freedom that permanent guidance-worsening removes.

**The advance worth pursuing now is coexistence of successive repairs—not another isolated dominator.** The uniform first-generation module gives a much better starting point, but should remain revisable where its optional commitments obstruct that coexistence.
