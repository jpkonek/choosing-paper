**Proceed, but correct the first two premises before setting the milestone.** Thin fills have not been shown to force nearby repairs under the general axioms, and a repair’s class does not determine the direction of its index. Those conclusions came from particular constructions.

There is, however, an explicit coherent loop worth testing. We need not leave its geometry entirely to search.

### 1. The first milestone: local, but not artificially constrained

A local construction around T is a reasonable first milestone **provided it contains genuinely varying sources, handles arbitrary measurable fills, and proves fixed-rival sections**.

Do not require every repair of a thin fill to approach its source. PP70’s proximity conclusion concerns in-family repairs on its particular curve; PP69’s convergence result assumes an additive certificate and a unique interior Bayes optimum. Neither is a general axiom-level conclusion. The uniform dominators from round 56 already warn against importing it.

Nor must every repair participate in an arbitrarily small three-step loop. Recurrence is an opportunity, not a new axiom. Permit branches, longer returns and repairs outside the local family if necessary—while accounting for their obligations.

### 2–3. An explicit candidate loop

Use the rescaling operation itself. It composes multiplicatively and therefore supplies exact returns.

For \(a>1\), define three positive diagonal multipliers:
\[
d_3=(a,a,a^{-2}),\qquad
d_1=(a^{-2},a,a),\qquad
d_2=(a,a^{-2},a).
\]
Their componentwise product is \((1,1,1)\).

Starting at the uniform index, set
\[
m_0=u,\qquad
m_1\propto d_3,\qquad
m_2\propto d_1d_3,\qquad
m_3\propto d_2d_1d_3=u.
\]
The corresponding scaled coherent models form an exact loop through T. As \(a\downarrow1\), all three approach T.

This geometry also fits the required source-to-defender comparisons:

- Moving from \(m_0\) to \(m_1\) decreases world 3’s likelihood relative to the others.
- The next move decreases world 1’s relative likelihood.
- The last decreases world 2’s.

Each move is therefore pointwise guidance-worsening at its designated world. The required comparisons
\[
C_{m_0}\preceq_3 C_{m_1},\qquad
C_{m_1}\preceq_1 C_{m_2},\qquad
C_{m_2}\preceq_2 C_{m_0}
\]
hold by guidance.

**This is an explicit coherent geometry, not a proof of fill domination.** The necessary refusals at the other worlds, the domination records, and their coexistence remain to be established.

There are two important freedoms to retain:

- A fixed move cannot repair every fill: the agreement obstruction still applies. The scale must be chosen with the rival.
- Fixing the first scale and demanding return after exactly two further repairs may fail when later rivals require different scales. Permit longer loops or revisable candidate choices before commitment.

This construction does not imply that a two-dimensional sheet is necessary. It provides a candidate family of loops. Their index placement remains a construction choice.

### 4. Defence: do not make score gluing the default

**The principal deliverable should be a joint record-based relation.** Scores may help construct or certify pieces, but “try scores first and glue them” risks repeating the earlier programme.

In particular, the toy vectors being tied at equal weights is not a defence theorem for the measurable models. They only demonstrate consistency of the required comparisons *among the sources*.

If scores are used, distinguish these roles:

- **A score witnessing particular comparisons:** permissible.
- **A score supplying every favourable comparison as an acceptance:** a substantial commitment, subject to the no-patching result.
- **Different scores for different disagreements:** permissible as a candidate, but (IB), (TC), protection and sections must be proved for the resulting rule.

Closing each fixed-rival section does not, by itself, make score gluing safe: mixed triangles can use comparisons from different pieces.

I would ask the constructor to exploit the loop’s already justified guidance comparisons and choose the additional domination and defence comparisons jointly. Keep defence obligations disjunctive; do not assign a defending world merely because one available score favours it.

### 5. Splices

Include **all measurable splices between the models actually installed**, particularly consecutive loop members, together with their common-context copies.

Include the T/\(N_k\) splices only if those \(N_k\) are retained. Do not register extra coherent models solely to enlarge the test; registration creates genuine obligations.

The previously discovered splice counterexamples should be checked immediately whenever their hypotheses apply. But do not demand that the original loop members themselves dominate every splice. New coherent defenders must remain available.

### 6. Universal defence is required

**Yes: every installed loop member must be defended against every measurable rival, jointly.** Restricting rivals to the geometric neighbourhood would evade the main problem.

For the first milestone, domination may be limited to the specified fill and splice families. Universal defence and protection of the installed coherent models should not be limited that way.

For sections, distinguish:

- source indices confined to the constructed local family;
- rivals ranging over the full measurable domain.

Prove sections for every fixed rival on that source family, including its accumulation points. This is a local extension result, not full (S) on the simplex.

### 7. Refereeing

Yes. Rescope the statements before they enter the construction:

- proximity of thin-fill repairs: retain its architectural hypotheses;
- infinite chains: retain the same-class and continuing-silence conditions;
- off-curve accumulation: do not call it a two-dimensional sheet;
- score-patching exclusions: distinguish preserving an entire score order from using scores as witnesses.

### The decisive task for this round

I would replace “search for loop moves” with:

> **Start with the exact rescaling loop above. Choose its scale and the fill-domination records jointly. Establish one relation supporting a non-isolated family of such repairs, with universal defence, protection, all measurable copies, and closed fixed-rival sections. Permit additional branches and coherent defenders when the loop alone cannot handle a splice.**

The adversary should first attack the combination of **two adjacent repair edges and one splice**, then a fixed rival approached along two branches. These are where geometry turns into a relational construction.

The promising new ingredient is not simply a triangular picture. It is **an exact, reversible coherent transformation whose three moves have different designated worlds**. That supplies the geometry without imposing an endless one-directional descent. The remaining work is to make the repair verdicts coexist—and that is where the round should concentrate.
