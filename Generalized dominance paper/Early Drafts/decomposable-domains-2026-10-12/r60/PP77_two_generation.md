**Plain English.**
- The essential mixed refusal (PP79 Theorem M) forces more than PP79 noticed. B must accept at world 2 a whole interval of rivals. Near the bottom of that interval the rivals are u¹-better than B at world 2, and at world 3 as well.
- So a second generation that keeps the essential refusal must contain acceptances that u¹ does not endorse. That holds for every same-ray defender, and for every pool defender that an endorsed sectioned base on 𝔾 could otherwise admit. One of these is excluded only under PP78's base.
- Endorsement survives only for splices that leave a whole outer shell switched. For the world-2 version of that family (Theorem 5.9), everything is proved, with PP78's base unchanged. For the world-1 version, two bookkeeping lemmas remain (E1, E2).
- The next round should therefore either design a non-endorsed world-2 class with its own (TC) potential, or prove the exact-tie lemma (§8).

## 2. Admissible same-ray scales for every radially non-conic splice

Rays are constant in odd world and in type, so each mixed ray of S lies in one sublayer L⁽ᵒ⁾. Let 𝔐_o be the set of mixed rays in L⁽ᵒ⁾. Put J_o := (ess inf λ, ess sup λ) over 𝔐_o (empty if 𝔐_o is null), and J(S) := J₁ ∪ J₂ ∪ J₃.

**Theorem 2.1 (PROVED).** Let S ⊆ L be radially non-conic. Then J(S) is a non-empty open set, and for every τ ∈ J(S):
- (a) P⁺ and P⁻ are non-null. If τ ∈ J_o, both meet L⁽ᵒ⁾ in positive measure.
- (b) d₂ satisfies (N1): B_τ is strictly better than X₂ somewhere, at every world.
- (c) (U1) holds against T and against every ray-3 member. It holds against B_{k,s} (k ∈ {1, 2}, n the third world) unless P⁺ ⊆ L⁽ⁿ⁾ ∩ L_k(s) and P⁻ ∩ L_k(s) = ∅. In particular (U1) holds against all of 𝔾 whenever P⁺ meets two sublayers or meets L⁽³⁾, and hence on all of 𝒮_a ∪ 𝒮_b.
- (d) Put s_o := mass(S ∩ L⁽ᵒ⁾). Then V(d₂) = Σ_o c_o(e − 3e_o) with c = (s₁ − β(τ), s₂ − β(τ), α(τ) − s₃). Moreover d₂ is null for at most one τ.

*Proof.*
- (a) Layers are cones, so a ray lies in L(τ) iff its level is below τ. The level distribution of 𝔐_o has no atoms, because level sets lie in finitely many planes. For τ ∈ J_o, a positive measure of mixed rays lies on each side of τ.
  - A mixed ray below τ lies in L(τ), and its part outside S lies in P⁻ ∩ L⁽ᵒ⁾.
  - A mixed ray above τ has its part in S inside P⁺ ∩ L⁽ᵒ⁾.
  - Some 𝔐_o is non-null because S is radially non-conic.
- (b) World 3: on P⁺, B_τ = T is 3-better than X₂ = B. Worlds 1 and 2: with o such that τ ∈ J_o, use (a) and the type table of §1:
  - o = 3: B is 1- and 2-better than T, on P⁻ ∩ L⁽³⁾;
  - o = 1: P⁻ ∩ L⁽¹⁾ gives world 1, and P⁺ ∩ L⁽¹⁾ gives world 2;
  - o = 2: P⁺ ∩ L⁽²⁾ gives world 1, and P⁻ ∩ L⁽²⁾ gives world 2.
- (c) Z ∈ 𝔾 carries d₂⁻¹ iff Z = X₂ on D, i.e. iff P⁺ ⊆ K_Z and P⁻ ∩ K_Z = ∅.
  - Z = T: impossible, since K_T = ∅.
  - Z = B_{3,s}: P⁺ has levels above τ, so P⁺ ⊆ L(s) forces s > τ. Then K_Z ⊇ L(τ) ⊇ P⁻ ≠ ∅.
  - Rays 1, 2: K_Z ⊆ L⁽ⁿ⁾ ∩ L_k(s).
- (d) Relative to B_τ, X₂ flips S∖L(τ) from T to B and flips L(τ)∖S back. The type table gives c_o = (π⁺_o − π⁻_o) for o = 1, 2 and c₃ = π⁻₃ − π⁺₃, with π^±_o := mass(P^± ∩ L⁽ᵒ⁾). Here π⁺_o − π⁻_o = s_o − mass(L⁽ᵒ⁾(τ)). Nullity needs c₁ = c₃, i.e. p(τ) = s₁ + s₃, and p is strictly increasing. ∎

*Remarks.*
- Theorem 2.1 covers every radially non-conic splice. Conic but incoherent splices (O7) have no mixed rays. The family of §5 contains conic ones, and its admissibility proof does not use mixed rays.
- V(d₂) depends on S only through (s₁, s₂, s₃). Its unfavourable worlds are the w with c_w > mean(c).

## 3. The exact refusal map of a same-ray second defender

For C ∈ 𝔾 put:
- U_C := D ∩ {C = B_τ} = (P⁺∖K_C) ∪ (P⁻ ∩ K_C), the correction set, non-null by (U1);
- M_C := D ∩ {C = X₂} = (P⁺ ∩ K_C) ∪ (P⁻∖K_C).

**Theorem 3.1 (PROVED; CHECKED, `refmap77.out`: 3,888 carrier–world–splice–scale cases, 0 mismatches against PP76 Thm 1.1).** Let τ satisfy (N1) and (U1), let C ∈ 𝔾 and w ∈ {1, 2, 3}.
- (i) d₂ forces C to refuse some rival at w (first generation, all copies) **iff U_C ⊆ Fav_w**.
- (ii) The refused rivals are exactly Z = C[G₁ ↦ B_τ][G₂ ↦ n], where:
  - M_C∖Fav_w ⊆ G₁ ⊆ M_C;
  - G₂ ∩ D = ∅;
  - n is strictly w-better than C on G₂.

  Every refused rival is strictly w-better than C on M_C∖Fav_w, and that set is non-null.
- (iii) A refused rival that is strictly w-worse than C somewhere (a **mixed** rival) exists **iff (i) holds and M_C ∩ Fav_w is non-null**.

*Proof.* Apply PP76 Thm 1.1 to r = d₂ at carrier C. On D the values are two, and C ∈ {B_τ, X₂}.
- Condition (a) asks G₁ ⊆ D ∩ {C ≠ B_τ} = M_C, with C weakly w-worse than X₂ there. That is automatic, since C = X₂ on M_C.
- Condition (b) asks B_τ to be weakly, hence strictly, w-worse than X₂ on D∖G₁ ⊇ U_C. That gives (i) and the lower bound on G₁.
- (d) holds because U_C is non-null.
- If G₁ ⊆ Fav_w, then D ⊆ Fav_w, contradicting (N1) at w. So M_C∖Fav_w is non-null, and there Z = B_τ is strictly better than C = X₂.
- Z is w-worse than C exactly on G₁ ∩ Fav_w, which can be chosen non-null iff M_C ∩ Fav_w is non-null. ∎

Write A^± := {o : P^± ∩ L⁽ᵒ⁾ non-null}.

**Corollary 3.2 (PROVED; CHECKED, `refmap77.out` tallies).**
- (a) **World 3.** Ray-3 carriers are affected iff P⁺ ⊆ L(s), and they are never mixed, since then s > τ and M_C ∩ Fav₃ = P⁻∖L(s) = ∅. T is never affected. A ray-k member (k ≤ 2) is affected only if P⁺ ⊆ K_C ⊆ L⁽ⁿ⁾.
- (b) **T.** T is affected at 1 iff 2 ∉ A⁺, and then mixed iff 2 ∈ A⁻. T is affected at 2 iff 1 ∉ A⁺, and then mixed iff 1 ∈ A⁻. So **T has an unavoidable mixed refusal whenever S misses L⁽¹⁾ or L⁽²⁾**, for every same-ray τ (families `no1`, `no2`).
- (c) **Ray-3 carrier B_s, world 1.**
  - s ≥ τ: affected iff A⁻ = {2} and P⁺ ∩ L⁽²⁾ ⊆ L(s); mixed iff P⁺ ∩ L(s) ∩ L⁽¹³⁾ ≠ ∅.
  - s < τ: affected iff 2 ∉ A⁺ and P⁻ ∩ L(s) ⊆ L⁽²⁾; mixed iff P⁻ ∩ L⁽²⁾ ⊄ L(s).

  World 2 is the mirror image.
- (d) **On 𝒮_a** (τ ∈ J(S), so A⁺ = {1, 2, 3} and A⁻ = {2}), the mixed forced refusals are exactly the following, all at **world 1**:
  - every B_{3,s} with s ≥ τ_F, B included (PP79's essential refusal and its section spread);
  - every B_{1,s} with F∖L(τ) ⊆ L₁(s) and G(τ) ⊄ L₁(s), where G(τ) := G ∩ L(τ) (family `cov`).

  Nothing else is mixed. T is unaffected. Ray-2 members are affected at 2 only if Sh₁ ⊆ L₂(s), and then only by guidance. 𝒮_b is the mirror image (world 2; ray 2 in place of ray 1).

*Proof.* Substitute U_C, M_C and Fav_w into Theorem 3.1, using K_C ⊆ L⁽ⁿ⁾ for rays 1 and 2. For instance, in (d) for B_{3,s}, s ≥ τ: U_C = P⁻ ⊆ L⁽²⁾ ⊆ Fav₁ holds, and P⁺ ∖ K_C = F∖L(τ) ∖ L(s) must vanish, i.e. s ≥ τ_F. Mixedness comes from P⁺ ∩ L(s) ∩ L⁽¹³⁾ ⊇ L⁽¹³⁾(s)∖L(τ) ≠ ∅. The other entries are similar one-line checks. ∎

## 4. The essential refusal forces a u¹-non-endorsed acceptance

Throughout this section S ∈ 𝒮_a and τ ∈ J(S). Then P⁺ = (F∖L(τ)) ∪ Sh₁ ∪ Sh₃, P⁻ = G(τ) := G ∩ L(τ), U_B = P⁻ and M_B = P⁺.

### 4.1 The interval of forced world-2 rivals

**Proposition 4.1 (PROVED).** The rivals B is forced to refuse at world 1 are exactly

  Z = B[G₁ ↦ T][G₂ ↦ n],  with F∖L(τ) ⊆ G₁ ⊆ P⁺, G₂ off D, and n strictly 1-better than B on G₂.

Every **pure reversion** in this interval (G₂ = ∅) is strictly 3-better than B, so it is refused at world 3 by strict (G). Hence **B ⪯₂ Z is forced for every pure reversion Z of the interval**, in every relation that satisfies the axioms, contains d₂ and defends B.

Let G₁ = (F∖L(τ)) ∪ Q₁ ∪ Q₃ with Q_o ⊆ Sh_o of mass x_o. Then

  V(B → Z) = f(−1, 2, −1) + x₁(2, −1, −1) + x₃(1, 1, −2),  so V₂ = 2f − x₁ + x₃.

PP79's Prop 1.6 is the top of the interval (x₁ = q₁, x₃ = q₃), where V₂ = 2f − q₁ + q₃ ≥ 0 under (Hq). The bottom-right corner (x₁ = q₁, x₃ = 0) has V₂ = 2f − q₁.

*Proof.* Theorem 3.1 with C = B, K_B = L: M_B∖Fav₁ = P⁺ ∩ L⁽²⁾ = F∖L(τ). On L, T is strictly 3-better than B. The vectors follow from the type table: reverting a (2,+) point worsens world 2, and reverting a (1,+) point worsens world 1. ∎

### 4.2 The forced non-endorsed acceptance

**Theorem 4.2 (PROVED; CHECKED membership and NUMERICS masses, `endorse77.out` [1]).** Let S ∈ 𝒮_a and let τ be any admissible same-ray scale. Recall f = mass(F∖L(τ)) ≤ q₁ = mass(Sh₂) = mass(Sh₁), with equality iff F ⊇ Sh₂.
- (a) If 2f < q₁, then in every relation that satisfies the axioms, contains d₂ at all worlds and defends B, Z⁰ := B[(F∖L(τ)) ∪ Sh₁ ↦ T] must be accepted at world 2, with

  V(B → Z⁰) = (2q₁ − f, 2f − q₁, −q₁ − f),  so V₂ < 0.
- (b) Assume (Y): the type-(A,3) odd-3 points outside L₃(t₁), where T is silent, have mass ≥ β(t₁). If f < q₁, let Y be such a set of mass y with max(0, 2f − q₁) < y < (q₁ + f)/2 (non-empty because f < q₁), and Z¹ := Z⁰[Y ↦ h]. Then B is forced to refuse Z¹ at 1, and

  V(B → Z¹) = (2q₁ − f − y, 2f − q₁ − y, 2y − q₁ − f),  with V₂ < 0 and V₃ < 0.

  So **B must accept Z¹ at world 2 or 3 with a negative u¹-increment**.
- (c) f < q₁ for **every S ∈ 𝒮_M** and every τ, because F ⊆ lose-A₂ misses gain-B₂ ∩ Sh₂, which is non-null since β is strictly increasing.

*Proof.*
- (a) Z⁰ is the pure reversion with G₁ = (F∖L(τ)) ∪ Sh₁. Apply Prop 4.1 with x₁ = q₁, x₃ = 0.
- (b) Membership: in Theorem 3.1(ii), G₁ = (F∖L(τ)) ∪ Sh₁ and G₂ = Y. Y lies off D because D ⊆ L. At a type-(A,3) point, h is correct at world 1, so h is strictly 1-better than S.
  - The c-vector is (−q₁, −f, −y). V₂ = c₁ + c₃ − 2c₂ and V₃ = c₁ + c₂ − 2c₃ give the two inequalities.
  - B must defend Z¹ somewhere (defence), and world 1 is excluded (forced refusal). Hence the conclusion.
  - (Y) supplies y < q₁ ≤ β(t₁).
- (c) As stated. ∎

CHECKED/NUMERICS (`endorse77.out` [1]): exact cells, 𝒮_M splice, two same-ray scales, three radial cut-offs. In all six cases:
- Z⁰ and Z¹ lie in B's world-1 family, and Z⁰ is strictly reverse-guidance at 3.
- The Monte-Carlo vectors (V/q₁) match (a) and (b). Examples: (1.99, −0.98, −1.01) for f/q₁ = 0.01; (0.58, −0.19, −0.39) for f/q₁ = 0.74, with y > 0.
- (Y) holds with mass 0.096 against β(t₁) = 0.015 (`check77.out` [d]).

**What this does to PP79's target.** PP79 §5 proposes moving the Z₃ records to a new world-2 class that is u¹-endorsed. Theorem 4.2 shows that the class must also contain the interval's lower rivals, which are not endorsed at any world other than 1, and world 1 is forced shut. PP79's Prop 1.6 checked only the top of the interval. **So the target is unreachable with an endorsed base whenever f < q₁, in particular on all of 𝒮_M.** Theorem 4.2 uses only (TC), strict (G), (IB) through PP76 Thm 1.1, and defence. The obstruction therefore sits at the level of *u¹-endorsement*, not of the axioms.

  - B's rivals satisfy V₁ − V₂ = 3(x + (q₁ − x₂) + z) ≥ 0 (mirror of Prop 5.1). On {v₁ ≥ v₂}, the cyclic 𝒟 never picks 2: N = {2} and N = {2, 3} need v₁ < v₂, and {1, 2} and {1, 2, 3} go to 1.
  - cl(A₂) ⊆ {v₁ ≤ M − κ|v| ≤ v₂} meets {v₁ ≥ v₂} only where v₁ = v₂ = M − κ|v|. A section limit lands there only from approximants with N ∋ 2 and 1 ∉ N.
  - The rivals revert Sh₁, so they are carried only by B. Near B, the neighbours' records are carried only by ray-3 members, by (H₁′). Along ray 3, V₁ − V₂ is constant. So no approximant has N ∋ 2 with 1 ∉ N.
  - Null rivals are carried by the end B alone, so they are accepted at 3 only (B ∉ the path P₂). So every forced refusal is base-refused, and B's base defence (𝒟, at 1 or 3) survives. The same argument gives openness of the refusal sets around B.
- **(c) 𝒪₃-triangles at 2.** Suppose one link is in 𝒪₃ (null, odd-3 supported). It cannot touch odd-1 or odd-2 points. So the other link moves Sh₁ ∪ Sh₂ from flip to T and moves G′ from T to flip. That link is not 2-guidance: reverting (1,+) points improves 2. Its carrier would be flip on Sh₁ ∪ Sh₂, hence B, and T on G′, which B is not. So it is neither carried nor guidance, and by (J3), (J5) it is not accepted. Both orientations are the same. This is PP78 Lemma 5.2 for d₂.
- **(d) Covers at 2.**
  - One carrier is B, because Sh₃ reaches level t₁.
  - The other, C′, equals T on G′ ⊇ 𝒞′. That excludes ray 2 (PP74 Lemma 4.1: L₂(σ) ∩ L₃⁽¹⁾(τ) ⊆ 𝒞′ is non-null) and ray 3 with s > 0 (H₂′). So C′ ∈ {T} ∪ ray 1.
  - With the notation of Theorem 5.2(c), mirrored:

    c(b) = (−q₁ − a₁ − b₁, −q₁ + k₂ − a₂ + b₂, q₃ + a₃ + b₃),  c(c) = (g′ + a₁ + b₁, a₂ − k₂ − b₂, −a₃ − b₃).

  - Suppose both lie in cl(A₂) ⊆ {v₁ ≤ M − κ|v| ≤ v₂}. Then:
    - for b: V₂(b) − V₁(b) = 3(x − a₁ − b₁) ≥ κ|V₃(b)|, where x := a₂ − k₂ − b₂ ≥ 0 and |V₃(b)| = 2γ + a₁ + b₁ + x + 2a₃ + 2b₃;
    - for c, the case M = V₂(c) contradicts the b inequality, so M = V₃(c), and then 3(x + a₃ + b₃) ≤ κ|V(c)|_∞ ≤ κ(2(g′ + a₁ + b₁ + a₃ + b₃) + x).
  - Eliminating x leaves (2κ/3)γ + (1 − κ/3)(a₁ + b₁) + a₃ + b₃ ≤ (2κ/3)g′, which is impossible because g′ ≤ (1 − θ)γ.
  - The other classes:
    - If c is 2-guidance, then a₂ = a₃ = b₃ = 0, so x ≤ 0 and b ∉ cl(A₂). Also b is not 2-guidance, because it reverts Sh₁.
    - b and c are never null: c₁(c) > 0 ≥ c₃(c) and c₃(b) > 0 > c₁(b).
    - b and c are never in 𝔇 (as in Theorem 5.2(c)).

  So no cover is accepted at 2.
- **(e) Two dominations.** Lemma 6.2.
- **(f) Sections.** 𝔇₂(𝒮_o^b) ∪ {∅} is closed in the record topology, because the holes form a closed set in measure with mass ≥ (1 + θ)γ/2 > 0. 𝔇₁ ∪ {∅} is closed (PP74 Prop 4.5). The base sections are PP78's. A section of 𝓡^b is the union of a base section and the preimage of a closed set under the continuous map p ↦ rec(C_p → Z) (by (CM)). So sections are closed, and every class is a property of the record, which absorbs copies.
- **(g) Defence, protection, ties, (C).**
  - By (a)–(d), no base acceptance is withdrawn.
  - An all-world acceptance into a member is either a domination, which would contradict (U1), or a base one, which PP78 excludes.
  - d₂⁻¹ is refused everywhere: it is not guidance by (N1), not carried by (U1), and not in 𝔇.
  - At any source, a 𝔇₁-forced refusal is strictly reverse-guidance and a 𝔇₂-forced refusal is either B's world-2 refusal or has V < 0. 𝓑's defence world has V_w ≥ (½ − κ)|V| > 0. So no source is jointly defeated. ∎

**What Theorem 5.9 achieves, and what it does not.**
- It is a measurable, jointly sectioned, two-generation construction. It has the first-generation fills, a family of second-generation splice dominations with their own section obligations, and a mixed refusal whose alternative is chosen together with it and survives copying and section closure.
- **It does not handle every incoherent T–B splice**, only 𝒮_o^b.
- **Its mixed refusal is not essential** in PP79's sense: 𝒮_o^b is disjoint from the mirror of 𝒮_M. Theorem 4.2 (mirrored) shows that an essential one forces non-endorsed acceptances.
- B is the end of ray 3. Steps (b) and (d) use this. Interior first defenders, where Prop 2.1(ii) adds outward ray-3 approximants, are not checked.

## 6. Interactions among second defenders

**Proposition 6.1 (joint defeat, sharpened; PROVED; CHECKED, `defeat77.out`).** Let d_a = rec(B_{τ_a} → X₂(S_a)) with S_a ∈ 𝒮_a, and d_b = rec(B_{τ_b} → X₂(S_b)) with S_b ∈ 𝒮_b, both admissible, and τ_a ≤ τ_b. Then B is refused the reversion rival

  Z = B[(F_a∖L(τ_a)) ∪ (F_b∖L(τ_b)) ↦ T]

at world 1 by d_a, at world 2 by d_b and at world 3 by strict (G), **iff F_a ∩ (L(τ_b)∖L(τ_a)) is null.** The case τ_a ≥ τ_b is symmetric, with F_b and the shell L(τ_a)∖L(τ_b). Conversely, if some reversion rival B[Q ↦ T] is refused at 1 by d_a and at 2 by d_b, then F_a misses that shell.

*Proof.* Apply Theorem 3.1 at B to both dominations.
- World 1 (d_a) requires F_a∖L(τ_a) ⊆ Q, Q ∩ G_a(τ_a) = ∅, and Q off D_a to be 1-improving. That means Q ∩ L⁽¹³⁾ ⊆ L∖L(τ_a).
- World 2 (d_b) requires F_b∖L(τ_b) ⊆ Q, Q ∩ G_b(τ_b) = ∅, and Q ∩ L⁽²³⁾ ⊆ L∖L(τ_b).
- Together: F_a∖L(τ_a) ⊆ Q ∩ L⁽²⁾ ⊆ L⁽²⁾∖L(τ_b), i.e. F_a misses L(τ_b)∖L(τ_a). The odd-1 condition is automatic for τ_a ≤ τ_b.
- Strictness comes from U_B ≠ ∅. ∎

*Consequences.*
- PP79's "defeat iff s_a = s_b" is correct for its F (half of every cell), and only for it. With τ_a ≠ τ_b and F_a missing the shell, B is defeated. `defeat77.out` shows this with exact cells: both dominations admissible, defeat witness (world 1 by d_a, world 2 by d_b, world 3 by guidance).
- **Sufficient rule:** disjoint scale sets for 𝒮_a and 𝒮_b, *and* every scale a right-increase point of its F, i.e. F ∩ (L(τ + ε)∖L(τ)) is non-null for all ε > 0. Such points exist in J(S) whenever F's level distribution charges (τ⁻, τ_F) (Theorem 2.1).
- This rule is not closed, because both scale sets must be dense. It is moot in §5, where each family uses one scale and only one of 𝒮_o, 𝒮_o^b is installed.

**Lemma 6.2 (two dominations; PROVED).** Let 𝔇 = 𝔇₁ ∪ 𝔇₂(𝒮_o), or 𝔇₁ ∪ 𝔇₂(𝒮_o^b).
- (i) No two members of 𝔇₂ are consecutive: X₂ = B ≠ T = B_τ on P⁺ ⊆ D ∩ D′.
- (ii) A pair (d₁, d₂) or (d₂, d₁) is consecutive only for d₁ of class 2 (class 1 for 𝒮_o^b). Then D₁ ∩ D₂ ⊆ Sh₁ ∩ L₂(s), respectively Sh₂ ∩ L₁(s). The other classes are excluded on G ∩ L_k(s) or on E∖L_k(s), using 𝒞 ⊆ G, E ⊆ odd-k and PP74 Lemma 4.1.
- (iii) In a consecutive pair, the third link ℓ of the triangle has old side X₂ and new side B_τ on D₂∖D₁ ⊇ Sh₃ ∪ G.
  - So ℓ is strictly better than its old side somewhere at every world: (N1) witnesses for d₂ at worlds 1, 2, 3 lie in Sh₂, G, Sh₃ (respectively G′, Sh₁, Sh₃), all outside D₁. Hence ℓ is not guidance.
  - ℓ is not member-carried, as in the (U1) argument.
  - ℓ is not in 𝔇.
  - ℓ is not null-odd, since it meets odd-3 and odd-2 (respectively odd-1) points.

  So ℓ lies in no acceptance class, and no triangle contains two dominations. ∎
