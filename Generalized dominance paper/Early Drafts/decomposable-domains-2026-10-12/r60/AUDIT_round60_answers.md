**I support the two-generation construction as the main task, with the three-ray question investigated separately. But several defaults still preserve more structure than necessary.** The next round should test whether repairs can coexist after their consequences are installed—not whether another rigid base can accommodate them.

Here are specific answers.

1. **Use two rays to start, but do not require the completed construction to remain a star.**

   There is no reason to postpone the second-generation test until Conjecture G is solved. However, the repaired two-ray theorem applies only to its stated family and relation. Adding new defenders does not automatically extend its section proof.

   The milestone should be:

   > One first-generation repair, followed by repairs of every incoherent measurable splice between its source and defender, in one relation with universal defence and closed sections for all committed sources.

   Include interactions among the second defenders. Do not require all three fill classes yet. **Treating every measurable splice in this configuration is more valuable than treating additional fill classes independently.**

2. **Build the three-ray allocation by records; do not freeze T’s choices first.**

   Your proposed choices at T pass the immediate directional test. They are candidates, not established commitments. A record carried at T may also be carried elsewhere, and those other carriers can impose additional acceptances or refusals.

   For each record, retain the possible sets of accepting worlds and eliminate possibilities as obligations accumulate. Fix a choice only when you can show it preserves the remaining obligations.

   Also, “the three rays collectively force all three worlds” is not itself a contradiction: the forced acceptances may concern different records. The decisive question is which forced verdicts coincide **after copying and section limits**.

3. **Never drop record-intrinsic verdicts. Drop only unnecessarily strong topological closure.**

   This distinction is essential:

   - **(IB) requires:** the verdict depends only on the record.
   - **(S) requires:** acceptance sections are closed when the rival is fixed and the selected source varies.
   - Neither requires acceptance to be closed under every convergent sequence of arbitrary records.

   Your replacement is appropriate if it means the **least closure under the actual rules**: common-context copying, required section limits, and (TC), iterated together. One pass is insufficient.

   “Compute the closure and check that nothing dangerous enters” is a verification specification, however, not yet an invariant. The proof must identify a property that survives every application of those rules.

4. **Second defenders may be any coherent models. Yes, give them genuine section obligations.**

   Rescalings and vertex-wise rescalings are useful candidate generators, not restrictions. Allow other credal sets when they satisfy the advice requirements better.

   Every committed defender needs an explicit place in the partial selection, with sections checked in the resulting index topology. Do not give it an isolated index merely to make its section obligations vacuous. Conversely, do not assume it must have its likelihood as its index or lie in a two-dimensional posterior neighbourhood.

5. **The combined invariant must allow withdrawal of optional base acceptances.**

   I would **not** prescribe “endorsed base plus domination class, retaining every base acceptance” as the general form. That is precisely the restriction most likely to obstruct a necessary mixed refusal.

   Use the endorsed base as an initial source of possible defences. Preserve guidance and whichever comparisons the joint proof actually needs. Allow other base comparisons to be withdrawn, provided their defence obligations are reassigned consistently.

   A useful invariant records four things:

   - accepted comparisons;
   - refused comparisons, including their fixed-rival neighbourhoods;
   - the remaining defending worlds for each source–rival obligation;
   - the coherent models and selection indices already committed.

   An extension must preserve their **joint consistency**, not merely show that each newly forced refusal is individually harmless.

   PP74’s “no consecutive dominations” can remain a sufficient device where available. It must not become a requirement for the entire programme. If two dominations can compose, examine the resulting triangle rather than rejecting the configuration automatically.

6. **Openness and a surviving defender are necessary, but not sufficient.**

   For each fixed rival \(X\), the indices satisfying \(C_p\not\preceq_iX\) must form an open set. This is a condition on the complete refusal allocation, not a separate robustness requirement for every arbitrary carrier model.

   At every selected source, the surviving defences must also coexist across:

   - both orientations of records;
   - (TC) consequences;
   - common-context copies;
   - section limits;
   - protection and the actual all-world tie condition.

   In particular, do not prohibit records carried in both directions. That would exclude many of the splice comparisons the round is supposed to handle.

**The most important improvement is to make the constructor choose a defence and its alternatives together.** When a domination forces a mixed refusal at one source, the output should identify an alternative defending world—and establish that installing that alternative does not destroy another required defence. This is where the construction must move beyond PP74.

I would give the round one explicit success criterion:

> A measurable, jointly sectioned two-generation construction that handles all incoherent source–defender splices and preserves universal defence, with at least one essential mixed refusal whose consistency is proved after copying and section closure.

That would be a significant advance. A conditional preservation theorem, another finite satisfiability result, or a construction whose new defenders have no substantive section obligations would not meet it.
