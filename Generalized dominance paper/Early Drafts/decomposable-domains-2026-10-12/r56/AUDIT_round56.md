### What is genuinely established

PP64’s final-system argument looks sound **provided every index is assigned and every required verdict is settled**. Open refusal reservations give closed sections; defence and KKM then establish the desired result, including global (C).

That resolves an important bookkeeping problem. It does not establish that the construction can reach such a final system.

“The first extension works” also needs qualification. What has been constructed is a **partial edge defence**. The new off-edge coherent dominator is shown to be necessary in any full extension; it has not actually been constructed together with its comparisons and reservations. PP65 explicitly notes that tameness of the first extension remains unproved.

So we have not yet demonstrated even one repeatable step of the proposed global construction.

### A concrete improvement: propagate forced refusals, not just acceptances

The finite counterexample exposes a specific omission that can be repaired without inventing another geometric restriction.

Write, at edge worlds \(i=1,2\),
\[
a_i=[T\preceq_i D],\qquad b_i=[D\preceq_i X],
\]
where \(D\) is the edge model and \(X\) is the fill rival.

Guidance and (TC) imply
\[
\neg a_1\lor\neg b_1,\qquad
\neg a_2\lor\neg b_2.
\]
The defence obligation at the edge requires
\[
b_1\lor b_2.
\]
Together these imply
\[
\boxed{\neg a_1\lor\neg a_2.}
\]

Consequently, once \(a_1\) is accepted:

1. \(b_1\) must be refused;
2. \(b_2\) must be accepted;
3. \(a_2\) must be refused.

**The supposedly legal second acceptance should therefore have been rejected immediately.** The partial-system test tracked too few consequences of its commitments.

This suggests a precise replacement for “minimal commitment”:

> Preserve unresolved defence disjunctions, and propagate both acceptances and refusals forced by (TC), guidance, protection and those disjunctions.

This uses the axioms themselves. It does not impose transitivity, prices, unique defenders or a predetermined family. It catches the displayed trap exactly. It is not, by itself, a complete extension theorem.

### The accumulation problem remains—and changes the limit argument

PP65 is right that acceptance limits must be tracked. But adding that requirement means the earlier chain-union proof no longer settles the stronger invariant.

For example, each finite stage may contain acceptances at finitely many \(q_n\to p\), without forcing acceptance at \(p\). Their union does force it. That new acceptance can then interact with other commitments.

Thus:

**PP64’s final-system theorem survives, but consistency after topological closure at limit stages still needs proof.**

At an unassigned \(p\), the forced acceptance should initially be recorded as a constraint on **whatever coherent model is eventually assigned there**. Substituting the current provisional model prematurely would unnecessarily restrict co-design.

One further correction: the countability restriction on revisions assumes that each revision changes a set with nonempty interior. It is not a consequence of “each index changes finitely often” alone. Likewise, a dense assigned set prevents further *continuous* revisions, not arbitrary revisions of a discontinuous provisional selection.

### What I would make round 56 accomplish

**Construct the missing dominators for an entire measurable family of fills, rather than another policy for processing isolated commitments.**

Fix one region \(E_0\) of T’s silence where worlds 1 and 2 agree, and consider
\[
X_E=T[E\mapsto\text{their correct answer}],
\qquad E\subseteq E_0.
\]

The construction should handle **all measurable \(E\)**—including arbitrarily small, nested, overlapping and nonconic sets—by jointly choosing:

- coherent dominators \(N_E\);
- their indices;
- the necessary defences and refusals;
- reservations that survive accumulation.

It must also include the forced rivals generated by these new defenders. Otherwise we repeat the old pattern of solving one class by moving the difficulty somewhere else.

The key change in the brief should be:

> Do not invoke KKM to conclude that a suitable new dominator must exist. Exhibit how to choose it, and prove that the choices coexist under signed logical closure and closed sections.

The adversary should attack that same construction, especially overlapping fills and a sequence of fills chosen after earlier commitments. A bare finite module is insufficient.

**That would be a substantial advance:** a uniform, genuinely recursive co-design operation on a continuum of rivals. Another conditional theorem saying “if every step succeeds, we obtain coverage” would not be.
