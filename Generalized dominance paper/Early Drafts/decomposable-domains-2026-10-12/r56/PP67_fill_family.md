## 1. Setting and the derived choice rule

**Setting.** As in Brief 56.
- T = D_M, with M = c·1 + εΔ, ε = 3/10, c = 7/30, and vertices v_i = c·1 + εe_i.
- Class M_k is where k is the odd world. A is the majority answer (correct at the other two worlds); B is the minority answer (correct only at k).
- Q₃ := Sil(T) ∩ M₃. The set E₀ ⊆ Q₃ is any measurable set; E₀ = Q₃ is allowed. X_E := T[E ↦ A] for non-null E ⊆ E₀.
- Verdict notation: X ⪯_w Y means "X at least as good as Y at w". ⊵_w is pointwise weak betterness.
- PP65's sets: P_w := {p : T ⪯_w C_p}; W_E := R₁(X_E) ∩ R₂(X_E).

### 1.1 What is forced, and the one design principle

By PP65 Prop 2.4 (referee: correct), any coherent dominator N_E of X_E satisfies:
- (F-a) T ⋠₁ N_E, T ⋠₂ N_E and T ⪯₃ N_E;
- (F-b) N_E ≠ A on a non-null part of E;
- (F-c) p(E) ∉ W_E ∪ P₁ ∪ P₂ ∪ [e₁, e₂].

**(G-closure), the design principle.** T's defence against N_E is forced to world 3 by (F-a). We require it to be a **guidance** acceptance, i.e. N_E is weakly 3-worse than T.

*Why this is derived, not imposed.* Otherwise T ⪯₃ N_E is a non-guidance acceptance at u. PP65 Prop 4.1 and the referee's generalised trap show that non-guidance acceptances at u are exactly what kill edge defences through (TC) without any committed refusal recording it. G-closure is the condition under which every (TC) consequence of (F-a) at u closes by guidance. This is the same derivation as PP64 Prop 4.2, now applied to the dominator.

**Consequence (D3; PROVED).** Under G-closure, on E ⊆ Sil(T) the model N_E takes values in {S, A} (coherent models are never contradictory). So (F-b) says N_E = S on a non-null part of E. A **uniform** choice N_E ≡ N for all E ⊆ E₀ must therefore be silent a.e. on E₀.

### 1.2 The least element

Let 𝒩_T := {coherent D_K : D_K is weakly 3-worse than T}.

**Prop 1.1 (PROVED; CHECKED).** Put K_N := conv(M ∪ M₀) and N := D_{K_N}. Then:
- (i) N ∈ 𝒩_T, and N is silent on all of Sil(T);
- (ii) every D_K ∈ 𝒩_T answers, with N's answer, a.e. wherever N answers;
- (iii) N = T ∧ D\*: N keeps the common answer of T and D\* and is S elsewhere.

So N is the least element (the most silent member) of 𝒩_T, and it is independent of E and of E₀.

*Proof.*
- **Setup.** Let P := {g : m·g > 0 for all m ∈ M, g₃ < 0}. P is open and non-empty. On P, T answers +, which is 3-wrong; on −P it answers −, also 3-wrong. On ±P, a coherent model weakly 3-worse than T must give the same answer.
- **(ii).**
  - Let D_K ∈ 𝒩_T and k ∈ K. Then k·g > 0 for a.e. g ∈ P.
  - If k·g₀ = 0 at some g₀ ∈ P, then k·g < 0 on a non-empty open subset of P near g₀, which is a contradiction. So k ∈ P\*, the dual cone.
  - P is the interior of cone(M)\* cut by {g₃ < 0}, so by polyhedral duality P\* = cone(M) + cone(−e₃).
  - A point λm − μe₃ (m ∈ M, λ, μ ≥ 0) lies in Δ iff λ − μ = 1 and λm₃ ≥ μ. As λ runs over [1, 1/(1 − m₃)], it traces the segment from m to π(m) = (m₁, m₂, 0)/(m₁ + m₂), its radial projection from e₃.
  - Hence Δ ∩ P\* = ∪_{m∈M} [m, π(m)]. This set is convex (an intersection of convex sets), contains M and π(M) = M₀, and lies inside conv(M ∪ M₀). So Δ ∩ P\* = K_N.
  - So K ⊆ K_N. Wherever σk·g > 0 for all k ∈ K_N, the same holds for all k ∈ K.
- **(i).**
  - K_N ⊇ M, so N answers only where T does, and with T's answer.
  - On ±P, each k = λm − μe₃ ∈ K_N has k·g = λm·g − μg₃ > 0 (for g ∈ P). So N keeps every 3-wrong answer of T.
  - Where T is silent, N is silent. Where T's answer is 3-correct, N gives it or is silent. So N is weakly 3-worse than T.
- **(iii).** D_{conv(K₁ ∪ K₂)} answers σ iff both D_{K₁} and D_{K₂} answer σ. ∎

CHECKED (`geom67.py`, 27,666 exact samples):
- N = T ∧ D\* at every sample;
- every sampled m ∈ K_N keeps all of T's 3-wrong answers;
- of 289 sampled m ∉ K_N, 288 drop one on the samples, and the remaining one lies within the boundary slack.

**Why the least element.**
- It is the only choice that is uniform in **both** E and E₀.
- Its gains at worlds 1 and 2 are maximal on R_B := M₃ ∩ {T = B}, where T's 3-correct minority answer is wrong at worlds 1 and 2 (Lemma 1.2(c)). These gains are what pay for the fills (§4).
- Any other member of 𝒩_T that is silent on E₀ is N plus some 3-wrong answers, so it carries the same obligations and more.

**Lemma 1.2 (PROVED; CHECKED, `geom67.out`).**
- (a) D\* = A a.e. on M₃ (PP29 Lemma 3.3(2)). D\* is weakly 3-worse than T (PP64 Lemma 4.2), and also weakly 3-worse than N.
- (b) The triples (T, N, D\*) take only the following types, by class:
  - on M₃: (S,S,A), (A,A,A), (B,S,A);
  - on M₁ (and symmetrically M₂): (S,S,S), (S,S,B), (A,A,A), (A,S,S), (A,S,B), (B,B,B);
  - unanimous g: all three give the unanimous answer.
- (c) R_B is a non-null open cone, for example around g = (−1, −1, 10). There T = B, N = S and D\* = A, so N and D\* are strictly better than T at worlds 1 and 2 on R_B.
- (d) L_N := {N ≠ T} is the set where T's answer is 3-correct and D\* differs. N = S there.

*Proof.*
- (a) PP29 and PP64 as cited. D\* ⊴₃ N because N = T ∧ D\* and D\* ⊴₃ T.
- (b) Weak 3-worseness allows only T = D\*, or T = S with D\* 3-wrong, or T 3-correct with D\* ∈ {S, 3-wrong}. N = T ∧ D\*. On M₃, D\* = A by (a).
- (c) At (−1, −1, 10), T says + (PP64 Lemma 4.2), which is B on M₃, and D\* says −. The inequalities are strict, so they hold on a cone. ∎

## 2. The module Σ_fam

**Indices and balls.**
- Fix p_N := (2, 2, 1)/5. Fix r > 0 such that the following three sets are pairwise disjoint, with B_u and B_N inside the open simplex:
  - B_u := B(u, r);
  - B_N := B(p_N, r);
  - B_\* := B(p\*, r) ∩ Δ.
- The radius r is fixed once and for all. It does not depend on any rival, world or E.

**Assignments and registration.** C_u = T, C_{p_N} = N and C_{p\*} = D\*. P = {T, N, D\*}. All three models are face-admissible: N because p_N is interior, D\* because K = M₀ lies in the edge.

**Commitments 𝒞.** Literals are (record, world) pairs, so (IB) is built in. For every non-null E ⊆ E₀:
- (C0) **Guidance.** All weak-guidance acceptances and strict-guidance refusals, for all records.
- (C1) **Domination.** N ⪯_w X_E for w = 1, 2, 3. The record is rec(N, X_E) = (L_N : S → T) ⊔ (E : S → A).
- (C2) **Edge defence at both edge worlds.** D\* ⪯₁ X_E and D\* ⪯₂ X_E. The record is (L_{D\*} ∖ E : D\* → T) (PP65 Lemma 2.1).
- (C3) **Mutual defences of the assigned models**, at worlds 1 and 2: N ⪯_w T, D\* ⪯_w T and D\* ⪯_w N. The reverse directions at world 3 are guidance: T ⪯₃ N, T ⪯₃ D\* and N ⪯₃ D\*.

**Signed closure.** Σ_fam consists of 𝒞 together with every literal entailed by 𝒞 and the ground clause set 𝒦_mod. 𝒦_mod contains:
- guidance units;
- every (TC) clause;
- rule III for P;
- direct (C) for P, including the F2 prohibition;
- Def(p, X) for p ∈ {u, p_N, p\*} and every model X;
- the reservation clauses below.

Write Acc_w and Ref_w for the accepted and refused records at world w.

**Reservations.** Res := {(B_M, M ⊕ ρ, w) : ρ ∈ Ref_w, M ∈ P carries ρ}. Every refusal at every assigned carrier, including all (IB) copies, is reserved on its carrier's ball.

**Provisional completion (not committed).** Ĉ := T on B_u, N on B_N, D\* on B_\*. The definition of Ĉ elsewhere is free.

**Pending (deliberately not committed).** These are kept as disjunctions:
- D\*'s defence against N's fills Y_E;
- the defences of T, N and D\* against all other rivals;
- every rival–rival record.

Theorem 4.1 shows that they are jointly satisfiable.

## 3. Forced literals and the forced rivals

**Lemma 3.1 (signed closure, main literals; PROVED).** Σ_fam contains the following, for all non-null E ⊆ E₀ and w ∈ {1, 2}.

- (a) **The audit's trap, resolved eagerly.** T ⋠_w D\* and T ⋠_w N. Hence a_w := [T ⪯_w D\*] is refused at both edge worlds, and the b_w(E) := [D\* ⪯_w X_E] are accepted.
- (b) **Generalised trap and Lemma 4.1(b) of PP64.**
  - Z ⋠_w D\* for every Z with X_E ⊵_w Z strictly, for some E. In particular this covers every partial splice Z_w = T[G_w ↦ D\*] of REFEREE_round55 Prop G, so every kill pair of that proposition has its literal at T refused.
  - T ⋠_w T[H′ ↦ D\*] for P^{D\*}_w ∖ E₀ ⊆ H′ ⊆ L_{D\*}, H′ ≠ L_{D\*} ∖ E. Here P^{D\*}_w is the part of L_{D\*} where D\* is strictly w-better than T.
- (c) **Prop K kills at N.**
  - Z ⋠_w N, for w = 1, 2, 3, for every Z with X_E ⊵_w Z strictly, for some E.
  - In particular T ⋠_w T[H ↦ N] for P^N_w ⊆ H ⊆ L_N (w = 1, 2), and D\* ⋠₃ N.
- (d) **Strictness of the domination.**
  - X_E ⋠₃ N for every non-null E.
  - X_E ⋠_w N (w = 1, 2) whenever E₀ ∖ E is non-null.
- (e) **PP65 Prop 2.3 at D\*, for every E.**
  - (b) T ⋠_{1,2} D\* by (a). T ⪯₃ D\* and D\* ⋠₃ X_E are guidance.
  - (c) X_E ⋠_{1,2} D\* whenever E₀ ∖ E is non-null; X_E ⪯₃ D\* is guidance.
  - (d) D\* ⋠₃ X_{E″} is guidance.
- (f) **T's defence.** T defends against N, D\*, every T[H ↦ N], every T[H′ ↦ D\*] and every X_E at world 3, by guidance.

*Proof.* Each item is the triangle rule "two accepted links and a refused reverse ⇒ refuse the third", unless it is guidance.

- **(a)** Prop 2.2 of PP65: T ⪯_w M, M ⪯_w X_E and X_E ⪯_w T (guidance), while T ⪯_w X_E is refused. Use M = D\* with (C2), and M = N with (C1).
- **(b), first part.** D\* ⪯_w X_E (C2) and X_E ⪯_w Z (guidance); then Z ⪯_w D\* would require Z ⪯_w X_E, which is refused.
- **(b), second part.** The H′ case is the first part with Z = T[L_{D\*} ∖ H′ ↦ D\*]: the referee's §1.6 computation, with E₀ in place of E.
- **(c)** The same triangle with N and (C1) at all three worlds.
  - **The two named instances.**
    - For the splices, take Z := T[L_N ∖ H ↦ N]. If H ⊇ P^N_w, then Z's N-part lies where N is weakly w-worse than T. On E, Z = S while X_E is w-correct. So X_E ⊵_w Z strictly, and Z ⋠_w N.
    - By (IB), rec(Z, N) = (H : T → N) = rec(T, T[H ↦ N]).
    - D\* ⊴₃ X_E holds since D\* = A on E and D\* ⊴₃ T.
- **(d)** N ⪯_w X_{E′} (C1) and X_{E′} ⪯_w X_E (guidance), for a non-null E′ ⊊ E at w = 3 (μ is atomless), or E′ = E₀ at w = 1, 2. (TC) would then demand X_E ⪯_w X_{E′}, which strict (G) refuses.
- **(e)** On E both D\* and X_E are A. Off E, X_E = T ⊵₃ D\*. The rest is (a).
- **(f)** N, D\* and both kinds of splice are weakly 3-worse than T (Prop 1.1, Lemma 1.2(a)), and X_E is 3-worse than T. ∎

**Unit propagation from (C2) is complete for the trap clauses.** REFEREE_round55 §4 shows that the chain a₁ ⇒ ¬b₁ ⇒ b₂ ⇒ ¬a₂ is found by unit propagation. Here b₁ and b₂ are both committed, so ¬a₁ and ¬a₂ are units.

## 4. Consistency: a certificate for the whole signed closure

**Theorem 4.1 (PROVED).** There is a total relation 𝒲 on all measurable four-advice models with the following properties.
- (i) Reflexivity, (IB), strict (G) at every world, and (TC) on every triangle.
- (ii) T, N and D\* are protected (no model weakly dominates them on their supports without tying), satisfy direct (C), and are defended on their supports against every model.
- (iii) Every literal of 𝒞 holds in 𝒲.

Consequently the signed closure Σ_fam is contained in 𝒲's verdicts. So Σ_fam has no record both accepted and refused, no F1 and no F2. Every pending disjunction Def(p, X) (p ∈ {u, p_N, p\*}, X arbitrary) and every direct-(C) obligation of P is satisfiable **simultaneously** with Σ_fam.

**Construction.**
- **The field.** Fix f_w(g, S) := 0. For each mixed g with odd world o and type τ (Lemma 1.2(b)), set
  - f(g, A) := ω(g)|g|·a_τ and f(g, B) := ω(g)|g|·b_τ;
  - f_w(g, contradictory) := ω(g)|g|·(1 + max(a_{τ,w}, b_{τ,w}, 0)).

  The rational vectors a_τ, b_τ are listed in `witness67.py`. They have the rank-monotone sign pattern: at worlds w ≠ o, a_w < 0 < b_w; at o, b_o < 0 < a_o.
- **Unanimous g.** f_w = −|g|, 0, |g|, 2|g| for correct, S, wrong, contradictory.
- **Weights.** ω := W on R_B, and ω := 1 elsewhere.
- **Totals.** Φ_w(Z) := ∫ f_w(g, Z(g)) dμ, which is finite since μ is Gaussian.
- **Extra key at world 3.** κ(Z) := μ{g ∈ E₀ : rank₃ Z(g) ≥ 2}.
- **The relation.**
  - X ⪯^𝒲_w Y iff Φ_w(X) ≤ Φ_w(Y), for w = 1, 2.
  - X ⪯^𝒲_3 Y iff (κ(X), Φ₃(X)) ≤ (κ(Y), Φ₃(Y)) lexicographically.

**Step 1: pointwise Bayes acts.** Take λ_T = (1, 1, 1), λ_N = (1, 1, ½) and λ_D = (1, 1, 0). Then T(g), N(g) and D\*(g) are the **unique** minimisers of λ·f(g, ·), at every g.
- This is a finite check over 15 types, done in exact rationals (`witness67.out`, first line).
- Examples, as (a; b): (B,S,A) on M₃ uses ((−1,−1,10); (1,1,5/2)); (A,S,B) on M₁ uses ((3,−1,−3); (−3/2,1,2)).
- Positive scaling by ω(g)|g| preserves argmins. Contradictory advice is never a minimiser.

**Step 2: the axioms of (i).**
- Each order is a total preorder, so reflexivity and (TC) hold.
- Each comparison depends only on the integral over the record's support, so (IB) holds.
- κ is non-decreasing in 3-rank and Φ_w is strictly increasing, so strict (G) holds.

**Step 3: protection, direct (C) and defence of T and N.**
- κ(T) = κ(N) = 0, because both are silent on E₀.

## 5. Reservations, closed sections, limits, and (H_acc)

*Proof.*

### 5.2 Answer to the relay: is (H_acc) maintained by the uniform step?

**Yes, for every clause the uniform step creates among literals at assigned indices that contains a module record.**

### 5.3 What is OPEN, and a costly fallback

**OPEN.** Two cases are not settled.
- **(O1)** (H_acc) for entailed negative clauses all of whose literals Σ_fam leaves undecided. Examples are clauses through rival–rival records, or through undecided rows of T, N and D\*.
- **(O2)** Clauses with literals at future carriers: the copy cascade.

## 7. Where the uniform rule stops

**Prop 7.1 (second-generation rivals have no assigned dominator; PROVED, axiom-level for the module).** Let E ⊆ E₀ be non-null and non-conic, and let P^N_1 ∪ P^N_2 ⊆ H ⊊ L_N with L_N ∖ H non-null and H non-conic. Then:
- Y_E = N[E ↦ A] and Z_H = T[H ↦ N] are incoherent;
- in every relation satisfying the axioms and containing Σ_fam, neither is dominated by T, N or D\*.

*Proof.*
- **Incoherence.** Coherent models are conic (Lemma H).
- **Y_E.**
  - T ⋠_{1,2} Y_E, by the triangle T → Y_E → N → T with N ⪯_w T (C3) and N ⋠_w Y_E (G).
  - N ⋠_{1,2} Y_E (G).
  - D\* ⋠₃ Y_E (G): Y_E ⊵₃ N ⊵₃ D\*, strictly off E.
- **Z_H.**
  - T ⋠_{1,2} Z_H (Lemma 3.1(c)).
  - N ⋠₃ Z_H (G): Z_H ⊵₃ N, strictly on L_N ∖ H.
  - D\* ⋠₃ Z_H, since Z_H ⊵₃ N ⊵₃ D\*. ∎

So the module's own defenders generate continua of incoherent rivals whose dominators must be **new models**. By PP65 Prop 2.2 with base N, any dominator N′ of Y_E has N ⋠_{1,2} N′. The same rule then applies one level down.

(D\*[E ↦ S] behaves differently. T is excluded for E₀ ∖ E non-null. N is excluded in 𝒲, by the fourth inequality of Step 5, but not by the axioms.)

**Prop 7.2 (the recipe does not iterate; PROVED).**
- (i) The recipe "dominator := base ∧ D\*" is idempotent: N ∧ D\* = N.
- (ii) Any G-closed dominator N′ of the Y_E is weakly 3-worse than N, hence in 𝒩_T. So it answers wherever N does (Prop 1.1(ii)). It equals N plus some 3-wrong answers on Sil(N) ∖ E₀.
- (iii) If E₀ = Q₃, no coherent model silent on Q₃ answers A at any point of R_B. So these additions lie in M₁ ∪ M₂, where each one improves one edge world and worsens the other.

*Proof.*
- (i) By Prop 1.1(iii), since N already agrees with D\* wherever it answers.
- (ii) By Prop 1.1.
- (iii) Fix (g₁, g₂) with common sign σ and vary g₃ against σ. Every vertex sum σk·g increases as |g₃| decreases, for every k ∈ Δ. So a coherent model answering A = σ at a point of R_B (large |g₃|) answers σ on the whole T-silent part of that vertical segment, which lies in Q₃. ∎

**Prop 7.3 (the guidance-closed uniform rule is non-Archimedean at world 3; PROVED).**
- (a) Σ_fam accepts rec(N, X_E) at world 3 for every non-null E, and refuses rec(N, T) at world 3 (G). Yet d(rec(N, X_E), rec(N, T)) = μ(E) → 0.
- (b) No relation X ⪯₃ Y ⟺ ∫f₃(g, X(g)) dμ ≤ ∫f₃(g, Y(g)) dμ, with f₃ real and integrable, contains these literals.
- (c) The same holds for **every** uniform dominator N with N ⋠₃ T, in particular every G-closed N ≠ T.
- (d) It disappears if N is tied with T at world 3 (not G-closed), or if N_E depends on E with N_E → T in measure.

*Proof.*
- **(b), (c).** Φ₃(X_E) → Φ₃(T) < Φ₃(N) as μ(E) → 0, by absolute continuity.
- **(d).** With a world-3 tie, Φ₃(N) = Φ₃(T) ≤ Φ₃(X_E). For the E-dependent case, take Φ₃(N_E) − Φ₃(T) ≤ ∫_E(f₃(A) − f₃(S)). ∎

This is why 𝒲 needs the extra world-3 key. The key is **not** an axiom-level necessity: Theorem 4.1 shows the pattern is consistent. It is a feature of combining uniformity with G-closure. No lexicographic level is added to the ranking; the pattern is a set of verdicts.

**Prop 7.4 (uniform generations are finite in additive certificates; PROVED).** Let 𝒲′ be any certificate additive at worlds 1 and 2, with bounded field. Suppose N⁰ = T, N¹, …, N^m are silent on E₀ and satisfy N^{j+1} ⪯_w N^j[E₀ ↦ A] for w = 1, 2. Then

  m ≤ (Φ_w(T) − inf_Z Φ_w(Z)) / α_w,  with α_w := ∫_{E₀}(f_w(S) − f_w(A)) dμ > 0.

*Proof.* Φ_w(N^{j+1}) ≤ Φ_w(N^j) − α_w. ∎

**Reading.** An unbounded recursion of fill-dominators must let the Φ-gap shrink with the fill: dominators of the fills of E must depend on E. With E_n shrinking, this brings back index accumulation p(E_n) → p_∞, which is exactly where (H_acc) and Theorem 5.4 of the referee are needed.

Whether an infinite **uniform** chain exists at the level of the axioms alone (non-additive at worlds 1 and 2) is **OPEN**.

**Where the uniform construction fails, precisely.**
- **Configuration.** The second generation: Y_E (E ⊆ E₀ non-conic) and Z_H (H ⊇ P^N_1 ∪ P^N_2).
- **Clause.** Existence of a coherent dominator (global (C)/(S)) for these rivals. The module supplies none (Prop 7.1), and the uniform recipe cannot supply one (Prop 7.2).
- **Level.** A feature of the uniform, G-closed choice rule (Props 7.2–7.4), not an axiom-level conflict. 𝒲 shows the module is consistent with every axiom except the final (S) and global (C), which are discharged only at D = Δ.

**Strongest uniform result proved.** Theorems 4.1, 5.1 and 6.1:
- one dominator and one edge defender per class;
- all fills of T's silence by the majority answer, for any E₀^k ⊆ Q_k;
- every signed consequence;
- every forced rival's comparisons with the assigned models;
- fixed reservations;
- no accumulation;
- an exact certificate.
