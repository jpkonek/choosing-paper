**No—not as the main plan.** The two-neighbourhood problem is a useful diagnostic, but I cannot justify calling it the route most likely to produce full coverage. Even success would leave universal section closedness, interactions between different refusal neighbourhoods, and the boundary largely untouched. We have repeatedly mistaken a solvable local problem for a mechanism that will extend globally.

I would change direction.

**The central problem is now global compatibility, not finding another exceptional comparison.** We can produce non-additive comparisons, protect individual targets, satisfy arbitrarily many prescribed tests, and meet everything except sections. What we lack is a construction principle that makes *all* the defence and refusal obligations coexist.

That suggests returning to co-design—but with a much stronger requirement than “choose another family and try rankings on it.”

### Make a global extension principle the principal target

The next proposed construction should specify how to extend a partially built system while preserving:

- T’s undominatedness;
- guidance, independence and (TC);
- the already established fixed-rival section conditions;
- defence obligations on every face;
- freedom to choose new coherent defenders.

**The new coherent defender must be an output of the extension step.** It should not be restricted in advance to posterior models, caps, edge segments, or another convenient family.

Equally importantly, the construction should not freeze every previously accepted comparison. That has repeatedly recreated an additive kernel or made later repairs impossible. It should preserve the mathematical obligations already secured, while allowing unnecessary comparisons to be revised.

This is ambitious and difficult. It is also the first target whose successful proof would actually explain how to obtain a full system.

### The first test must include the limit problem

An extension theorem that handles only finite constraints is insufficient. Theorem C now makes that especially clear.

The construction needs to retain **positive neighbourhoods of refusal**, not merely refusals at individual indices. Their sizes may depend on the rival and world; no common lower bound is required. But they cannot silently shrink to zero in the final system.

Thus the proposed extension step must answer:

> After adding a new rival and choosing its coherent defenders, which previously established refusal neighbourhoods remain valid, and why will the final relation still have closed sections?

That question belongs in the construction’s invariant from the beginning. It cannot be postponed to a final continuity argument.

We explored versions of this much earlier. Returning to it is justified only if we now use what the intervening work has established: defender choice must remain flexible, additive endorsement must not be preserved, and finite satisfiability alone cannot supply the limit argument.

### Do not lock the programme to the posterior family

The posterior family is a valuable test case. It is not the objective.

Its pair geometry is favourable, but its kink geometry creates difficulties for several architectures. We should not require a full positive result to realise every posterior model merely because that family is explicit.

The objective is:

> Given T, construct a ranking and whatever coherent family that ranking can support, including the face behaviour required by (S).

A proposed extension may add a different coherent model or alter the provisional selection. The actual undominated class remains something to prove, not something we can declare.

### How I would allocate the next effort

1. **Main effort: one genuinely global construction proposal.** Require its extension operation, preserved conditions, and limit argument to be written down before undertaking large calculations.
2. **Adversary: attack that operation at its first nontrivial extension.** Use the existing swap, splice and guidance traps. Do not merely test another finite table.
3. **Small diagnostic effort: the two-neighbourhood problem.** Use it to reject an extension proposal quickly. Do not let it become another multi-round programme.
4. **Keep the negative route separate.** A proof that the axioms themselves conflict is valuable, but exclusions of further scoring architectures should no longer be described as approaching that conclusion.

The success criterion should be severe: **does the proposed mechanism explain how to handle the next arbitrary rival without sacrificing earlier obligations, and how to pass to the final system?** If it does not, it is another module, however elegant.

I cannot honestly promise that this global approach is tractable, or claim to have the missing extension lemma. But I do think it is better aligned with the intended breakthrough than another refinement of the local refusal pattern. **The two-neighbourhood task should be a test of a global idea, not a substitute for one.**
