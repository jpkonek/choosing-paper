### What has genuinely been achieved

The application now has one mechanism that works across finite and infinite sets of worlds:

- **Minimax finds a coherent improvement.**
- **Trimming, where needed, ensures that the same change cannot be made from a coherent model.**
- **The ranking permits these improvements while protecting coherent models.**

The key improvement over earlier rounds is that the proof no longer depends on constructing a specially indexed family of coherent defenders. Each incoherent model receives a suitable replacement directly. There is no need to solve the old selection problem.

The minimax argument also deserves a particularly clear explanation. It temporarily allows fractional acceptance of gambles to obtain a compact convex optimisation problem. The null-boundary assumption then ensures that an optimal fractional choice is an ordinary acceptance set. **This is why the method delivers an actual coherent model rather than merely a randomised or fractional substitute.** That is an illuminating part of the mechanism, not disposable technical machinery.

### The most impactful tasks for the next round

**1. Write the application around the repair theorem, with the ranking as its consequence.**

Introduce the main result and its interpretation before developing the technical setting in full. A reader should understand early that:

> Every incoherent acceptance pattern admits a uniformly better coherent replacement, and the replacement can be chosen so that its change would never be available from a coherent acceptance pattern.

Then establish precisely what “incoherent,” “better” and “available” mean.

The outline’s proof chain is appropriate. However, the main text need not reproduce every equivalent formulation, domain classification and auxiliary example. Retain those that explain the mechanism or delimit the headline theorem; move the rest to appendices.

In particular, the general restricted-domain corollary is useful. The detailed classification of one-sided domains is secondary now that the full-domain result covers all targets.

**2. Make the relationship between the papers explicit—and avoid introducing another unnecessary requirement.**

LDG should separate three questions:

- What follows once coherent undominated replacements exist?
- What independently checkable conditions ensure their existence?
- How can suitable rankings themselves be constructed?

The application answers the third question directly. **It should not be forced through LDG’s Bayes-choice or compact-family sufficient conditions.** Those are valuable methods for other applications, not requirements that the new construction must satisfy.

This distinction is the lasting lesson from the excessive reliance on (S). The definitive papers should embody it in their organisation, rather than repeatedly recounting the historical mistake.

**3. State the philosophical achievement and its limitation together.**

The universal construction proves that the agreed ranking requirements are compatible with—and can represent—exactly coherence. It also supplies a substantive repair theorem explaining how incoherent models can be improved.

But its ranking uses the distinction between changes available and unavailable to coherent models. Consequently, it does **not by itself derive coherence from independently justified accuracy comparisons**.

Neither dismiss the theorem as trivial nor present it as an independent accuracy argument. The repair theorem is substantial; the motivation for restricting score comparisons remains a separate philosophical question.

**4. Make the scope readable without weakening the headline unnecessarily.**

I recommend presenting the main result with a full-support admissible measure, then immediately explaining that full support can be dropped if distinct credal sets need not remain distinguishable.

That gives the principal statement its clearest interpretation: distinct credal sets correspond to distinct coherent models. The weaker measure assumptions remain an explicit strengthening, rather than allowing the headline to include cases where many targets collapse into the same equivalence class.

Also state prominently:

- the gamble space is separable and has an order unit;
- models are identified up to null sets;
- arbitrary sets of worlds do not mean arbitrary spaces of bounded gambles;
- every incoherent model has **some** uniformly improving replacement, although not every dominating comparison has a uniform margin.

These qualifications belong in the statements and introduction, not only in a concluding scope section.

### Keep the structured example—but sharply reduce its role

**My decision is to keep one representative veto–Brier example, with its necessary proof in an appendix.** The extra two pages are justified if they establish a meaningful contrast: complete, transitive, score-based rankings can cover genuinely imprecise targets, although the universal construction uses a weaker ordering structure.

The present outline proposes substantially more than one example: a general coverage theorem, a description of the visibility class, a figure and references to several extensions. That is too much for this purpose.

Use a representative contamination result. Give the rule and its significance in the main text; include the required admissibility and separation arguments in the appendix. Preserve the broader structured-construction results separately. Do not leave the example dependent on an argument removed from LDG.
