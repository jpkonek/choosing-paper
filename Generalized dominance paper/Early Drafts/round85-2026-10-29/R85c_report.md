## 1. Setting and the ranking

Finitely many worlds Ω={1,…,n} until §5; E=ℝⁿ. μ is a finite Borel measure satisfying (M1) and (M2); standard Gaussian is the model case. Models are measurable sets modulo null sets. D_Q, Q_B and H(B)=D_{Q_B} (H(B)=E if Q_B=∅) are as in app_long `def:hull`. Write ‖ψ(g)‖=max_ω|ψ_ω(g)|.

**Field conditions.**
- **(F)** Each ψ_ω is measurable and sign-respecting: ψ_ω(g)g_ω>0 for a.e. g. Also ∫‖ψ‖dμ<∞, and N_p={p·ψ=0} is null for every p∈Δ.
- **(C)** Every Bayes set B_p={p·ψ>0}, p∈Δ, is almost coherent.

**Definition (ψ-hull ranking R_H^ψ).** For a change X→Y put A=Y∖X and B=X∖Y. Then Y⪰_ωX iff X=Y a.e., or
- s_ω(Y)>s_ω(X), where s_ω(Z)=∫_Zψ_ω dμ, and
- either the change is guided at ω, or A⊆H(B) a.e.

**Lemma 1 (boxes; PROVED).** Let 0<l_ω<u_ω, ψ_ω=l_ω g_ω⁺−u_ω g_ω⁻ and Bx=∏[l_ω,u_ω]. For p∈Δ put K_p={p∘v/(p·v): v∈Bx}. Then K_p is a credal set in Δ_{supp p}, and
- p·ψ(g) = min_{v∈Bx}(p·v)(k_v·g), where k_v=p∘v/(p·v);
- hence sign(p·ψ(g)) = sign(P_{K_p}(g)).

So B_p={P_{K_p}>0}=D_{K_p} a.e. and N_p={P_{K_p}=0}, which is null by (M2). Boxes satisfy (F) and (C).

*Proof.* For each ω, min over v_ω∈[l_ω,u_ω] of v_ω g_ω is l_ω g_ω if g_ω≥0 and u_ω g_ω if g_ω<0. So p·ψ(g)=min_v Σp_ωv_ωg_ω = min_v (p·v)(k_v·g). Since p·v>0, the minimum is positive, zero or negative exactly when min_v k_v·g is. K_p is the image of a box under a projective map with positive denominator, so it is compact and convex. ∎

## 2. Structural properties

**Proposition 2 (PROVED).** Under (F), R_H^ψ has the following properties.
- **Independence**, in the arbitrary-set form: every verdict depends only on (A,B).
- **World-by-world guidance.**
- **No ties between distinct models**, so transitivity through ties holds.
- **Every accepted non-identity comparison strictly raises s_ω**, so the three-model condition and finite-chain consistency hold.

*Proof.*
1. s_ω(Y)−s_ω(X)=∫_Aψ_ω−∫_Bψ_ω, and guidance and H(B) depend only on (A,B).
2. Let the change be non-null and guided at ω. Then ψ_ω>0 a.e. on A⊆{g_ω>0} and ψ_ω<0 a.e. on B, so s_ω rises strictly and Y⪰_ωX. The reverse comparison would need s_ω(X)>s_ω(Y), which is false. So Y≻_ωX.
3. The remaining items follow from strict increase, as in app_long `lem:structure`. ∎

## 3. (a) Protection

**Theorem 3 (PROVED).** Assume (F).
1. **(i)** If C is almost coherent and C⊆B_r a.e. for some r∈Δ, then C is undominated. (C) is not needed here.
2. **(ii)** Let μ be atomless. If C is almost coherent and C⊄B_r a.e. for every r∈Δ, then some non-null B⊆C has s_ω(B)<0 at every ω. The pure removal C→C∖B then dominates C.

*Proof of (i).*
1. Let Y dominate C. Then Y≠C, and every world accepts the change, which adds A (disjoint from C) and removes B⊆C.
2. *Case A⊆H(B).* B⊆C=D_Q a.e. gives H(B)⊆D_Q (`lem:hull`(c)), so A is null.
   - Then s_ω(B)<0 at every ω, and B is non-null.
   - But B⊆B_r a.e. and N_r is null, so r·ψ>0 a.e. on B. Hence r·s(B)=∫_B r·ψ>0.
   - This contradicts r≥0, r≠0 and s(B)≪0.
3. *Case A⊄H(B).* Every world accepts by guidance, so the change is guided at every world. Then A lies a.e. in the nonnegative orthant and B in the nonpositive orthant. A coherent C contains the first and meets the second in a null set (app_long `lem:guidedeverywhere`), so A and B are null. This contradicts Y≠C. ∎

*Proof of (ii).*
1. By Lyapunov's theorem, R_C={s(B):B⊆C} is a compact convex subset of ℝⁿ containing 0.
2. Suppose R_C misses the open orthant −ℝⁿ₊₊. Separation gives λ≠0 and c with λ·z≥c≥λ·w for z∈R_C and w∈−ℝⁿ₊₊.
   - Letting w=−te_i with t→∞ gives λ≥0.
   - Letting w→0 and taking z=0 gives c=0.
3. So ∫_Bλ·ψ≥0 for every B⊆C, that is, λ·ψ≥0 a.e. on C. With r=λ/Σλ and N_r null, this gives C⊆B_r a.e., which is excluded.
4. Hence some B⊆C has s(B)≪0; B is non-null since s(∅)=0.
5. The pure removal has A=∅⊆H(B). It strictly raises every s_ω, so it is accepted at every world. Its reverse is refused. ∎

So **the analogue of §5's ranking no longer protects every coherent model. It protects exactly the upward closure of the ψ-Bayes family.** The integral-score case is the special case in which the Bayes family is the precise models, and their upward closure is all coherent models.

**Corollary 4 (boxes; PROVED).** Let μ be Gaussian, or more generally satisfy (M1), (M2) and full support, and be atomless. Then D_Q is undominated under the box hull ranking iff K_r⊆Q for some r∈Δ.

*Proof.* By Lemma 1, B_r=D_{K_r}. If K_r⊆Q, then D_Q⊆D_{K_r}.
- Conversely, let k∈K_r∖Q. Separate k strictly from Q in aff Δ, and add a multiple of 𝟙 (states give 𝟙 value 1). This gives g with k·g<0<P_Q(g).
- A ball about g lies in D_Q∖D_{K_r} and has positive measure. Now apply Theorem 3. ∎

**Corollary 5 (PROVED; worked examples).**
1. **Vacuous model.** V=D_Δ is protected by every box. Under complete integral rankings and W, C2 Thm 4.2 makes V dominated; here it is not.
2. **The audit's T** (two worlds, Q=[1/4,3/4], symmetric box ratio ρ=u/l). K_r=[r/(r+ρ(1−r)), ρr/(ρr+1−r)] lies inside Q iff ρ/(3+ρ)≤r≤3/(3+ρ). Such an r exists iff ρ≤3.
3. **The contamination triangle** T1=D_M, M={m:m_i≥a}, 0<a<1/3, symmetric box.
   - For coordinate i, the worst element of K_r takes v_i=1 and v_j=ρ for j≠i. So K_r⊆M iff r_i≥aρ/(1−a+aρ) for every i.
   - Summing over i, some r qualifies iff ρ≤(1−a)/(2a); then r=u works.
   - For a=0.2 this means ρ≤2. The hexagon B=[1,2]³ of the handoff touches M exactly at its vertices (2,2,1)/5.

**Proposition 6 (PROVED).** Let μ have full support and be atomless. Let each ψ_ω be continuous and *strictly commission-averse*: ψ_ω(g)+ψ_ω(−g)<0 whenever g_ω≠0. Every full box qualifies, since ψ_ω(g)+ψ_ω(−g)=−(u_ω−l_ω)|g_ω|. Suppose Q⊆h^⊥ for some h with every h_ω≠0. Then D_Q is dominated under R_H^ψ.

*Proof.*
1. By Theorem 3(ii) it suffices to exclude r∈Δ with r·ψ≥0 a.e. on D_Q.
2. Suppose such an r exists. The set {P_Q>0}⊆D_Q is open, and contains h+ε𝟙 and −h+ε𝟙 for every ε>0.
3. Continuity and full support give r·ψ≥0 everywhere on that open set. Letting ε→0 gives r·ψ(h)≥0 and r·ψ(−h)≥0.
4. But r·(ψ(h)+ψ(−h))=Σ_ω r_ω(ψ_ω(h)+ψ_ω(−h))<0. ∎

*Precise models.* If p is not a vertex, choose h with p·h=0 and no zero coordinate. Pick ω₁,ω₂∈supp p, put h_ω=1 for every other ω, take h_{ω₁}=t≠0, and solve for h_{ω₂}; for all but one value of t, h_{ω₂}≠0. Hence **every precise non-vertex model is dominated under every continuous, strictly commission-averse hull ranking.** The precise vertex models D_{e_i} are protected, since K_{e_i}={e_i}.

**Corollary 7 (coverage by one box ranking per target; PROVED).** Call a box full if l_ω<u_ω for every ω. D_Q is protected by the hull ranking of some full box iff
- Q contains a vertex, or
- Q∩Δ_F has nonempty interior relative to aff Δ_F, for some face F with |F|≥2.

*Proof.*
- *"If", first case.* If e_i∈Q, then K_{e_i}={e_i}⊆Q.
- *"If", second case.* Otherwise pick r in the relative interior of Q∩Δ_F, with a relative ball of radius η inside Q. Take the box [1,1+κ]ⁿ: then |k_ω−r_ω|≤κ for every k∈K_r, so K_r⊆Q for small κ.
- *"Only if".* Let K_r⊆Q, with F=supp r.
  - If |F|=1, Q contains a vertex.
  - If |F|≥2, K_r contains the image of the open box ∏_F(l_ω,u_ω) under v↦r∘v/(r·v). This is a diagonal homeomorphism of ℝ^F₊₊ followed by normalisation, which is an open map onto relint Δ_F. So K_r has nonempty relative interior. ∎

In two worlds the targets left out are exactly the precise interior models (Prop 6); §6 handles them with a different field. In three worlds, T1 and every target with nonempty interior are covered.

## 4. (b) Selection: the neighbourhood construction with ψ

Assume (F) and (C), finite Ω, (M1) and (M2). Put

    ρ(X,Y) = ∫_{X△Y} (1+‖ψ(g)‖) dμ.

**Lemma 8a (consistent rivals; PROVED).** Let X be consistent and incoherent, Q=Q_X and G=D_Q. Then

    γ(X) := min_{p∈Δ} [ ∫_G (p·ψ)⁺ dμ − p·s(X) ] > 0.

*Proof.*
1. X⊆G a.e. by `lem:hull`(b).
2. For fixed p, the maximisers of Z↦p·s(Z) over Z⊆G are G∩B_p modulo null sets, since N_p is null.
3. G∩B_p=D_Q∩D_{K_p}=D_{conv(Q∪K_p)} a.e. is coherent. X is incoherent, so X≠G∩B_p and the bracket is positive.
4. The bracket is continuous in p, by dominated convergence with the envelope ‖ψ‖ (and linearity of p·s(X)). Δ is compact. ∎

**Lemma 8b (inconsistent rivals; PROVED).** Let X be inconsistent, and put h(q)=∫_X min(q·ψ(g),0)dμ for q∈Δ.
1. h is concave and continuous, and negative everywhere.
2. Let q₀ maximise h, U=B_{q₀}, X₊=X∩U and L₀=X∖U. Then s_ω(L₀)≤h(q₀)<0 at every ω.
3. X₊ is consistent, and with G=H(X₊):

       γ(X) := min_p [ ∫_G (p·ψ)⁺ − p·s(X) ] ≥ −h(q₀) > 0.

*Proof.*
1. q↦q·ψ(g) is linear, so h is concave. It is continuous by dominated convergence.
2. If h(q)=0, then q·ψ≥0 a.e. on X, so X⊆B_q a.e. since N_q is null. B_q is coherent by (C), so X would be consistent. Hence h<0 on the compact set Δ, and its maximum is negative.
3. *Directional derivative toward e_ω.* For q_t=q₀+t(e_ω−q₀), the difference quotient of min(·,0) is bounded by |ψ_ω−q₀·ψ|≤2‖ψ‖, which is integrable. N_{q₀} is null. So the right derivative is ∫_{L₀}(ψ_ω−q₀·ψ). Maximality makes it ≤0, giving s_ω(L₀)≤∫_{L₀}q₀·ψ=h(q₀).
4. X₊⊆B_{q₀}=D_{K_{q₀}}, so K_{q₀}⊆Q_{X₊}. Also X₊⊆G a.e.
5. Hence ∫_G(p·ψ)⁺ ≥ p·s(X₊) = p·s(X)−p·s(L₀) ≥ p·s(X)−h(q₀). ∎

**Why the audit's §7 is not copied.** The audit concludes that Y∖U is inconsistent because its payoff integral is negative at every world. That inference uses linearity: a consistent set has nonnegative integral under a supporting state. It FAILS for box scores. With ρ=2 and t=0.7, two equal-mass patches near (1,−t) and (−t,1) are consistent (q=(½,½) supports both), yet each world's box score is (1−2t)·mass<0. Lemma 8b avoids the issue: the hull clause is secured through the consistent part X₊, not through inconsistency of the removed part.

**Theorem 8 (neighbourhood selection; PROVED).** Let X be incoherent. In the consistent case put U=E and X₊=X; in the inconsistent case take U and X₊ from Lemma 8b. Let Q=Q_{X₊}, G=D_Q and γ=γ(X). There are τ∈(0,1) and r_X>0 such that the fixed assignment

    b_p = G_τ ∩ B_p,   G_τ = D_{Q_τ},   Q_τ = (1−τ)Q + τΔ,   p ∈ Δ,

has the following properties.
- Every b_p is coherent (=D_{conv(Q_τ∪K_p)} a.e.) and undominated.
- For every model Y with ρ(X,Y)<r_X, the closed sets K_ω(Y)={p: s_ω(b_p)−s_ω(Y)≥γ/2} satisfy conditions 1–3 of the audit's §2.
- In particular some b_{p*} beats Y by at least γ/2 at every world, and dominates Y.

*Proof.*
1. **Buffer.**
   - Q_τ decreases as τ↓0, since (1−τ′)q+τ′s=(1−τ)q+τ[(1−τ′/τ)q+(τ′/τ)s] for τ′<τ.
   - Every h with P_Q(h)>0 has P_{Q_τ}(h)=(1−τ)P_Q(h)+τ min_ω h_ω>0 for small τ.
   - So G_τ↑G modulo the null set {P_Q=0}, and e_τ:=∫_{G∖G_τ}‖ψ‖dμ→0. Fix τ with e_τ<γ/4.
2. **Trimming.**
   - Put R=X₊∖G_τ. Since X₊∩G_τ⊆D_{Q_τ}, `lem:trim` gives Q_R=Q.
   - (In the degenerate case X₊ null, Q=Δ, so Q_τ=Δ and the next step is vacuous.)
3. **Robust hull inclusion (finite worlds).**
   - (1−τ)q+τΔ={q′∈Δ: q′≥(1−τ)q}, which is a relative neighbourhood of q. So Q⊆int_Δ Q_τ, and K:=Δ∖int_Δ Q_τ is compact and disjoint from Q_R.
   - Let F_Z(q)=μ(Z∩{q·g<0}). It is continuous in q by (M2) and dominated convergence, vanishes exactly on Q_Z, and satisfies |F_Z−F_{Z′}|≤μ(Z△Z′).
   - So a:=min_K F_R>0 if K≠∅. This is a measure of violated mass, not an integral of gamble values.
   - Let Z=Y∩U. Then μ(Z△X₊)≤μ(Y△X)≤ρ(X,Y). If ρ(X,Y)<a/2, then F_{Z∖G_τ}>a/2 on K, so Q_{Z∖G_τ}⊆Q_τ (or is empty), and H(Z∖G_τ)⊇G_τ.
   - H is increasing in its argument (`lem:hull`(a)), so H(Y∖G_τ)⊇H(Z∖G_τ)⊇G_τ.
4. **Hull clause for every p.** b_p⊆G_τ gives Y∖G_τ⊆Y∖b_p. Hence b_p∖Y ⊆ G_τ ⊆ H(Y∖G_τ) ⊆ H(Y∖b_p). This is the audit's chain, with the monotonicity in the correct direction.
5. **Uniform gap.**
   - p·s(b_p)=∫_{G_τ}(p·ψ)⁺ ≥ ∫_G(p·ψ)⁺−e_τ.
   - |p·s(Y)−p·s(X)|≤ρ(X,Y).
   - So, for ρ(X,Y)<r_X:=min(γ/4, a/2), we get p·(s(b_p)−s(Y)) > γ−γ/4−γ/4 = γ/2 for every p.
6. **Closed sets and coverage.**
   - p↦1_{B_p} is continuous in measure, because N_p is null for every p∈Δ (boundary priors included). So p↦s(b_p) is continuous and K_ω(Y) is closed.
   - The weighted inequality gives some ω∈supp p with p∈K_ω(Y).
7. **KKM.**
   - KKM gives p* in every K_ω(Y). Each world accepts Y→b_{p*}, by strict score gain and step 4. The reverse is refused, so there is no all-world tie.
   - b_{p*}⊆B_{p*}, so it is undominated by Theorem 3(i). ∎

**Corollary 9 (countable condition; PROVED).** The models with metric ρ form a subspace of L¹((1+‖ψ‖)dμ), which is separable. The balls {Y:ρ(X,Y)<r_X} cover the incoherent models, so countably many suffice (Lindelöf). Their fixed assignments form a countable collection satisfying the audit's §2 condition. Every incoherent model is dominated, by a coherent undominated model.

**What replaces δ.** It is the *ψ-expected-choice gap* γ(X), the margin by which the best p-weighted ψ-choice inside the relevant hull beats X, minimised over priors. In the inconsistent case it is bounded below by the *ψ-violation* −max_q∫_X min(q·ψ,0)dμ. The b_p are not ψ-Bayes sets but **trimmed ψ-hull models**: ψ-Bayes sets intersected with a trimmed coherent hull. A ψ-Bayes set alone fails the hull clause in general.

## 5. Infinite worlds

**Theorem 10 (PROVED).** Let Ω and E be as in appshort (E separable, containing 𝟙), with μ satisfying (M1) and (M2), and ψ(g)=l g⁺−u g⁻ with 0<l<u. Then Proposition 2, Theorem 3(i) and the countable condition of the audit's §3 hold. They hold with Θ=S′ and θ_F(p)=Σ_{ω∈F}p_ωδ_ω, where S′ is the state space of the closed sublattice E′ of ℓ^∞(Ω) generated by E.

*Modifications to the finite proof, each checked:*
1. **The space E′ and the score.** E′ is separable (rational lattice polynomials of a dense sequence), and S′ is weak-* compact and metrizable. ψ:E→E′ is norm-continuous with ‖ψ(g)‖≤u‖g‖, so m_ψ(X)=∫_Xψ dμ∈E′ is a Bochner integral of ψ(g), not of g. Also s_ω(X)=m_ψ(X)(ω).
2. **Bayes sets.** For q∈S′, the map φ(x)=lx⁺−ux⁻ is superadditive on ℝ, so q∘ψ is superadditive and positively homogeneous on E. It is ≥l·inf g>0 on E₊₊ and ≤0 when sup g≤0.
   - So B_q={q∘ψ>0} is an open convex cone. By appshort `lem:states`(b), B_q={P_{K_q}>0} for a credal set K_q on E, and cl B_q∖B_q⊆{P_{K_q}=0}.
   - Since q∘ψ(g+t𝟙)≥q∘ψ(g)+lt, the tie set {q∘ψ=0} lies in cl B_q∖B_q, which is null by (M2).
3. **Theorem 3(i).** Let C⊆D_{K_r} with r∈S′, and suppose B⊆C is non-null with s_ω(B)<0 at every ω. Then m_ψ(B)≤0 pointwise, so r(m_ψ(B))≤0. But r(m_ψ(B))=∫_B r∘ψ>0. This is the audit's own pure-removal argument, with no uniform bound needed.
4. **Gaps.** Lemmas 8a and 8b hold verbatim with q∈S′, q·s(X) read as q(m_ψ(X)), and the derivative taken toward δ_ω∈S′.
5. **Buffer.** (1−τ)Q+τS is generally not a weak-* neighbourhood of Q. Fix a metric d on S, and let Q_ε be the closed convex hull of {q:d(q,Q)≤ε}. Then:
   - Q_ε is compact and convex, and Q⊆int Q_ε.
   - ∩_εQ_ε=Q: separate q∉Q from Q by some g∈E, and use compactness.
   - Q_ε⊇(1−λ)Q+λS for small λ, by uniform continuity of (q,s,λ)↦(1−λ)q+λs. So `lem:trim` applies to G_ε=D_{Q_ε}.
   - Also P_{Q_ε}↑P_Q, so G_ε↑G modulo null sets.
6. **Robust hull.** F_Z(q)=μ(Z∩{q(g)<0}) is weak-* continuous on S, by (M2) and dominated convergence. The robust inclusion then follows exactly as in Step 3.
7. **Assembly.** q↦s_ω(b_q) is continuous on the metrizable S′ by dominated convergence and null ties, so K_ω(Y)⊆S′ is closed. Support coverage on Δ_F follows from q(m_ψ(·))=Σp_ω s_ω(·) at q=θ_F(p). Finite KKM, the finite-intersection property and compactness of S′ finish, as in the audit's §3.
8. **Separability.** ρ(X,Y)=∫_{X△Y}(1+u‖g‖)dμ.

## 6. (c) Coverage beyond boxes, and the variants

**Theorem 11 (transfer; PROVED).** Every step of §§2–4 uses only (F) and (C). So for every field satisfying (F) and (C):
- R_H^ψ satisfies the structural axioms and the countable condition;
- it protects exactly the upward closure {C coherent: C⊆B_r a.e. for some r}, with "exactly" needing atomlessness;
- it dominates every incoherent model with a coherent undominated model.

By C2 Prop 1.4 (round 84), (F) and (C) hold exactly when the *complete* ψ-score ranking satisfies W. So **every additive field that works under W in its complete form yields a hull ranking under the countable condition that protects the whole upward closure of its Bayes family, not just the Bayes sets themselves.**

**Can the hull clause reach beyond the Bayes sets of ψ? Yes: exactly to their upward closure.**
- Under the complete box ranking (and, under W, any box-endorsed ranking, by C1 Thm 2–3) only Bayes sets survive.
- The hull clause blocks the comparisons that would dominate a target D_Q with Q⊋K_r, which is not itself a Bayes set. A dominating change at such a model must add gambles outside the hull of what it removes, so it is accepted only where guided, and the guided changes available to a coherent model are null.
- The same clause licenses the trimmed-hull dominators of incoherent models.

**Contamination triangle test.** T1 (a=0.2) is protected under the symmetric box for ρ≤2 (Cor 5). Every incoherent model is dominated (Cor 9). §8 gives a concrete dominator for a perturbed T1.

**Proposition 12 (precise targets in two worlds; PROVED).** Fix β∈(0,1) and κ(t)=1+β·min(ln²t,1), so 1≤κ≤1+β. Define ψ by:
- ψ₁(g)=g₁ if g₁≥0;
- ψ₁(g)=g₁κ(g₂/(−g₁)) if g₁<0<g₂;
- ψ₁(g)=(1+β)g₁ if g₁<0 and g₂≤0;
- ψ₂ by exchanging coordinates.

Then ψ is continuous and sign-respecting with bounded loss weights in [1,1+β]. It satisfies (F) and (C), and its Bayes set at r=(½,½) is the precise half-plane {g₁+g₂>0}. So R_H^ψ protects that precise model, and Theorem 8 applies.

*Proof.*
1. On Q₊₋={g₁>0>g₂}, ψ₂/ψ₁ is determined by x=g₁/(−g₂), and B_r∩Q₊₋={F(x)>r₂/r₁}, where F(x)=x/κ(x).
   - F is strictly increasing. For |ln x|≤1, F′(x)=(κ−xκ′)/κ² and κ−xκ′=1−β+β(ln x−1)²>0. For |ln x|>1, F=x/(1+β).
   - F maps (0,∞) onto (0,∞). Symmetrically on Q₋₊, with y=g₂/(−g₁).
2. B_r contains Q₊₊ and misses Q₋₋. It is the open sector bounded by the rays at x₀=F⁻¹(r₂/r₁) and y₀=F⁻¹(r₁/r₂). Tie sets are rays, hence null.
3. κ≥1 gives x₀≥r₂/r₁ and y₀≥r₁/r₂, so x₀y₀≥1. Hence the sector has angle ≤π and is a coherent cone.
4. Equality x₀y₀=1 holds iff κ(x₀)=κ(y₀)=1, that is, iff x₀=y₀=1, that is, iff r=(½,½). At that r the sector is the half-plane. (Numerically, x₀y₀=1 at r₁=½ and 1.69, 2.23 at r₁=0.35, 0.2.)
5. Continuity across the axes follows from κ(0⁺)=κ(∞)=1+β. ∎

Centring κ at c in place of 1 (κ(t)=1+β min(ln²(t/c),1) in one quadrant, and centre 1/c in the other) protects any precise interior model. This field is commission-averse except along one pair of opposite rays, which is exactly where Prop 6's strictness hypothesis fails; it is not strictly proper. With the box family, **every coherent model in two worlds is protected by some commission-averse hull ranking**. For n≥3 (precise non-vertex models, and lower-dimensional credal sets with no vertex and no relative interior in any face), an analogous field is CONJECTURE.

**Remark 13 (lexicographic and intersection variants; PROVED).**
- **Lexicographic layers.** Suppose the score inequality is replaced by a lexicographic one, with first layer ψ¹ satisfying (F) and any later layers, and the same gate. Every comparison accepted by R_H^{ψ¹} is still accepted. Ties remain only between equal models, so dominance means acceptance at every world.
  - Hence the protected class shrinks or stays the same.
  - The dominators of Theorem 8 still work, so the countable condition survives.
  - Under the countable condition, the blind-first-layer lexicographic orders that round 84 proposed as the remaining escape for T1 are no longer needed.
- **Intersections.** Take the intersection of box judges, accepting only if every judge's score rises, with the hull gate. By the separation argument of Theorem 3(ii), C is protected iff C⊆B_Λ for some Λ in the simplex over (judge, world) pairs.
  - Each B_Λ is the Bayes set of an intermediate box, with per-world (l,u) a convex combination of the judges'. So intersections protect no target outside Cor 7's class.
  - Their selection needs KKM over (judge, world) pairs, which is not the audit's §2 form. UNCLEAR, and not needed.

## 7. (d) The hull clause without the word "coherence"

For a model B let ess(B) be the closed support of μ restricted to B. Since E is separable, B∖ess(B) is null; and Q_B={q: q(b)≥0 for all b∈ess(B)}, by continuity of q.

**Proposition 14 (PROVED; finite worlds, and verbatim for separable E).** Up to a null set (the boundary convention for nonnegative gambles, null under (M2)),

    H(B) = { a ∈ E : a ≥ Σ_{j≤k} λ_j b_j + ε𝟙 pointwise, for some finitely many b_j ∈ ess(B), λ_j ≥ 0, ε > 0 }.

So A⊆H(B) a.e. says: **almost every added gamble is strictly better, by a uniform margin, than some nonnegative combination of removed gambles.**

*Proof.*
1. Let K=cone(ess B)+E₊, a convex cone containing E₊. The right-hand side is K+E₊₊.
2. K+E₊₊ is open and contained in K. Conversely, if a∈int K then a−ε𝟙∈K for small ε. So the right-hand side equals int K=int cl K, the last because K is convex with nonempty interior.
3. By Hahn–Banach, a continuous λ with λ≥0 on K is positive. If it is nonzero it is a multiple of a state, and it is nonnegative on ess(B), so it lies in cone(Q_B).
   - If Q_B=∅, then cl K=E, so int K=E=H(B).
   - Otherwise cl K={P_{Q_B}≥0}, whose interior is {P_{Q_B}>0}: if P_{Q_B}(g)=0, then g−t𝟙 has P_{Q_B}<0.
4. Finally {P_{Q_B}>0}=D_{Q_B}=H(B) up to the convention. ∎

**Does this change anything?**
- *Mathematically, no.* It is the same relation, so every result above stands.
- *Conceptually, it clarifies.*
  - The clause never mentions the class of coherent models, any credal set, or the target. This differs from appshort's `def:integral`, whose clause (ii) quantifies over almost coherent models.
  - But the condition is the natural-extension operator of desirability, posi(B∪E₊) with a strict margin. It encodes D2–D4: positive scaling, sums and monotonicity. In that sense the gate is **built from the coherence axioms, though not from the coherent class**. The score ψ itself is coherence-free.
  - For LDG this is not circular. The general theorem quantifies over every ranking that satisfies its axioms, and this ranking serves only as an existence witness for admissibility. But a referee may say the hull ranking "knows" desirability logic, and the paper should say so openly.
- *It is the largest gate the protection proof allows* (CHECKED). Let P be the protected class and B any model whose hull contains some K_r. The coherent models in P containing B are the D_Q with K_r⊆Q⊆Q_B, and their intersection is D_{Q_B}=H(B). So any gate F used in the proof of Theorem 3(i), which needs F(B)⊆C whenever B⊆C∈P, must have F(B)⊆H(B) on such B. For B whose hull contains no K_r, no member of P contains B, and the gate could be enlarged to E without affecting protection. I have not checked whether that enlargement is useful.
- *A coherence-free gate built from ψ alone would protect at most the Bayes family.* The gate ∩{B_r: B⊆B_r} protects every B_r, by the proof of Theorem 3(i). But it does not keep additions inside a non-Bayes target such as T1, and the complete box ranking already protects the Bayes family with no gate. So reaching beyond the Bayes sets genuinely uses the desirability closure.
