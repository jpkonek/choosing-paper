Two worlds · coherent interval models · checked 19 October 2026

Five Patches

Audit's two-world example of non-additive defences, drawn out and checked. The question: does it show, simply and concretely, what it means to generate non-additive comparisons without any global pricing system, and to use incomparability to organise their coexistence and the worlds' defence duties?

Verdict

Yes, as an illustration. Every mathematical claim in the example checks out, once the notation slip audit itself corrected is fixed. Two of the three ideas are shown cleanly; the third only partly.

  • Non-additive comparisons without prices: shown. Four licensed comparisons out of two coherent models admit no positive additive prices, and the reason is visible in one line.
  • Incomparability organises coexistence: shown, and more sharply than audit stated it. Any three of the four licences do have prices. Only all four together don't. Leaving chained comparisons unranked is exactly what keeps the four from colliding.
  • Collective defence duties: only partly. The example shows the two worlds dividing four specific rivals between them. It does not show a full system: most rivals still have no defender.

The licences are written down by hand. So the example shows what the sought comparisons look like. It does not supply a rule that generates them, which audit names as the missing first component.

1 · The setting

Two worlds, two coherent models, five patches

A gamble pays g₁ in world 1 and g₂ in world 2. The example uses only gambles of the form g = (1−t, −t), which win in world 1 and lose in world 2. Under probability p for world 1, such a gamble is worth p − t. A coherent model built from an interval [l, u] of values of p:

  • accepts g when t < l;
  • accepts −g when t > u;
  • is silent in between.
2 · The licences

Four comparisons world 1 accepts outright

Each row is a rival that changes the base model on a few patches and agrees with it everywhere else. World 1 declares the base no worse than the rival. These are whole-pattern licences: no prices justify them, and combining two rows is not automatically licensed.

4 · How incomparability makes room

Every three licences can be priced; all four cannot

This is where incomparability does its work. Choose any three of the four licences: some positive prices endorse them strictly. Only the full set has no prices. A ranking that installed every chained comparison would run the four licences end to end and conclude that a model is no worse than a strict improvement of itself. Leaving those chains unranked avoids that. The three-model no-reversal condition only forbids short loops, and no short loop exists.

5 · Who defends what

The worlds split the rivals, and many are left over

World 2 makes only guidance comparisons. Each of the four licensed rivals improves world 2 on one patch and worsens it on another, so world 2 leaves them unranked, and world 1 alone defends against them. A one-sided rival goes the other way. For example, A with good advice added on a is better at world 1 and worse at world 2, so world 2 defends A against it.
