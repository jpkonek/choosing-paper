## Standing framework (binding)
- Worlds finite (n). Problems: gambles h ∈ ℝⁿ modulo sign, Gaussian (or abs. continuous, finite first moment) measure μ. Advice ranks: correct < silent < wrong < contradictory.
- Coherent model D_K = {h : m·h > 0 ∀ m ∈ K}, K nonempty closed convex ⊆ Δ; models modulo null sets.
- Ranking axioms: strict guidance (G); independence (IB); transitivity or at least (TC); the dominance conclusion (every incoherent evaluable model dominated by a coherent undominated model; equivalently, with closure (C), U_L = C_L); direct (C); target in C_L; decomposable domain (closed under splicing and convergence in measure) with local freedom (LF) at the target. One ranking per target.
- (S), (B), selections, Bayes-index matching and closed sections are NOT requirements. Do not reintroduce them.
- Success criterion for a construction: (1) compact score range / undominated weak dominators exist; (2) every undominated model is coherent; (3) target undominated; plus (G), (IB), (C), (LF), domain conditions.
- Avoid approaches equivalent to the CPM realisation problem (designing h-dependent "local priors" so that Bayes acts are coherent for every prior). Do not make ℓ^m proper.

## Lead's sketches (UNVERIFIED — check, repair or refute; do not assume)
S1. Visibility. For an extreme point v of K let F_v = {h ∈ cl D_K : v·h = 0}. Then F_v ⊆ {h_j ≤ 0} iff the ray from e_j through v continues into K (v ∈ conv(K ∪ {e_j}) with v ≠ ... ) — call v "visible from j". F_v ⊆ {h_j ≥ 0} iff moving from v toward e_j enters K ("anti-visible"). Otherwise F_v meets both signs of h_j ("crossing"). Conjecture: the auditor's condition is equivalent (up to compactness/uniformity details) to "every extreme point of K is visible from some world". Corollaries: every full-dimensional K ⊆ relint Δ with smooth boundary; every polytope whose vertices are visible.
S2. Collar obstruction. Suppose a ranking's worlds are lexicographic: convex, positively homogeneous, non-negative veto levels (any number), then a final strictly guiding error level; vetoes vanish on the target. Guidance forces each veto at world j to vanish on U ∩ {h_j ≥ 0}; zero sets Z_j are convex cones containing D_K and U ∩ {h_j ≥ 0}; if F_v meets {h_j > 0} then Z_j contains a "collar" just beyond relint F_v. For the reversed triangle every vertex is non-visible from every world; points in relints of the three faces have a positive combination in int ℝ³₊ (e.g. (4,0,−2) ∈ F_{v1}, (−2,0,4) ∈ F_{v3}, ½(−1,2,0) ∈ F_{v2}); hence D_K ∪ R' (R' in the collars) Brier-dominates D_K at every world with zero vetoes. So such rankings cannot cover the reversed triangle.
S3. For K ⊆ relint Δ full-dimensional, if some model has all leading levels zero, the target must have all leading levels zero (otherwise separation forces the target's Brier weight β to be supported on a proper subset of worlds, but β ∈ cone(K) has full support).
S4. Non-convex veto zero sets produce incoherent undominated models (unique minimisers over intersections of zero sets are undominated by the converse in the VB note).
S5. Replacing Brier by concave sign-correct secondary scores c_j (still giving convex minimisers) cannot cover the reversed triangle: summing to zero on each face forces each c_j to be linear on each face, with supergradients in the dual of U ∩ {h_j > 0}; this forces c_j = κ̂_j h_j on the faces where v is not anti-visible from j, and the continuity equations at the three extreme rays give x_2 = x_3 = 2x_1 and x_1 = x_3 = 2x_2 (x_j = π_j κ̂_j), so x = 0.
S6. Hausner–Wendel: a complete, transitive, translation-invariant and positively homogeneous preorder on ℝ^k is lexicographic in linear functionals. So complete transitive (IB) rankings depending on finitely many integral statistics are lexicographic.
