\noindent
The coverage theorem in Section 4 of \texttt{truth\_directed\_losses.tex}
already gives full coverage under its weaker background
requirement. The constant odds-ratio limit is also already in
\texttt{nonadditive\_losses.tex}, Remark \texttt{twoshun}.
The limit construction in \emph{Coverage after Neutral Abstention} is an
adaptation of those results, with a higher contradiction penalty and an
explicit uniform bound; it should not be credited as a new coverage idea.

Here we check the remaining adaptation: the write-in construction can
rank contradiction strictly worse than bad advice on \emph{all} models,
while retaining independence, closed choice, and identification of
undominated models. This is conditional on a broader weakening than the
currently agreed exception for the vacuous target. It does not change
that agreement. The adaptation below is author-checked, not independently
refereed.

\section{Exactly what would be weakened}

Fix any nonempty compact convex $M\subseteq\Delta$ and put
\[
 U=\{g:\underline{P_M}(g)>0\},\qquad
 \underline{P_M}(g)=\min_{m\in M}m\cdot g.
\]
The target $\Dscr_M$ agrees with $U$ modulo null sets. Count each binary
problem once on $H=\{g:g_1>0\}$, equipped with normalized Gaussian
measure $\mu$. For world $i$, its good orientation is
$h_i(g)=\operatorname{sgn}(g_i)g$. Define
\[
 c_i(g)=|g_i|\mathbf1_{\{h_i(g)\in U\}},\qquad d_i(g)=|g_i|.
\]
Coordinate hyperplanes are null and can be ignored. Integrate the table
\[
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
 \text{advice}&\text{good}&\text{silence}&\text{bad}&\text{contradictory}\\\hline
 \ell_i&0&c_i&c_i+d_i&2(c_i+d_i)
\end{array}
\tag{1}
\]
to obtain $L_i(X)$ for every measurable advice model $X$.

These penalties satisfy good $\le$ silence $<$ bad $<$ contradiction.
Good advice and silence tie exactly when the good orientation lies
outside $U$. Thus they strictly reward accepting a good gamble only when
the target itself accepts that gamble. On unanimous problems the good
orientation is strictly positive, hence belongs to $U$, so all four
ranks remain strictly ordered. Independence makes the stated indifference
apply in every common context.

For $M=\Delta$, (1) is precisely the accepted exceptional table: the
penalties are $0,0,|g_i|,2|g_i|$ on mixed problems and
$0,|g_i|,2|g_i|,4|g_i|$ on unanimous problems. For any other target the
tie set $\{g:h_i(g)\notin U\}$ depends on the target's interior, so the
weakened guidance order would change from target to target. This broader
weakening is \emph{not} adopted. The exception is limited to $V$, because
it is induced by a change to the background order (decision of
12 October 2026).

\section{The adaptation works}

\begin{theorem}
The rankings generated by (1) are complete, evaluate all models, satisfy
(IB), (LA), (S), and (C) modulo null sets, and satisfy the weakened
guidance order just described. Their undominated models are exactly
\[
 C_p=\Dscr_{\operatorname{conv}(M\cup\{p\})},\qquad p\in\Delta,
\tag{2}
\]
modulo null sets. In particular $\Dscr_M$ is undominated. Allowing these
systems for every target establishes full (A) in every finite dimension.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
First consider the old write-in error loss $L_i^{\rm old}$ for the
identity map on $U$, with symmetric Gaussian weight. On a binary problem
its four penalties, up to a common normalization of measure, are
\[
 0,\quad c_i,\quad c_i+d_i,\quad d_i.
\tag{3}
\]
Indeed missing the good orientation costs $c_i$, accepting the bad
orientation costs $d_i$, and giving bad advice makes both errors.
The old contradiction cost need not exceed the bad-advice cost; this is
the part that needs changing.

The Bayes characterization in Section 4 of
\texttt{truth\_directed\_losses.tex} supplies the
following facts for this old loss. Its selected models accept precisely
\[
 B_p=U\cap\{g:p\cdot g>0\}.
\]
They are coherent modulo null sets, undominated, and $p$-Bayes; their
loss profiles are continuous in $p$. Every model has a selected weak
dominator, and the undominated models are exactly the $B_p$ modulo
null sets. For the identity map the needed indifference sets are
$U\cap\{p\cdot g=0\}$, which are null at every prior, including boundary
priors. Finally
$B_p=\{g:\min(\underline{P_M}(g),p\cdot g)>0\}$, giving (2).

Table (1) changes only contradictions, increasing their penalty at every
world by $2c_i+d_i>0$. Hence
\[
 L_i^{\rm old}(X)\le L_i(X)\quad\text{for every }X,
 \qquad L_i^{\rm old}(C_p)=L_i(C_p).
\tag{4}
\]
Every old selected weak dominator therefore remains a weak dominator
under the new loss. If $X$ weakly dominated $C_p$ under the new loss,
(4) would make it weakly dominate $C_p$ under the old loss. Old
undominatedness makes the old profiles equal, and old identification
then gives $X=C_p$ modulo null sets. Thus $C_p$ remains undominated,
and profile equality still identifies it modulo null sets. Any new
undominated $X$ ties with its selected weak dominator, so must itself
equal a selected model. This proves the asserted characterization,
(LA), and (C).

Old $p$-Bayes optimality and (4) also show that $C_p$ remains $p$-Bayes.
Consequently, for every $X$, some $i\in\supp p$ has $L_i(C_p)\le L_i(X)$.
The selected profiles are unchanged and continuous, so every section
$\{p:L_i(C_p)\le L_i(X)\}$ is closed. This proves (S); it does not
require uniqueness of Bayes models at boundary priors.

The bound $\ell_i\le4|g_i|$ gives finite losses on all models.
Integrals of local penalties satisfy (IB), because shared contributions
cancel. Table (1) gives precisely the stated weak and strict guidance
comparisons. For every $p\in M$, (2) equals $\Dscr_M$, proving target
coverage and, on varying $M$, full (A).
\end{proof}

\section{What the older obstruction does and does not imply}

The two older restrictions should be kept distinct.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=4pt]
\item The CPM boundary-support theorem, now v19b Corollary 12.11,
excludes targets with two boundary points of \emph{different} supports
from CPM error-loss coverage. This includes nested as well as
incomparable supports.
\item Theorem 2.4 of \texttt{truth\_directed\_losses.tex} assumes
truth-directedness, availability of the specified thin-cone competitors,
and an exchange-rate bound at two worlds. Vanishing exchange rates
ensure that bound. Its target obstruction uses \emph{incomparable}
supports. This includes suitable nonadditive losses, but is not a
theorem about all independent ordinal rankings. Its application to CPM
exchange rates additionally specifies domain and weight regularity.
\end{itemize}
The old truth-directed order is not literally the present four-rank
order on contradictory models. However, its incomparable-support
dominance witness uses regular models: the target is silent on each
added cone. On $A_{ij}$, the point $b$ of the target credal set gives
$b\cdot g<0$, while $a_i>0=a_j$ and the other positive coordinates give
$a\cdot g>0$. Thus the target accepts neither $g$ nor $-g$; the other
cone is symmetric. Consequently the same trade proof applies to current
rankings whenever the requisite pricing inequalities hold for these
regular comparisons. A change only to contradiction penalties does
not remove that obstruction.

The three-vertex additive obstruction under strict (G) and (S) uses no
vanishing-rate assumption. The complete-ranking obstruction for $V$
uses no additive representation either, but requires (IB), (S),
completeness, and $n\ge3$. These are different sets of hypotheses, not
a single sequence of progressively stronger theorems. In particular,
the successful strict two-world construction in v3 shows that omission
blindness is not universally necessary for boundary coverage.

The justified conclusion is therefore conditional. If every target may
ignore missed gains outside its own interior, the theorem above settles
full coverage, with complete finite-valued losses. If the exception
remains limited to $V$, coverage of all proper targets under the standard
axioms is still open. The older exchange-rate theorem does not decide
between these policies, or prove that the broader weakening is necessary.
